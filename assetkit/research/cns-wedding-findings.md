# CNS wedding dress ragdoll investigation

Input: Nexus mod 3504 wedding dress (`CNSRepacked/5a2518e03d16a0cd`, `CH_P_EVE_02_Body`), the 2026-11-01 10:45 F7 log, and its extracted physics and animation assets. This is a diagnostic, not a confirmed fix.

The body's own physics asset contains the simulated `Ab-L/R-Breast-Link` bodies, parented and jointed to `Dm-L/R-Breast`. In the latest recorded three-second F7 sample, the left link's final socket transform is approximately 151 degrees from its reference local rotation; its parent remains close to its initial relative rotation. The corresponding body reports simulation enabled. Those measurements do not establish whether the solver or an animation node produced the final socket rotation; a bone socket is not a direct reading of its rigid-body pose. Jiggle After Death was enabled in that particular run; a separate user test reported the same visual failure with it disabled.

`cns_skin_probe.py` applies the recorded left-link rotation to the dress's actual reference vertices using their exported skin weights. The link and its descendants influence 25,826 vertices. On the reference pose, this isolated rotation predicts median displacement of 19.73 cm and 90th percentile of 25.30 cm among affected vertices. This is a counterfactual linear skinning calculation, not a simulation of Unreal's physics or animation graph: it excludes translation, all other bone motion, morphs and collisions. The left link's reference ancestry has unit scales, checked using a separate export that includes scales. The test fixture independently checks a known 180-degree rotation and an uninfluenced vertex.

The hip setup is different: the dress's body physics asset has no `Ab-L/R-Hip` rigid bodies, but its post-process animation blueprint contains spring nodes for `Ab-L/R-Hip-Reg` and its `CH_P_EVE_42_Physics` override has bodies for both hip chains. Each hip subtree influences more than 22,000 vertices; this makes hip deformation plausible but does not establish the same failure mechanism.

In the subsequent 10:55 F7 test, comparing the logged parent-relative hip rotations with the extracted reference skeleton gives approximately 11 degrees on the left and 7 degrees on the right while the post-process was off; the `Hip-Reg` rotations were within 0.3 degrees of reference. This does not establish whether the buttock looks correct: thigh rotation, the small translation changes and other weighted bones also affect the shape. In particular it does not show the roughly 150-degree breast-link failure on these hip bones.

Previous runtime attempts included locking the link constraints, separate spring-node suppression, and disabling the post-process rigid-body node; the user observed that breast deformation persisted. A later F7 diagnostic switched the entire post-process graph off for half a second: in both trials the left link remained at essentially the same extreme rotation (for example, quaternion -0.775/-0.225/-0.574/0.140 before and -0.773/-0.228/-0.577/0.131 with the graph off). The left hip's relative rotation also remained nearly unchanged, but this alone does not prove the hip is deformed. This strongly reduces the likelihood that the post-process graph causes the observed link rotation; it still does not directly measure the rigid-body pose or explain why its constraint did not hold. The temporary graph toggle has been removed.
