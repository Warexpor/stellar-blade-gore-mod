# Stellar Blade Gore Mod

A gore mod for Stellar Blade: when Eve dies she ragdolls, bleeds and can lose a limb. Three parts:

- **Data patch** (one IoStore pak, `ZZ_StellarBladeGore_P`): death shows that ragdoll the player, and physics fixes for every outfit. Works on its own.
- **UE4SS script** (`StellarBladeGore`): [gore](#gore) (dismemberment through her own skinning, caps, blood), the [active ragdoll](#active-ragdoll) (masses, momentum, impact, muscle tone), respawn fixes and a log.
- **`sbslice.dll`**: a small native helper the gore needs, loaded by UE4SS.

Formerly `sb-eve-ragdoll-death` / `EveRagdollDeath` / `EveRagdollProbe`; the installers remove the old names.

## How it works

Deaths are driven by `SBShowData` "shows". `Local/Data/CharacterDeadTable` row `Player` picks the show per death type. Most of them (stand, skill steps, explosions) are the shared `Dead/Dead_Monster_*` shows, which already contain an `SBShowRagdollKey` (channel 23 in `KeyContanerMap`). The player never ragdolls only because the key's constructor sets `bIgnorePlayer = true` (`mov byte [rbx+0x1F0], 1` in `USBShowRagdollKey::USBShowRagdollKey`), and no asset overrides it.

The patch (`mod/ragdoll_spec.json`):

- **clone**: copies the shared `Dead_Monster_EffectType_Stand / Explosion / ExplosionLight` and `SkillType_Step0-3` shows to `CH_P_EVE_01/Ragdoll/EveRD_*` with `bIgnorePlayer=false` and Eve-sized impulses (the enemy values of 5k–18k launched her across the map). The `Player` row of the death table points at the copies, so enemy deaths stay fully vanilla. Every `*DeadUIDelayTime` of that row is 8 s (vanilla 0): the death screen fades in only after her fall and the gore have played out; the script gets her up at `recoverAfter` (10 s), under that screen.
- **inject**: adds a ragdoll key, cloned from `Dead_Monster_SkillType_Step1`, to Eve's own death shows: `P_Eve_Dead`, `P_Eve_Dead2`, `P_Eve_Dead3`, `P_Eve_Dead_Nikke`, `Eve_Dead_Down`, `P_Eve_Dead_Falling` and `P_Eve_AirDead`. Each key is tuned per show (impulse, start time), and `bKeepPlaying` is set. Every Eve ragdoll key also gets `ImpulseLimitVelocity=700` and `CapsuleComponentFollowingSocketName=None`. The constructor defaults that to `Bip001-Pelvis`, which drags the player's still-colliding movement capsule onto the ragdoll every frame and flings her around spinning.
- **table**: `Player.AirborneDeadShow` points at the vanilla `CH_P_EVE_01/Basic/P_Eve_Dead_Air`, which does not exist, so airborne deaths had no show at all. It now points at `P_Eve_AirDead`.

- **collision**: Eve's blueprint (`CH_P_EVE_01_Blueprint`) has two components on her own skeleton that block `PhysicsBody`: the sword box `WeaponPhysicsCollision` (on `Bip001-Prop1`) and the `SBHair` mesh. A ragdoll runs on the `Ragdoll` profile (object type `PhysicsBody`), so they shove her own bodies every frame. The patch appends an Ignore entry for `PhysicsBody` to their raw collision responses; nothing else in the blueprint changes. Outfit or hair mods that replace this blueprint will conflict.

- **hit bone**: vanilla keys leave `bOverrideHitBone` false, so every death pushes `ImpulseBoneName` (Spine1). Ragdoll key code (`0x14245eb70`) takes the bone from the hit info when the flag is set and falls back to `ImpulseBoneName` when the hit has none. Eve's keys set it, so a leg hit folds the leg and a head hit snaps the head. Explosions keep the centre push.
- **outfits**: every outfit mesh that `EquipmentTable` can put on Eve (SkeletalMesh on her body skeleton; hair excluded) gets two patches. `assetkit/outfits.py` finds them, and `build.sh` runs it before patching.
  - **physics**: vanilla physics assets let Pelvis collide with both thighs although their capsules overlap by ~10 cm at rest. The solver pushes them apart every frame, which was the flinging. Almost every other pair is disabled, so limbs pass through each other. The patch makes bodies collide across five groups (torso, each arm, each leg). Pairs inside a group never collide: some outfits ship with neighbouring torso bodies colliding, and CH_P_EVE_09 spun from it. Pairs closer than 0.5 cm in that outfit's bind pose stay disabled, for every body. The accessories (hair, cape, breast and hip jiggle, neck pieces: every body outside the five groups) collide with nothing of hers, only the world (`accessory_collision`, off): CH_P_EVE_09 still had 114 accessory pairs colliding with her main bodies, the hip and breast bodies inside her torso shoved the thighs and arms every frame and the cape and hair got pinned between her and the floor, so she shook on the ground (pelvis turning 150-300°/s while lying still). These pairs are measured per asset from the mesh's reference skeleton and the body capsules, and for `CH_P_EVE_30` the result matches the hand-checked list. `LinearDamping` drops from 5.0 to 0.2: at 5.0 she fell at about 2 m/s and looked floaty. `AngularDamping` drops from 10 to 1 (`body` in the spec; a body that has no such property gets one): she toppled and turned as if under water. Some outfits (25 physics assets, e.g. CH_P_EVE_09) give twist or helper bones a body, as the anchor of a cape or skirt chain, but no constraint to anything above it. In gameplay those bodies are kinematic, so it never shows; in a ragdoll they fly free and drag the skin weighted to them, which bent and stretched her arms. Each of them gets a locked joint to the nearest ancestor body, framed at the bind pose (`outfits.lock_orphans`, on by default). The main joints bend about their twist axis (PhAT framed them as hinges) within a range centred on a zero of the asset's own, as soft limits: knees went 19° past straight, elbows 24°, hips bent forward only 30° and back as far, the spine 5° a joint, and under a push the soft limits gave way further. Each joint gets the range of `outfits.ranges` (degrees its far end may swing towards her front and her back from the bind pose: knees 140 back and 3 forward, elbows 145 and 3, hips 110 forward and 20 back, shoulders 120 and 40, each spine joint 15 and 8, the neck 25 and 15). `outfits.py` measures each asset's zero at the bind pose and turns the joint's frame 2 about its twist axis to centre the range there. `swing` overrides cone limits (the hips spread 30° instead of 20), and `hard_limits` turns every limit of the main joints hard (`bSoftConstraint` off). A main joint's motion limited to 0° (CH_P_EVE_09's pelvis-to-spine cone) is locked instead: a hard limit with no range shook it every frame. These bodies are kinematic in gameplay, so only ragdolls change.
  - **cpuaccess**: sets `bAllowCPUAccess` on LOD0. The skin cut reads it (see Gore).

  Every patched mesh is a full copy in the pak (all 134 make it about 560 MB compressed), and it replaces any outfit mod for the same mesh. Use `outfits.only` / `outfits.skip` (mesh-name globs) in the spec to trim the list, or set `outfits.enabled` to false to drop all of it. Meshes and physics assets that don't round-trip byte-exactly through UAssetAPI are skipped and listed in the build log. `ClothingAssetCommon` exports stay raw bytes, because UAssetAPI loses cloth LOD data when it writes them back.

Scripted deaths are left vanilla: swimming, ocean, sliding, desert hole, grinder, laser and balance beam.

## Build / install

Needs `git`, `cargo`, `python3` and `curl`. The .NET 10 SDK is fetched into `.cache/` if it is missing. Point `SB_GAME` at another install if needed.

```bash
./build.sh --install
```

```bash
./build.sh --uninstall
```

The script and DLL: `./ue4ss/install.sh` (see [UE4SS script](#ue4ss-script)).

The build pins and fetches [retoc](https://github.com/trumank/retoc), [UAssetAPI](https://github.com/atenfyr/UAssetAPI) and the [SB header dump](https://github.com/a5tronomy/SB), generates `.usmap` mappings from the headers (`assetkit/mkusmap.py`), extracts the assets from the installed game, patches them (`assetkit/patch_shows.py`), repacks `dist/ZZ_StellarBladeGore_P.{utoc,ucas,pak}` (UE4.27 TOC layout, zlib blocks through `assetkit/patches/retoc-compress.patch`; `SB_COMPRESS=none` stores them raw) and reads the result back to verify it (`assetkit/verify.py`). The output lands in `SB/Content/Paks/~mods/`.

The vanilla side (extracted packages, their JSON, the outfit scan) is cached in `.cache/work` under a key of the game's pak files, the mappings, the tool sources and revisions and the spec's outfit selection, so later builds only patch, pack and verify; the first build after a game update extracts everything again. Outfit meshes never go through JSON: `sbtool meshinfo` reads what the outfit scan needs, `sbtool cpuaccess` sets the flag in place and `sbtool checkcpu` verifies that nothing else changed. sbtool's batch commands run `SB_JOBS` packages at a time (default: half the cores), and retoc compresses blocks in parallel.

Tuning: edit `mod/ragdoll_spec.json` (field names as in `USBShowRagdollKey`) and rebuild. `DirectionType`: 0 Static, 1 HitDirection, 2 TargetToSelf, 3 HitLocationToSelf, 4 Velocity, 5 StaticLocal.

## Gore

The game's own dismemberment (`ASBCharacter` slicing, `0x142129c30`, used by the player's sword on some monsters) can't work on Eve. It copies the cut region into procedural meshes and hides it on the original through a vertex-colour override plus the material parameter `DissolveMaskRadiusFactor`. Only the gore master `MA_CH_DamageGore` has that parameter, and cooked materials can't be given one. So the cut is made in the skinning instead, on her own mesh with her own materials.

`native/sbslice` is a tiny freestanding DLL (clang + lld, no MSVC; `native/sbslice/build.sh`). It loads as a UE4SS C++ mod and waits for `gore.lua` to send it the address of the unused cheat UFunction `SBRewardGroupDropFormationPathLocation`. It then swaps that function's native thunk for its own handler, which takes commands through the cheat's string parameter. No byte signatures and no code patches are involved: the exe has no ASLR, and the thunk is matched by its address.

How a cut works (`c<component>,<lod>,<bone>`, a plane point and a normal):

- **Split.** For a plane through bone B, the DLL reads LOD0's positions, indices and skin weights (CPU copies) and skins every vertex in the current pose. A vertex weighted to B's subtree and in front of the plane joins the limb and keeps only limb bones. Every other vertex keeps only body bones. A limb hanging free of the cut goes with B whichever side of the plane it hangs: a bone of B's subtree whose joint and its parent's both lie more than 3 cm in front of the plane, and everything under it (the arms on a waist cut). By position alone, a hand hanging down past the waist stayed on the legs, and her gloves and the suit's cuffs floated where she was cut.
- **Seam.** No vertex is shared across the cut any more. Every vertex of a triangle that crosses the plane goes 100 % to a sink on its own side: a helper bone near the joint that sits where the bind pose puts it relative to its parent, preferably a leaf with few vertices of its own, chosen per mesh section. The section has to contain the sink, because the engine drops weights on bones a section doesn't have. Before each cut `gore.lua` sends `n<component>,<bone>,...`, the bones that may never be a sink: the main skeleton (`Bip001-*`; the pelvis, a clavicle and the head had been picked, and a sink gives up its weights everywhere), hair, capes and breasts (they swing, and the seam swung with them) and accessory bones (`Ab_*`).
- **Only the body.** A section that doesn't go round the cut (the same test as the caps below: straps, plates, pads, a cape, a loose sleeve) gets no seam. Each of its connected pieces that crosses the cut goes along whole with the side holding at least 85 % of its weight; one tied to both vanishes whole into a sink, so nothing of it can stretch. Pieces over 4000 vertices or reaching more than 40 cm from the cut are cut like the body instead. A seam through them had collapsed onto whatever bone their section had, often far off or swinging, and stretched across the body. The log shows `pieces=moved/vanished`.
- **Collapse.** The sink's inverse bind matrix becomes a zero scale at its own origin, so the seam collapses onto the sink's live position whatever drives it (animation or physics). Triangles across the cut become lines, and the ring next to them closes the opening. The sink's own vertices move to their strongest other bone first. Weight that leaves any bone (a sink, a helper the cut drops) goes to that bone's nearest ancestor that stays, else to the strongest other bone: a helper without a body rides its parent at a fixed offset, so the parent skins the vertex where the helper did. Moved to the strongest bone, a split's shoulder skin went onto the chest and tore off the clavicle by up to 23 cm once she moved. `HideBone` can't do this: a hidden bone renders at its parent's bind origin and stops updating, which stretched the severed limb back to the cut.
- **Apply and report.** The weights go to the engine's own `USkinnedMeshComponent::SetSkinWeightOverride`. The DLL reports both openings, fitted to the rim vertices in their new pose: centre, axes and an ellipse in the cut plane. The cap belongs on the limb, not on what is strapped to it, so rim points are grouped by mesh section first. A section whose points go round the cut axis (11 of 16 directions) is a layer of the limb. The innermost layer and the ones sitting tight on it (skin, a body suit) get the cap; straps, plates, capes and loose sleeves are left out and close up in their own material. With no layer at all, every point counts. Of what is left, points further than 1.6× the median from the cut axis are dropped too. The log line shows the grouping (`bsec=section/points/directions/radius`, `bcore=` the kept sections, `l...` for the limb side).
- **Open.** The sinks are recorded but collapse only on `k`, a few frames later. Collapsing them under the old weights would pull every vertex those helpers moved onto their origins until the GPU has the new weights.

The command is `c<component>,<lod>,<bone>[,<mode>,<radius mm>[,<sink>,<remap>]]` (Dent: `...,5,<radius mm>,<depth mm>`). Besides the plain cut (mode 0) there are six modes:

- **Whole (1).** Every vertex follows B's subtree; no seam. Eve's face is its own mesh, weighted to the head and the neck, so a beheading moves all of it onto the head (cut in Plane mode like the neck, 8300 of its vertices vanished into the stump and the head came off faceless).
- **Plane (2).** The limb side is what is in front of the plane and within the radius of its axis, whatever the weights. The head's subtree is the head alone (the clavicles hang off the neck) and the outfit skin has no head bone near the neck, so the neck is cut by position, mid-neck.
- **Hole (3).** No limb. A ray from inside a bone segment, pointing out of the body, finds where it first meets the mesh; the skin within the radius collapses onto a sink inside the body. Only the body's layers are holed: of the sections in the ball, the innermost along the ray and those within 0.8 cm of it. A plate or pad over the skin stays whole, and if the ray came out through one, the hole moves in to the skin under it (`layersoff=`). The answer adds `hit=` (the surface point) and `wb=` (the bone carrying most of the rim). Wounds no longer use it: the collapse pulled the whole patch into one point, a puckered star under a meat lid.
- **Rupture (6).** A blunt blow's burst: Dent's ray and layers, but every vertex within the radius takes 98 % of the projecting sink (a trace stays on its bones so `wb=` is found), so the skin goes down to the floor with a hard edge. Lua covers the crater with a ragged lid of torn flesh on the surface.
- **Dent (5).** Blunt blows and stabs. The same ray and layer rule as Hole, but nothing opens: the body's skin within the radius across the ray is pressed in onto a plane `<depth>` under the surface point, smoothly. Each vertex keeps its own weights and blends in a projecting sink (the Side mode trick) by `(1 - q²/r²)²`, q its distance from the ray, so the centre lands on the plane, the rim doesn't move, and nothing is pushed outward. A dent never reuses another cut's sink. The answer adds `hit=`, `wb=` and `dented=` (vertices moved).
- **Side (4).** Half of a split, at any angle: what is in front of the plane stays, what is behind it goes. It isn't collapsed into points: the first version did that with one sink per section, so the whole other half, legs included, went into one spot near the chest and every triangle along the midline stretched from the hips or the neck to it. Now the torso just behind the plane (5 cm) is flattened onto it, and what lies deeper collapses to a point on the plane in its band (flattened, a slice across the waist would spread the shoulders out past the opening). Its sinks get a projection instead of a zero scale: inverse bind matrix, then onto the plane (2 cm inside the kept side, behind the lids; at 0.3 cm the sheet came out in front of the lid wherever she bent) in the sink bone's frame as posed at the cut. Each vertex lands where it was, only pushed onto the plane, so the midline closes without anything reaching across. The sheet is banded the way the lid is cut into pieces: each vanishing vertex goes to the band of the spine bone it lands nearest to (Lua's `SPLIT_RIDE`, sent after the limbs), and a band's sinks must ride that bone (the first main bone up their chain; under a clavicle or a breast they swing on their own). A bone no free helper rides (CH_P_EVE_09's Spine and Spine1) gets no band. A flattened vertex is weighted between the two bands of its section it lies between, by where it sits along the spine, so the sheet bends like skin. With four bands along the opening and whatever sink was nearest, a pelvis helper carried 43 cm of the sheet up to the ribs, clavicle helpers swung with the arms, and once she bent the sheet tore between bands and came out through the lid (the playtest's stretch probe: thousands of stretched triangles, all on the split's own sinks). A limb never splits lengthwise: Lua sends `g<component>,<gone roots>,0,<kept roots>,0,<ride bones>` (each arm and thigh by the joint it hangs from, shoulder or hip: by the middle of its first segment, an arm hanging past a slash across the stomach went with the legs and was cut off at the shoulder), the kept half's limbs stay whole wherever they lie, and the other half's limbs collapse to a point on the plane where they joined. Kept skin lets go of every helper past the plane and on it (within 1 cm), so the other half's sinks never pull on it, and the halves no longer need re-cutting. Both halves are components of one mesh asset, so their sinks are shared: a vertex the vanilla skin puts wholly on a helper the other half has just made its sink is reweighted like any other (it used to count as an earlier cut's seam vertex and was flattened with the other half: CH_P_EVE_09's neck, on `Ab-NeckSub`). A section with no helper to flatten onto (CH_P_EVE_09's neck lace knows only the neck and the spine) borrows the half's nearest one into its BoneMap, like the face below. The opening runs head to crotch and isn't convex. The DLL traces it as one outline (`s0p=`, up to 200 points): the rim points and the mesh's own cross-section with the plane are drawn on a 0.8 cm grid, the outside is flooded from a corner, the inside filled and its largest piece's border chained; a fill much smaller than its outline's area means the outside leaked in through a gap, and the gap closing is doubled up to 12 cm. Bands along it only pick the sinks now. `h<component>,<mode>,<head bone>` (point and normal: the neck plane) goes before a half's split cut: Eve's hair (`CH_P_EVE_Hair02`: a root and its hair chains, no body bone) can't be halved, so the head never is. Mode 1 keeps everything above the neck plane whole and closes the strip of neck under it on the far side; mode 2 lets the head go into a sink at the neck and sends a second opening, `n` (`np=`). No sink may be a bone the head's or the neck's skin hangs on (sinks are the mesh asset's, so the other half's head would let go of it), and such a bone is never one the kept skin drops, wherever it lies (`Ab-NeckSub` swings past the plane in a pose). The answer adds `proj=` and `point=`. `<sink>` forces a sink bone for sections that have nothing to flatten onto: Eve's eyes, teeth and brows only know the head bone, so the face borrows `Bip001-Spine1`, which no face vertex uses. The DLL adds it to the section's BoneMap in place when the array has room (an engine array always has room for 8 entries); a fuller one is swapped for a copy with room for 512, put back on undo. Borrowing only in place had left a waist split's copy with 10617 vertices of the upper body standing on the legs, its sections full and, on `k`, points the component's `MasterBoneMap` entry for it at the master's bone `<remap>` (the head), so the face's vanished side flattens in the head's frame.

`p<src>,<dst>` copies one component's pose onto another on the same skeleton, for the split's copy.

`s<component>,<lod>` is the stretch probe: it skins every vertex exactly as the GPU does (the component's pose, its overrides) and answers `ok stretched=<n> of <triangles> hidden=<n> longest=<cm> bones=<bone>:<count>:<cm>;...`, the triangles with an edge more than 3x and 15 cm longer than at bind (`hidden`: those wholly on sinks, the flattened sheet meant to sit behind the lids) and the bones they hang on most. `gore.lua` logs it with bone names (`split stretch, her half / copy` at 0.5 and 2 s after a split).

In every mode, a limb-side vertex in a section that can't follow the limb, and every limb-side vertex of a section that has seam vertices on that side but no limb sink, vanishes: it joins the body side's seam and collapses with it, which beats a triangle stretched from the flying piece to the body. The log shows `vanished=`.

`x<component>,<lod>` releases the override, restores that mesh's matrices and the component's borrowed `MasterBoneMap` entries. `r` restores every collapsed matrix and borrowed BoneMap entry, since they live on the shared mesh asset.

`ue4ss/StellarBladeGore/Scripts/gore.lua` drives each cut:

1. Every killing blow cuts, whatever hit her (a Gorilla's fist cuts like a blade: there are no blunt or stab wounds on death). A killing hit the game reported (see the split below) cuts along its blade (`gore.blow`, `bladeChance`); otherwise it rolls what the blow does (`tune.gore.death`: a limb, the head, the torso, or a split when the blow came down from above; the split is described below). What comes off flies the way the blow went (from the attacker through her, else the swing, else her death push): a piece at `deathThrow` (650 cm/s), a split's cut off half at `splitFly` (350), every body of it (`piece flies with the blow` in the log). It picks the part nearest the hit and forces LOD0 while she is cut (the face too, for a head).
2. Two ticks later it cuts at `tune.gore.cutAt` along the bone and places the caps hidden. On a death the plane leans until it contains the killing blow's direction (the death key's impulse: attacker to Eve, plus some of the hit direction; the game exposes no blade swing), at most `blowTilt` degrees off square; then a small random tilt. Accessory chains that hang off the limb's root bone stay on the body: a chain of at least 3 bodies of its own (not `Bip001`), anchored on that bone, whose top joint lies over 3 cm off the bone (twist bones lie on it). CH_P_EVE_09's cape hangs off the upper arm's twist bone half way down the arm, and part of it went off with the arm. `gore.lua` names them to the DLL (`g<comp>,0,<chain roots>` before the cut; sent empty otherwise), which leaves every vertex mostly on them on the body, on their own bones. When the limb comes off, each chain lets go of it and hangs from the nearest body above the limb (the clavicle, the pelvis) on a `PhysicsConstraintComponent` ball joint where the chain starts (40° swing, 20° twist), destroyed on recovery (`<chain> kept on <bone>` in the `off` log line). `test/real/run.sh` with `ARM=1` cuts the left upper arm of the real mesh: no cape vertex may go with the arm and no forearm vertex stay (`NOKEEP=1` shows the old split cape).
   A head cuts the face whole first, then the neck by position 0.7 of the way from the neck joint to the head (4.1 cm over the joint; 0.5 sat by the shoulders, and before that it was 1.8 cm over and 2.9 cm under the joint); the face reaches down to 1.2 cm under the head's joint and goes with the head, whose cap sits on the face's neck. A wound casts its ray against the killing blow (out of the body towards the attacker), off the bone's axis. Per segment r is 6.5 cm torso, 5 thigh, 3.5 upper arm and calf, times `woundScale`. A blunt blow bursts the skin (`bluntWound` "rupture", Rupture mode): a crater 0.9 r wide and 0.5 r deep (`ruptureWidth`, `ruptureDepth`) under a ragged lid of torn flesh (12 points at 70-125 % of the radius, stretched), bleeding for all of `bleedTime`. `bluntWound` "dent" gives the older smooth squash instead, 1.8 r wide and 0.7 r deep (`bluntWidth`, `bluntDepth`), bleeding `woundBleed` (0.6) of `bleedTime`; in a playtest it read as a janky dent. A stab is a pit 2.5 cm wide and 0.6 r deep, at most 4 cm (`stabWidth`, `stabDepth`), bleeding for `bleedTime`. Dents and stabs get torn flesh at the bottom (`woundFlesh`): the meat material on a flattened dome on the pressed-in skin, `woundFleshShare` (0.55) of a dent's radius, most of a stab's. Every wound drips and pools like a cut (22 cm). Wounds are only made by F10 (a blunt wound and a stab in turn): a death always cuts.
3. Two ticks after that it sends `k`, breaks the ragdoll constraint at that bone, so the limb's bodies carry it away (on a living Eve too, for F9), and gives every body of the piece a push (`throw` away from the body; `deathThrow` along a killing blow).
4. It shows the caps. Each is a thin domed lid built as a procedural mesh on the opening's outline (the convex hull of the trimmed rim points, which the DLL sends in order), so its edge sits on the skin. If the procedural mesh can't be made, a flattened sphere fitted to the ellipse is used instead. Both use the game's meat material (`MI_A_Meat_01_1`, `capMaterial`), each through its own instance whose emissive `BloodColor` is turned down to a faint dark red (`capBloodColor`; the effect material's own 10, 2.4, 0 glowed bright orange), and are attached to the bone of that side. The game has no human cross-section; `MI_CutDamage`, used before, is a monster dissolve material and looked like a broken glossy texture on a lid. Each limb and neck stump also gets a snapped bone, the game's `Generic/GenericAsset/GM_Brokenbone` static mesh with its damage-bone textures, along the opening's normal, centred on the area centroid of the opening's outline (the rim's mean sat off centre where the rim has more vertices), `stubBone` (0.3) of the opening's width thick and sticking out `stubOut` (0.5) of its narrow radius; its long axis is read from its bounds (`bone stub mesh:` in the log). On the spine, neck, head or pelvis the stub sits towards her back (`SPINE_BACK`, 0.55 of the way from the opening's centre to its rim), where the spine is, not in the middle of the opening. Every cut (F9, F11 too) also raises her skin's own blood layer: Eve's skin master (`MA_Skin_EVE`, `MF_SkinBloodFX`) reads `BloodFactor` and `BloodOffset` from the game-wide `MPC_PCDirtyOffset`, so `bodyBlood` ramps them over 1.5 s and puts them back on recovery (`body blood:` lines show the values before and after). It covers all of her, not just around the wound. **Floor blood**: every bleeding opening of a cut (F9 and F11 too) is followed for `poolTime` (12 s). While it moves (she falls, the piece flies) it drips small splats every `dripEvery` (18) cm; where it comes to rest a pool spreads under it, fast at first and slowing (`poolGrow`, 5 s to 63 %), as large as that part bleeds (a neck or thigh about 55-60 cm radius, a forearm 30, each half of a split 90). The decals are the game's own floor blood from the cinematics (`MI_FloorBloodDecal_*` on the deferred decal master `MA_A_BloodDecal_01`), traced down onto the floor under the opening and turned to its slope; her meshes take no decals meanwhile. They don't flow: on a slope a pool is still a round stain. A death without a cut keeps the single pool under her pelvis. A limb cut while she stands gets the death ragdoll's collision profile on the mesh until she recovers: her living mesh has no physics collision with the world, so the piece fell through the floor.
5. It plays the game's own gore sounds (`sounds`, `soundVolume`, through `GameplayStatics.PlaySoundAtLocation`): `Mon_slice_Big_Cue` on a cut, with `PC_LinkAttack_Boss_Gore_Splash_Cue` on a beheading, a waist cut or a split; `hit_common_blood_power_00_Cue` and `CPS_LinkAttack_Flesh_Impact_FX_Cue` on a rupture; `PC_LinkAttack_Boss_Flesh_Stab1_Cue` on a stab.
6. It spawns the game's Niagara blood: a splat, a bleed from each opening for `bleedTime` seconds, attached to that side's cap (so the severed piece bleeds from its stump and not from its middle) and pointed out of the opening along its normal. The bleed is `NS_Hit_Flesh_Slice_BloodOnly` (the flesh-slice burst without its meat stub, a cone in the effect's own space), scaled to `bleedSize` (0.6; the full-size effect was too big for a stump), fired again every `bleedPulse` (0.35) s so each spurt follows the stump as it turns; `bleedAxis` names the effect's cone axis, and the `bleed: opening faces ...` log line, once per death, shows where the effect's Z and X really point. `NS_Blood_Flooded_Attached`, the bleed before, locks its emission to a fixed axis (`SBLockAxis`) whatever it is turned to, so every spurt went the same wrong way. Last, floor blood: each opening drips while it moves and pools where it comes to rest, and a death without a cut pools under her pelvis once she settles. The pools are the parking lot cinematic's puddle decals (`MI_D_NoiseBloodDecal_03`/`_04`, one solid blob each), traced onto the floor; the Raven cinematic's (`MI_FloorBloodDecal_1-4`, a sheet of splats under a starburst mask) and the game's looping floor effect read as stretched streaks. All of it is the red blood (`MI_A_DefaultBlood`, the common decal): the hit and splat had been the monster ones, the Naytiba's glowing orange liquid.

A **split** (`death.split`, only when the killing blow came down from above) cuts her in half top to bottom along the blade. The split comes first and the fall after it: from the moment it starts, her ragdoll is held where it is (gravity off, every body's velocity zeroed each tick, the muscle tone kept at full strength, at most 1 s), and when it opens both halves get back the velocities her bodies had (the death key's push, her momentum) and fall (`held still for the split`, `released after ...` in the log; the hold matches her mesh by address, as UE4SS hands out a new wrapper each call, and F11 never held before that). It used to start falling first and come apart a moment later. A split never has a fall before it: on an animated death (`active.animated`) she ragdolls the moment the split is rolled, held still while it is cut (`splitAnim` 0). With `splitAnim` above 0 both halves would play on with her fall as one body (the copy master-posed to her mesh) for that many seconds, then come apart by physics at the speed the animation gave each body. The split brings its own blood: no hit splash at the death, and the seam bleeds `splitBleedDelay` (0.3 s) after it opens, once the halves have come apart (the hit's blood had come first and read as blood, then a split). `main.lua` hooks `SBNetworkPlayerController:ClientBroadcast_SkillHitResult`, the game's per-hit message with the swing's direction, the attacker's forward and a left-to-right / right-to-left / front attack direction, and keeps the hits that land on Eve (all logged, to calibrate the direction; the first 12 elsewhere too). Its parameters are read in the order the SDK dump declares (`inTurn, inSkillGuid, inSkillResultID, inCasterGUID, inTargetGUID, inSkillHitResult, inAttackGroupName, inHitResult, inHitLocation, ...`); an earlier version read them shifted, so no hit ever matched her. A death within `hitWindow` (0.6 s) of a hit whose direction points down by more than `splitDown` (0.6) may split; the plane holds the swing and the attacker's forward, made to contain her spine. `gore.lua` then:

1. adds a copy of her body (same mesh, materials and transform, not ticking, hidden);
2. copies her pose onto it, decides which arm and thigh go with which half, and cuts both bodies in Side mode, one keeping each side of the plane. Her half keeps the head whole, with her face and hair untouched on their own animation; the copy's neck closes (the `h` neck plane above). Each half gets one flat lid over its whole opening and the copy a domed one over its neck, her half one over the strip of neck it lost. Where the plane runs along the spine, each lid shows it halved: one `GM_Brokenbone` piece per spine segment (pelvis to neck), flat in the plane from joint to joint, towards her back, riding the segment's lower bone (`splitSpine`, `splitSpineWidth`, `splitSpineFlat`; `N spine pieces` in the lid's log line). The flat lid is the traced outline ear-clipped, with a skirt reaching `splitLidGrow` (0.4 cm) past the skin, `splitLidPush` (0.2 cm) out of the half, both faces; it comes in pieces, each triangle on the nearest spine bone, so it bends with the ragdoll (`split lid on ...` in the log). Halving the head had made the hair freak out (posed by the body, its chains lost their own simulation) and left one half bald or with a whole head;
3. moves every component of hers that is posed by her body or rides a socket on the lost half (gloves, suit pieces, accessories) onto the copy, so nothing floats where she was cut (`split: N pieces moved`; put back before the copies go). Whatever rides the head (hair, hairpins on `SC_HairACC`, earrings) stays on her;
4. collapses, gives the copy the pose she is in now and its own ragdoll (her physics asset, the Ragdoll profile, no collision with her), her velocity, and shows it. On each half, the limbs that went with the other half come off and fall free, invisible, at their own mass (the copy's head goes the same way). At 0.02 of their mass they were feather-light chains that shook on the floor; kinematic, they froze in the air with whatever outfit piece still rode them. Hiding them with their bodies removed (`HideBoneByName` with `PBO_Term`) stopped her ragdoll the moment the split opened, and her mesh never simulated again until the level reloaded. The spine bodies weigh half, the halves get `splitPush` (300 cm/s) apart, and a lengthwise cut's upper bodies `splitTip` (250) more, so the halves fall open. It bleeds along the lids.

The copy is made without collision, so it gets its physics bodies by `SetCollisionEnabled` when it opens (a profile change alone never made them, and the copy stood frozen); `split copy ragdoll:` and `split copy at 0.5 s / 2.0 s:` log whether it simulates and where its pelvis is.

Known rough edges: the physics bodies are full width on both halves, and accessories on other components aren't split (each goes whole with the half it hangs off). F11 ragdolls her and splits her along the camera's view. F5's test death always splits her top to bottom (`f5Split`), at once, with no death show before it.

**Cutting along the blade.** The skill-hit message never came in a single-player session (0 calls), so the killing blow comes from three places, best first: the game's skill hit (within `hitWindow`), the last hit reaction on her mesh (`PhysicalHitForce*` and `VertexShakeInfo`, within `meshHitWindow`, logged as `mesh hit:`), or the nearest other character within `attackerReach` (800 cm). Without a swing, one is made up across the push from there: level, slanted or down, through where the mesh says she was hit or else a part of her picked with odds (`swing_at` in `main.lua`): mostly the chest and stomach, then the neck and arms, a leg about 1 in 6, a limb struck 40 % of the way down it. An even pick of 12 bones, a third of them legs and struck at the joint, had put nearly every death through a leg. `death: blow from ... (made up: <bone>)` names it, and the cut prefers the limb it aimed at. A death cuts along that blade instead of rolling (`bladeChance`, 0.85), but a blade through her body splits her only `bladeSplit` (0.2) of the time, else the odds are rolled: the made-up blade goes through a spine bone, so every real death had been a split. The blade moved in the plane holding the swing's direction and the attacker's forward, through where it hit; when those two are (nearly) parallel, the game's attack direction picks a level plane (left-to-right, right-to-left, slanting a little) or an upright one (front). What the plane goes through decides the cut:

- an arm or leg it crosses within `limbReach` (12 cm) of the hit, when the hit is more than `spineReach` (15 cm) from the spine: that limb, at the crossing, leaning with the blade up to `sliceTilt` (50°) off square. A plane running along the limb doesn't count;
- a plane past the spine that only meets a thigh at its hip joint (under 0.25 down it), or a calf when the hit is close to the spine: across the waist instead (`it only met ...`);
- the neck, with the chest clearly on the other side from the head: a beheading, leaning with the swing;
- anywhere else through or along the spine: the body in two along the plane, at any angle (down the middle, across the waist, shoulder to hip). It is the split above with the blade's plane: the half with the head stays her own mesh and keeps the head, face and hair whole, and where the plane crosses the spine both halves' ragdolls break there, so neither piece is held on by the invisible bodies of the other end. A plane that meets the spine only between the pelvis and the first spine bone went through her hips (the legs stayed, the crotch flattened into the cut face with the suit stretched over it): it moves up to 40 % of the way from Spine to Spine1, at the same angle (`it crossed the hips`). A plane along her with every arm and leg on one side only grazes her (a 45° blade on an animated death left one half a sliver and the copy a whole body over her, so nothing looked cut): it cuts across the waist instead (`it ran along her`). As a last check, a split leaving either half under `splitMinShare` (12 %) of her vertices, or anything unsunk, is undone and becomes a waist cut (`split ... degenerate`);
- nothing usable (no plane, or a plane missing her): the usual roll.

The log line `blade (...)` shows the plane, the hit's direction, forward and attack direction, how far each spine bone is from the plane and what it chose. Shift+F5 ragdolls her and cuts along a made-up blade through a part of her picked as for a death (level, 30°, 60° or straight down, the attacker where the camera is).

On revive it undoes all of it: skin weights, collapsed bones, caps, effects, forced LOD, physics state and the split's copies.

## Active ragdoll

**Animated deaths (experimental, `active.animated`).** Stand and light-hit deaths play her own fall instead of a ragdoll. The death table points `StandDeadShow`, `Step0DeadShow` and `Step1DeadShow` at `P_Eve_Dead`, `P_Eve_Dead2` and `P_Eve_Dead3` (they pointed at the animation-less ragdoll clones), and the ragdoll key in those, `P_Eve_Dead_Nikke` and `Eve_Dead_Down` carries `ImpulsePower 1` and `ImpulseLimitVelocity 701`. That key is only the signal that she died: `main.lua` reads the 701 in `RagdollInfo`, turns physics back off within a frame and lets the show's animation play on. It holds its end pose (`bHoldEndTime`), so nothing jiggles on the floor. Cuts happen on the animated body as on a standing Eve (F9): the piece simulates on its own. A waist cut or a split switches to the ragdoll before cutting (`animated death: ragdoll after all`). Heavy hits (`Step2`, `Step3`), explosions, airborne and ledge deaths stay ragdolls. Only death shows carry a ragdoll key, so a knockdown (`Result_Knock_Down*`, `Down_BS`, `Groggy_*`, getups) never counts as a death. The log says `death: animated fall, physics off` or `death: ragdoll show`. More falls: the data mod builds eight death shows from her knockdown falls (`Ragdoll/EveDeath_*`: `Result_Knock_Down_Start`, `_Start1`, `_Forward`, `_Backward`, `Result_State_KnockDown_S_Fw`, `_S_Bw`, `_Tumble_Fw`, `_Tumble_Bw`), each a copy of `P_Eve_Dead` with the fall swapped in (`animshow` in `patch_shows.py`), held on its last frame, `Eve_Dead_Common` (death face and voice) kept and no ragdoll key. Nothing in the game refers to them and they aren't in its asset registry, so UE4SS's `LoadAsset` can't find them: `P_Eve_Dead` refers to all eight (`preload`): a play-show key each, in its play-show channel but disabled (`bEnable` false) and timed at 1e9 s, so they load with it, stay loaded while it is, and it never plays them. A bare import of them, the first try, was dropped by the IoStore conversion; keys in no channel loaded them, but nothing referred to the keys and the next garbage collection took them and the falls (`show ... didn't load` a minute after the show loaded; the line now says whether the host and its first key are there); `verify.py` checks the keys. The knockdown shows and animations themselves are untouched. An animated death picks one of the nine at random (her own show's fall or one of these, `animatedPool`) and plays it over her death show through `SBBlueprintLibrary.PlaySBShowToPlayerCharacter` (`animated death: fall ...`; `PlaySingleShow` is only on the game's show interface, which Lua can't call, and silently did nothing). CUE4Parse can't decode the game's animation codec, so their end poses aren't checked offline: at 3 s and 5 s the log gives her head's height over the floor and flags a fall that doesn't end lying (`NOT LYING, drop this fall`). `meshtool anims` reads the lengths.

`ue4ss/StellarBladeGore/Scripts/active.lua` runs on every ragdoll (real deaths and F7), settings under `active` in `tune.lua`:

- **Masses.** Vanilla physics assets set `bOverrideMass` on the spine and jiggle bodies without a value, so each weighs the engine default of 100 kg; cape and accessory links weigh up to 80 kg. The main bodies get a human distribution (about 60 kg in all, `massScale`). Every other body is capped at `accessoryMaxKg`, and all of those hanging off one main body share `accessoryShare` (15 %) of its mass: CH_P_EVE_09's cape hangs off the upper arms (a twist body and 7 links a side) and the ponytail off the head (9 links), and at 0.8 kg a link they weighed 6.4 kg on a 1.7 kg arm and 7.2 kg on a 4.5 kg head and pulled both out of their joints. The log line shows the masses before and after and how many hang off each body. A split's copy gets the same masses (it kept the outfit's 100 kg chest bodies). `stretch at ...` lines (0.5, 1, 2 and 3 s) show the three main joints furthest off their length at the start, in cm; joints gore cut or broke on purpose (a severed limb's root, the split's spine break) are left out.
- **Momentum.** The death show stops the capsule before the ragdoll starts, so she used to drop on the spot even at a sprint. Her speed from the last third of a second carries into the fall (`momentum`, `momentumMax`).
- **Impact.** A push along the killing blow (the death key's own impulse, else from where the blow came: her mesh's hit or the nearest enemy, else from the hit location through the pelvis), spread over the upper body (`impact`, 450 cm/s, `impactUp`), plus a small random turn about the vertical (`twist`, 35°/s). A death used to drop dead on the spot: the key gave no impulse and nothing else knew the direction.
- **Muscle tone.** A `PhysicalAnimationComponent` drives every main joint towards the animated pose in local space, so it shapes the fall but never holds her up. It stays at full strength for `tone.hold` (0.35 s) and fades over `tone.fade` (2.5 s) down to `tone.rest` (10 %), which stays: fully limp, she folded flat at every joint like a sack. The angular damping (`tone.damping`, 0.3 of the strength) keeps limbs from flailing. Legs get about 40 %, so the knees give first and the torso folds over them. The head, hands and feet get none: pulled towards an animated pose the floor was in the way of, they shook at 500-5000°/s while the rest of her lay still. Every body's top angular speed is capped (`spin.main` 720°/s for the torso, arms and legs, `spin.ends` 360 for the head, hands, feet and accessories) and put back to the engine's 3600 when she gets up. A severed limb's root drive is dropped at once.
- **Settling.** On the floor she kept jittering (head and limbs turning at 50-80°/s for seconds): the tone pulling towards the animated pose against the floor, and accessory bodies far lighter than the main ones. Accessories are now capped at 0.8 kg. She counts as lying still when her main bodies (and a split's copy's) are all slower than `settle.speed` (15 cm/s) or none got further than `settle.drift` (4 cm) over the last `settle.window` (0.5 s); bodies that no longer simulate (a split half's gone limbs) don't count: after the joint limits went in, a body shivering in place stayed fast enough that speed alone almost never settled her. The tone lets go the moment she lies still (it pulled her against the floor and the limits), and after `settle.time` (0.5 s) of it every body is put to sleep (`settled after ...`); at least `settle.after` (1 s) in. Past `settle.most` (3 s) she settles once nothing drifts more than `settle.driftMost` (6 cm), however fast it shivers. Every 2 s she doesn't, `not settled at ...` names the fastest body and the one that drifted furthest. Something moving her faster than `settle.wake` wakes her, and she settles again. A split's copy gets no tone: it plays no animation, so the drives would pull it into the bind pose.
- **Weapons.** Her sword hangs off a prop bone without a physics body, so in a ragdoll it stayed in the air where she died. Each weapon showing (mesh slots Weapon1-4) is attached to an invisible box (`PhysicsActor`, not colliding with pawns, CCD on) (its imported bounds, at most `weaponMaxCm` long: the padded bounds made a 217 cm box for her sword) that falls and tumbles, starting with her hand's speed plus `weaponToss` (150 cm/s), weighing `weaponKg`. Recovery puts each back on its socket (`dropWeapon` false turns it off).

- **F7.** A repeatable fall to tune all of this on, pushed two ticks after physics starts (pushed the same tick, most of it was lost: her pelvis read 0 cm/s right after, her head 100 instead of 900; `F7: pushed ...` logs the pelvis speed before and after). Her whole body gets `push` (450 cm/s, every body the same velocity change, so her centre of mass goes), and on top her upper body is shoved `impulse` (900 cm/s) backwards from her own facing, spread over the chest, neck, head, arms and pelvis, and her feet (calves less) are swept the other way at `legKick` (550), so she goes over onto her back (`direction`, `relativeTo` "camera" for the camera's view, `up`). It was one 1200 cm/s push on Spine1 away from the camera, which yanked that body out of the rest. The F7 ragdoll gets everything above except momentum and the death's impact.

Recovery removes the component, puts the masses back and the weapons on their sockets before gore's cleanup recreates the bodies.

A death reloads the level, and the old Eve is freed. The script used to keep her pawn and ask UE4SS whether it was still valid, which reads the freed object: the game crashed on the new Eve's first tick. It now follows the actor whose `ReceiveTick` runs it; a new one is taken once the old one has been silent for a second, and everything held from the old one is dropped without being touched (`new Eve ...` in the log).

## Death face

Eve's death face is [Proper Death Expression](https://www.nexusmods.com/stellarblade/mods/2716) by **blabmice**, used with credit as its permissions allow (details and the file's origin in `third_party/proper-death-expression/README.md`). The vanilla face is a blank stare.

- `vendor` job: the author's `EVE_Facial_ActionVoice_DIE1` facial animation (12 s) ships unchanged in place of the vanilla one, which the death voices `DIE1` / `DIE2` already play.
- `voice` job: `CSS_PC_EVE` gets `DIE3`, the same face with a breath cue at 0.0001 volume, as the author's fixes do.
- `notify` jobs: the grapple, explosive and falling death animations play no death voice, so each gets a new `SBAnimNotify_CharSESound` for `DIE3` at the author's time. The author's own fix reused an existing notify in each, which silenced a cloth or footstep sound; here every vanilla notify stays.

`verify.py` checks the vendored package is unchanged, the new voice copies `DIE1` except for its sound, volume and start, and each animation has exactly one new notify with every vanilla one kept. Don't install the original mod alongside this one; it replaces the same files.

## Asset tools

`assetkit/sbtool`: `dump <uasset> <json>`, `fromjson <json> <uasset>`, `dumpall <content_root> <json_root> [class]` (batch dump with a JSON round-trip check, package paths on stdin), `validate <dir>` (byte-exact round-trip check), all with `SB_USMAP` set. `build.sh` prefers `mappings/SB.usmap` (UE4SS `DumpUSMAP` from the game, full engine schema) over the header-generated one. Only that file decodes engine assets like `SkeletalMesh` and `PhysicsAsset`.

`assetkit/meshtool` (CUE4Parse, built with `assetkit/meshtool/build.sh`) reads meshes from retoc legacy output. `dump` writes bones, sections, positions and skin weights as JSON; `bones` writes only reference skeletons, used by the outfits step; `gltf` writes a `.glb` that imports into Blender with all 323 bones and weights. New meshes can't go back in without the UE editor to cook them. The generated mappings decode 172/197 of Eve's shows byte-exactly. The rest only miss engine structs (camera-shake oscillators, post-process settings) and still round-trip as raw exports.

## Legacy

`legacy/` holds the old 0.x UE4SS Lua mod and the native `PlayShow` log hook. It is superseded: it needed exe byte signatures and a polled text file, and it never worked reliably.

## UE4SS script

`ue4ss/StellarBladeGore` is the UE4SS script. It started as a probe for the ragdoll flinging (runtime fix + logger) and now also runs the gore and the active ragdoll. Install it with `./ue4ss/install.sh`, which sets up the Stellar Blade UE4SS build (pinned and checksummed), and add the Steam launch option `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="dwmapi=n,b"`.

The first probe run showed the impulse isn't the problem. Her pelvis barely travels, but every bone keeps 500–1400 cm/s and up to 9000 deg/s and picks up energy again after settling. That points to self-collision. The mesh runs the `Ragdoll` profile (object type `PhysicsBody`, blocks `PhysicsBody`), so the bodies of her outfit's physics asset collide with each other and with `SBHair` (PhysicsOnly, attached to the head).

On each ragdoll the script makes the mesh ignore `PhysicsBody` and turns `SBHair` collision off. It keeps self-collision when the outfit's physics asset carries the patch (`tune.selfCollision = "auto"`, detected by the pelvis body's damping). It restores both on recovery, and re-attaches the mesh to the capsule if respawn left it detached.

The invisible model after respawn had two causes in SB's engine. `ASBCharacter::SetLockupSimulatePhysics(true)` copies each mesh's world transform into `LockupTransform` (an SB addition to `USkeletalMeshComponent`), and the mesh stays pinned there; the script clears that lock on recovery. The death ragdoll also sets a non-reflected counter on `USceneComponent` (+0x180) that stops the parent from moving the component. Only the mesh had it, so the capsule respawned and the body stayed at the death spot. Lua cannot reach it, so `sbslice.dll` clears it (formationPath `u<hex>` on the same cheat), on recovery and once after the respawn. The probe runs from Eve's `ReceiveTick`: this game gives UE4SS no `UEngine::Tick` hook, so `ExecuteInGameThread` callbacks can land on Blueprint worker threads, and one deadlocked the game. It logs bone motion to `Win64/ue4ss/Mods/StellarBladeGore/stellarbladegore.log`.

With `cameraFollow` on (off by default: it fought how the death plays out, and the game died a second after it moved a really dead Eve's capsule), during any ragdoll her capsule, which the camera rides, drifts after her pelvis instead of staying where she died: nothing within `cameraSlack` (40 cm), then `cameraRate` (0.06) of the rest each tick, held `cameraLift` (95 cm) over it, ignoring her bodies meanwhile so it never shoves them (top level of `tune.lua`: `cameraFollow`, `cameraSlack`, `cameraRate`, `cameraLift`). A recovery then gets her up where she lay.

Sandbox keys: F7 ragdolls her with a shove onto her back using `Scripts/tune.lua` (`push` on the whole body, `impulse` on the upper body, `legKick` sweeping her feet the other way, `direction` relative to her facing or the camera, `up`, self/hair collision, mass scale), F8 recovers, F6 re-reads `tune.lua` without a restart, F9 cuts a limb off, F10 wounds her (blunt, then stab), F11 splits her (Gore), Shift+F5 cuts along a made-up blade. (`SBSuicide` is a no-op in the shipping build, so there is no real death key.) F5 plays her animated death shows in turn (`P_Eve_Dead`, `Dead2`, `Dead3`, then `P_Eve_Dead` with each of the eight knockdown falls) through `SBBlueprintLibrary.PlaySBShowToPlayerCharacter` (`PlaySingleShow` is interface-only and can't be called from Lua): their ragdoll key does nothing while she is alive, so the script starts the death itself `f5Delay` (0.5) s into the fall, as an animated death, with blood and recovery but no death screen. With `f5Split` (the default) no show plays: the death starts on the next tick and splits her top to bottom at once (`F5: show ... via SBBlueprintLibrary: played` in the log). On first load the script also runs UE4SS `DumpUSMAP` once, giving the full engine schema needed to edit physics assets. Remove it with `./ue4ss/install.sh --uninstall`.
