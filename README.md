# Stellar Blade Gore Mod

A gore mod for Stellar Blade: when Eve dies she ragdolls, bleeds and can lose a limb. Three parts:

- **Data patch**, two IoStore paks built from your installed game and the other mods in it: `ZZ_StellarBladeGore_P` (core, ~11 MB: death shows that ragdoll the player, the death table, face, voice) and `ZZ_StellarBladeGore_Outfits_P` (~560 MB: every outfit mesh with the CPU access the cut needs, and its physics asset with the ragdoll fixes). Works on its own.
- **UE4SS script** (`StellarBladeGore`): [gore](#gore) (dismemberment through her own skinning, caps, blood), the [active ragdoll](#active-ragdoll) (masses, momentum, impact, muscle tone), [fixes for outfits the data patch missed](#other-mods), respawn fixes and a log.
- **`sbslice.dll`**: a small native helper the gore needs, loaded by UE4SS.

Formerly `sb-eve-ragdoll-death` / `EveRagdollDeath` / `EveRagdollProbe`; the installers remove the old names.

## How it works

Deaths are driven by `SBShowData` "shows". `Local/Data/CharacterDeadTable` row `Player` picks the show per death type. Most of them (stand, skill steps, explosions) are the shared `Dead/Dead_Monster_*` shows, which already contain an `SBShowRagdollKey` (channel 23 in `KeyContanerMap`). The player never ragdolls only because the key's constructor sets `bIgnorePlayer = true` (`mov byte [rbx+0x1F0], 1` in `USBShowRagdollKey::USBShowRagdollKey`), and no asset overrides it.

The patch (`mod/ragdoll_spec.json`):

- **clone**: copies the shared `Dead_Monster_EffectType_Stand / Explosion / ExplosionLight` and `SkillType_Step0-3` shows to `CH_P_EVE_01/Ragdoll/EveRD_*` with `bIgnorePlayer=false` and Eve-sized impulses (the enemy values of 5k–18k launched her across the map). The `Player` row of the death table points at the copies, so enemy deaths stay fully vanilla. Every `*DeadUIDelayTime` of that row is 7 s (vanilla 0): the death screen fades in only after her fall and the gore have played out; the script gets her up at `recoverAfter` (9 s), under that screen.
- **inject**: adds a ragdoll key, cloned from `Dead_Monster_SkillType_Step1`, to Eve's own death shows: `P_Eve_Dead`, `P_Eve_Dead2`, `P_Eve_Dead3`, `P_Eve_Dead_Nikke`, `Eve_Dead_Down`, `P_Eve_Dead_Falling` and `P_Eve_AirDead`. Each key is tuned per show (impulse, start time), and `bKeepPlaying` is set. Every Eve ragdoll key also gets `ImpulseLimitVelocity=700` and `CapsuleComponentFollowingSocketName=None`. The constructor defaults that to `Bip001-Pelvis`, which drags the player's still-colliding movement capsule onto the ragdoll every frame and flings her around spinning.
- **table**: `Player.AirborneDeadShow` points at the vanilla `CH_P_EVE_01/Basic/P_Eve_Dead_Air`, which does not exist, so airborne deaths had no show at all. It now points at `P_Eve_AirDead`.

- **collision**: Eve's blueprint (`CH_P_EVE_01_Blueprint`) has two components on her own skeleton that block `PhysicsBody`: the sword box `WeaponPhysicsCollision` (on `Bip001-Prop1`) and the `SBHair` mesh. A ragdoll runs on the `Ragdoll` profile (object type `PhysicsBody`), so they shove her own bodies every frame. The patch appends an Ignore entry for `PhysicsBody` to their raw collision responses; nothing else in the blueprint changes. Outfit or hair mods that replace this blueprint will conflict.

- **hit bone**: vanilla keys leave `bOverrideHitBone` false, so every death pushes `ImpulseBoneName` (Spine1). Ragdoll key code (`0x14245eb70`) takes the bone from the hit info when the flag is set and falls back to `ImpulseBoneName` when the hit has none. Eve's keys set it, so a leg hit folds the leg and a head hit snaps the head. Explosions keep the centre push.
- **outfits**: every outfit mesh that `EquipmentTable` can put on Eve (SkeletalMesh on her body skeleton; hair excluded) gets two patches. `assetkit/outfits.py` finds them, and `build.sh` runs it before patching.
  - **physics**: vanilla physics assets let Pelvis collide with both thighs although their capsules overlap by ~10 cm at rest. The solver pushes them apart every frame, which was the flinging. Almost every other pair is disabled, so limbs pass through each other. The patch makes bodies collide across five groups (torso, each arm, each leg). Pairs inside a group never collide: some outfits ship with neighbouring torso bodies colliding, and CH_P_EVE_09 spun from it. Pairs closer than 0.5 cm in that outfit's bind pose stay disabled, for every body. The accessories (hair, cape, breast and hip jiggle, neck pieces: every body outside the five groups) collide with nothing of hers, only the world (`accessory_collision`, off): CH_P_EVE_09 still had 114 accessory pairs colliding with her main bodies, the hip and breast bodies inside her torso shoved the thighs and arms every frame and the cape and hair got pinned between her and the floor, so she shook on the ground (pelvis turning 150-300°/s while lying still). With `self_collision` off (the default) every pair of her bodies is disabled as well, the groups too: an arm or leg landing on her own body set every joint jittering until each part found the floor, and the runtime switch below didn't stop it. Limbs may pass through her. These pairs are measured per asset from the mesh's reference skeleton and the body capsules, and for `CH_P_EVE_30` the result matches the hand-checked list. `LinearDamping` drops from 5.0 to 0.2: at 5.0 she fell at about 2 m/s and looked floaty. `AngularDamping` drops from 10 to 1 (`body` in the spec; a body that has no such property gets one): she toppled and turned as if under water. Some outfits (25 physics assets, e.g. CH_P_EVE_09) give twist or helper bones a body, as the anchor of a cape or skirt chain, but no constraint to anything above it. In gameplay those bodies are kinematic, so it never shows; in a ragdoll they fly free and drag the skin weighted to them, which bent and stretched her arms. Each of them gets a locked joint to the nearest ancestor body, framed at the bind pose (`outfits.lock_orphans`, on by default). The main joints bend about their twist axis (PhAT framed them as hinges) within a range centred on a zero of the asset's own, as soft limits: knees went 19° past straight, elbows 24°, hips bent forward only 30° and back as far, the spine 5° a joint, and under a push the soft limits gave way further. Each joint gets the range of `outfits.ranges` (degrees its far end may swing towards her front and her back from the bind pose: knees 140 back and 3 forward, elbows 145 and 3, hips 110 forward and 20 back, shoulders 120 and 40, each spine joint 15 and 8, the neck 25 and 15). `outfits.py` measures each asset's zero at the bind pose and turns the joint's frame 2 about its twist axis to centre the range there. `swing` overrides cone limits (the hips spread 30° instead of 20), and `hard_limits` turns every limit of the main joints hard (`bSoftConstraint` off). A main joint's motion limited to 0° (CH_P_EVE_09's pelvis-to-spine cone) is locked instead: a hard limit with no range shook it every frame. `locked_joints` locks joints whole (none: locked clavicles hung both arms rigidly off the light neck and the spine buzzed instead). Any motion limited under `lock_below` (6°): the 5° clavicle cones, shoulder roll, hip sideways swing, neck and pelvis-to-spine twist sat at their hard limits all the time and every joint buzzed, even in mid-air. These bodies are kinematic in gameplay, so only ragdolls change.
  - **cpuaccess**: sets `bAllowCPUAccess` on LOD0. The skin cut reads it (see Gore).

  Every patched mesh is a full copy in the outfits pak (all 134 make it about 560 MB compressed). An outfit mod's mesh is patched in its own version, see [Other mods](#other-mods). Use `outfits.only` / `outfits.skip` (mesh-name globs) in the spec to trim the list, or set `outfits.enabled` to false to drop all of it. Meshes and physics assets that don't round-trip byte-exactly through UAssetAPI are skipped and listed in the build log. `ClothingAssetCommon` exports stay raw bytes, because UAssetAPI loses cloth LOD data when it writes them back.

Scripted deaths are left vanilla: swimming, ocean, sliding, desert hole, grinder, laser and balance beam.

## Build / install

Needs `git`, `cargo`, `python3` and `curl`. The .NET 10 SDK is fetched into `.cache/` if it is missing. Point `SB_GAME` at another install if needed.

```bash
./build.sh --install
```

```bash
./build.sh --uninstall
```

`SB_OUTFITS=0 ./build.sh --install` leaves the outfits pak out.

### Other mods

The build reads every container in `Paks/~mods` (subfolders too) over the game's own, in the order the game mounts them (paths sorted case-insensitively, a later one wins), and patches what they ship in their version: a nude or outfit mod's mesh and physics asset, a face mod's face, a mod's `EquipmentTable` and the new meshes it points at. So an outfit mod's look stays and gets the cut and the ragdoll fixes. Packages taken from another mod are listed in the build log and `dist/sources.txt`. Rebuild (`./build.sh --install`) after adding or removing such a mod: the paks hold a copy of what they patched, so a removed mod's outfit would stay, and a new one would load unpatched. A mod that loads after ours (its file name sorts after `ZZ_StellarBladeGore_P`) wins in game over what both replace; the build names it, and renaming it to sort earlier fixes that. Mods that only replace textures or materials don't touch anything of ours. Encrypted containers can't be read and are skipped with a note. Mod containers of an older TOC layout (`DirectoryIndex`, as Eve with no Clothes on ships; the game's are `PartitionSize`) are read alongside the game's: `assetkit/patches/retoc-mixed-toc.patch` lets retoc mix layouts that share the chunk IDs and package format (everything up to `PerfectHash`). An outfit mod's mesh exported without a physics asset (Eve with no Clothes on's `CH_P_EVE_InnerSuit`) still gets CPU access (the build log names it), and in game `physfix.lua` gives it the outfit's own `<mesh>_Physics` (patched by the data mod), else `tune.physfix.fallback` (the base body's): without one she has no bodies and can't ragdoll at all. A given asset is the component's own and can be dropped again (gore's undo re-makes the bodies from the mesh's asset, which is none): `physfix` gives it again on the next tick she is animated (`lost the physics asset it was given`), and every script asks `physfix.asset` (component's, mesh's, else the given one) for her physics asset. Mods only ever change our input: the cache key covers their containers.

An outfit the data patch didn't get (a mod added since the build, one that loads after ours, or no outfits pak) is caught by `physfix.lua` when she puts it on: the log says `physfix: outfit ..., physics ...: NOT patched, LOD0 CPU access false`. Without CPU access it may not be cut (gore logs the DLL's `no CPU copy`), and she just ragdolls; the flag isn't the whole story (InnerSuit read false and still cut). Its physics gets what can be done from the script: the main bodies' damping (in the asset's defaults, bodies re-made while she is animated), hard joint limits, and a locked joint for loose bodies (cape anchors with no joint) while she is ragdolled. Self-collision stays off for such an asset. The data patch's re-centred joint ranges and measured collision pairs need the reference skeleton, so they come only from a rebuild. `tune.physfix` switches each part.

`ue4ss/install.sh` keeps other UE4SS mods: it only adds its two lines to `mods.txt`. `--uninstall` removes this mod's script and DLL and leaves UE4SS; `--uninstall-all` removes UE4SS too.

The script and DLL: `./ue4ss/install.sh` (see [UE4SS script](#ue4ss-script)).

The build pins and fetches [retoc](https://github.com/trumank/retoc), [UAssetAPI](https://github.com/atenfyr/UAssetAPI) and the [SB header dump](https://github.com/a5tronomy/SB), generates `.usmap` mappings from the headers (`assetkit/mkusmap.py`), extracts the assets from the installed game, patches them (`assetkit/patch_shows.py`), repacks `dist/ZZ_StellarBladeGore_P` and `dist/ZZ_StellarBladeGore_Outfits_P` (`.utoc`, `.ucas`, `.pak`) (UE4.27 TOC layout, zlib blocks through `assetkit/patches/retoc-compress.patch`; `SB_COMPRESS=none` stores them raw) and reads the result back to verify it (`assetkit/verify.py`). The output lands in `SB/Content/Paks/~mods/`.

The source side (extracted packages, their JSON, the outfit scan) is cached in `.cache/work` under a key of the game's and the other mods' containers, the mappings, the tool sources and revisions and the spec's outfit selection, so later builds only patch, pack and verify; the first build after a game update extracts everything again. Outfit meshes never go through JSON: `sbtool meshinfo` reads what the outfit scan needs, `sbtool cpuaccess` sets the flag in place and `sbtool checkcpu` verifies that nothing else changed. sbtool's batch commands run `SB_JOBS` packages at a time (default: half the cores), and retoc compresses blocks in parallel.

Tuning: edit `mod/ragdoll_spec.json` (field names as in `USBShowRagdollKey`) and rebuild. `DirectionType`: 0 Static, 1 HitDirection, 2 TargetToSelf, 3 HitLocationToSelf, 4 Velocity, 5 StaticLocal.

## Gore

The game's own dismemberment (`ASBCharacter` slicing, `0x142129c30`, used by the player's sword on some monsters) can't work on Eve. It copies the cut region into procedural meshes and hides it on the original through a vertex-colour override plus the material parameter `DissolveMaskRadiusFactor`. Only the gore master `MA_CH_DamageGore` has that parameter, and cooked materials can't be given one. So the cut is made in the skinning instead, on her own mesh with her own materials.

`native/sbslice` is a tiny freestanding DLL (clang + lld, no MSVC; `native/sbslice/build.sh`). It loads as a UE4SS C++ mod and waits for `gore.lua` to send it the address of the unused cheat UFunction `SBRewardGroupDropFormationPathLocation`. It then swaps that function's native thunk for its own handler, which takes commands through the cheat's string parameter. No byte signatures and no code patches are involved: the exe has no ASLR, and the thunk is matched by its address.

How a cut works (`c<component>,<lod>,<bone>`, a plane point and a normal):

- **Split.** For a plane through bone B, the DLL reads LOD0's positions, indices and skin weights (CPU copies) and skins every vertex in the current pose. A vertex weighted to B's subtree and in front of the plane joins the limb and keeps only limb bones. Every other vertex keeps only body bones. A limb hanging free of the cut goes with B whichever side of the plane it hangs: a bone of B's subtree whose joint and its parent's both lie more than 3 cm in front of the plane, and everything under it (the arms on a waist cut). By position alone, a hand hanging down past the waist stayed on the legs, and her gloves and the suit's cuffs floated where she was cut.
- **Seam.** No vertex is shared across the cut any more. Every vertex of a triangle that crosses the plane goes 100 % to a sink on its own side: a helper bone near the joint that sits where the bind pose puts it relative to its parent, preferably a leaf with few vertices of its own, chosen per mesh section. The section has to contain the sink, because the engine drops weights on bones a section doesn't have. Before each cut `gore.lua` sends `n<component>,<bone>,...`, the bones that may never be a sink: the main skeleton (`Bip001-*`; the pelvis, a clavicle and the head had been picked, and a sink gives up its weights everywhere), hair, capes and breasts (they swing, and the seam swung with them) and accessory bones (`Ab_*`). For a cut through the spine it adds everything under the clavicles and thighs: they swing on their own, and the bind pose's arms hang at the waist, where a strap's seam had collapsed onto a triceps helper. For a limb or head cut it adds every limb but the one being cut: a thigh cut in a fall, her hand resting by the hip, took `Ab-L-Elbow` for the hip's seam, which then rode the flailing arm as a sheet from the hip to the elbow (`ELBOWHIP=1` in the harness puts the elbow on the hip joint and fails if any sink is on another limb). For a limb or head cut it also sends the bones with a physics body (after `fffe`), and the seam then follows the stump: a sink must ride the stump's own body or the one next to it (two bodies away scores ×64), a seam whose sink would ride another body borrows a still helper of the stump's body into its section, and the seam collapses to the middle of the opening, 2 cm inside, instead of the helper's origin (a thigh's seam on a chest helper 44 cm away was drawn up to it as a sheet). Bones the watch saw moving stay out, except as a last resort for a seam whose only other choice is two bodies away (`HIPMOVED=1` in the harness turns the hip's correctives). A cut through the torso sends no bodies and keeps the sinks where they were.
- **Only the body.** A section that doesn't go round the cut (the same test as the caps below: straps, plates, pads, a cape, a loose sleeve) gets no seam. Each of its connected pieces that crosses the cut goes along whole with the side holding at least 85 % of its weight; one tied to both goes whole with the side holding most of its vertices (a strap over the shoulder weighted 2:1 to the arm but lying on the chest stays on the chest), so nothing of it can stretch. It used to vanish whole into a sink, which read as the cut taking a piece of her outfit. Pieces over 4000 vertices or reaching more than 40 cm from the cut are cut like the body instead, unless at most 15 % of their vertices lie on the other side (CH_P_EVE_09's cape, 9 % of it past an arm cut, had a seam torn across it). A seam through them had collapsed onto whatever bone their section had, often far off or swinging, and stretched across the body. A piece reaching more than 6 cm past the plane on both sides, with over 15 % of its vertices and 3 % of its weight on each, is cut too: sent whole, a waist cut's belt straps stood up off her hips like horns and a harness piece reaching 16 cm down her hips went with the upper body. Such a seam takes the sink nearest itself, borrowed into its section's BoneMap when the section has none of that side (CH_P_EVE_09's harness knows only `Bip001-Spine` on the hips). The log shows `pieces=` (how many went whole).
- **Collapse.** The sink's inverse bind matrix becomes a zero scale at its own origin, so the seam collapses onto the sink's live position whatever drives it (animation or physics). Triangles across the cut become lines, and the ring next to them closes the opening. The sink's own vertices move to their strongest other bone first. Weight that leaves any bone (a sink, a helper the cut drops) goes to that bone's nearest ancestor that stays, else to the strongest other bone: a helper without a body rides its parent at a fixed offset, so the parent skins the vertex where the helper did. Moved to the strongest bone, a split's shoulder skin went onto the chest and tore off the clavicle by up to 23 cm once she moved. `HideBone` can't do this: a hidden bone renders at its parent's bind origin and stops updating, which stretched the severed limb back to the cut.
- **Apply and report.** The weights go to the engine's own `USkinnedMeshComponent::SetSkinWeightOverride`. The DLL reports both openings, fitted to the rim vertices in their new pose: centre, axes and an ellipse in the cut plane. The cap belongs on the limb, not on what is strapped to it, so rim points are grouped by mesh section first. A section whose points go round the cut axis (11 of 16 directions) is a layer of the limb. The innermost layer and the ones sitting tight on it (skin, a body suit) get the cap; straps, plates, capes and loose sleeves are left out and close up in their own material. With no layer at all, every point counts. Of what is left, points further than 1.6× the median from the cut axis are dropped too. The log line shows the grouping (`bsec=section/points/directions/radius`, `bcore=` the kept sections, `l...` for the limb side). `bb=` and `lb=` name the bone each side's rim rides (the most weighted one over its rim vertices, sinks left out): the caps, stubs and bleeding ride it, not the cut bone's parent, so they stay on the stump's skin (a thigh's parent is `Bip001-Spine`, while its stump hangs on the pelvis helpers, and the stub slid to the edge of the stump as she moved).
- **Open.** The sinks are recorded but collapse only on `k`, a few frames later. Collapsing them under the old weights would pull every vertex those helpers moved onto their origins until the GPU has the new weights.

The command is `c<component>,<lod>,<bone>[,<mode>,<radius mm>[,<sink>,<remap>]]`. Besides the plain cut (mode 0) there are three modes (3, 5 and 6 were the old wounds: a hole, a dent and a rupture, removed with them; a hit she lives through now leaves a mark, see Hits she lives through). `m<component>,<lod>,<reach cm>` asks where a ray from LocX/Y/Z along DirX/Y/Z meets the skin, changing nothing (`hit=`, `n=`, `bone=`, `out=`):

- **Whole (1).** Every vertex follows B's subtree; no seam. Eve's face is its own mesh, weighted to the head and the neck, so a beheading moves all of it onto the head (cut in Plane mode like the neck, 8300 of its vertices vanished into the stump and the head came off faceless).
- **Plane (2).** The limb side is what is in front of the plane and within the radius of its axis, whatever the weights. The head's subtree is the head alone (the clavicles hang off the neck) and the outfit skin has no head bone near the neck, so the neck is cut by position, mid-neck.
- **Side (4).** Half of a split, at any angle: what is in front of the plane stays, what is behind it goes. It isn't collapsed into points: the first version did that with one sink per section, so the whole other half, legs included, went into one spot near the chest and every triangle along the midline stretched from the hips or the neck to it. Now the torso just behind the plane (5 cm) is flattened onto it, and what lies deeper collapses to a point on the plane in its band (flattened, a slice across the waist would spread the shoulders out past the opening). Its sinks get a projection instead of a zero scale: inverse bind matrix, then onto the plane (2 cm inside the kept side, behind the lids; at 0.3 cm the sheet came out in front of the lid wherever she bent) in the sink bone's frame as posed at the cut. Each vertex lands where it was, only pushed onto the plane, so the midline closes without anything reaching across. The sheet is banded the way the lid is cut into pieces: each vanishing vertex goes to the band of the spine bone it lands nearest to (Lua's `SPLIT_RIDE`, sent after the limbs; each half sends only the spine bones it keeps, `SPLIT_RIDE.of`: the copy's head breaks free in its ragdoll and spun at up to 8000 deg/s, and the lid and sheet that rode it rose from the body in tall dark sheets), and a band's sinks must ride that bone (the first main bone up their chain; under a clavicle or a breast they swing on their own). A bone no free helper rides (CH_P_EVE_09's Spine and Spine1) gets no band. A flattened vertex is weighted between the two bands of its section it lies between, by where it sits along the spine, so the sheet bends like skin. With four bands along the opening and whatever sink was nearest, a pelvis helper carried 43 cm of the sheet up to the ribs, clavicle helpers swung with the arms, and once she bent the sheet tore between bands and came out through the lid (the playtest's stretch probe: thousands of stretched triangles, all on the split's own sinks). A limb never splits lengthwise: Lua sends `g<component>,<gone roots>,0,<kept roots>,0,<ride bones>[,0,<dropped chains>]` (each arm and thigh by the joint it hangs from, shoulder or hip: by the middle of its first segment, an arm hanging past a slash across the stomach went with the legs and was cut off at the shoulder), the kept half's limbs stay whole wherever they lie, and the other half's limbs collapse to a point on the plane where they joined. Accessory chains off the torso or neck (the top of each chain of 2+ physics bodies outside `Bip001`, not under a limb or the head) go the same way, each to the side where its bodies lie on average: kept whole with the kept roots, or dropped whole (each vertex mostly on it collapsed to a point in its band). Halved, the neck chain's swinging half stretched to the flattened sheet across the opening. Kept skin lets go of every helper past the plane and on it (within 1 cm), so the other half's sinks never pull on it, and the halves no longer need re-cutting. Both halves are components of one mesh asset, so their sinks are shared: a vertex the vanilla skin puts wholly on a helper the other half has just made its sink is reweighted like any other (it used to count as an earlier cut's seam vertex and was flattened with the other half: CH_P_EVE_09's neck, on `Ab-NeckSub`). A section with no helper to flatten onto (CH_P_EVE_09's neck lace knows only the neck and the spine) borrows the half's nearest one into its BoneMap, like the face below. The opening runs head to crotch and isn't convex. The DLL traces it as one outline (`s0p=`, up to 200 points): the rim points and the mesh's own cross-section with the plane are drawn on a 0.8 cm grid, the outside is flooded from a corner, the inside filled and its largest piece's border chained; a fill much smaller than its outline's area means the outside leaked in through a gap, and the gap closing is doubled up to 12 cm. Bands along it only pick the sinks now. `h<component>,<mode>,<head bone>` (point and normal: the neck plane) goes before a half's split cut: Eve's hair (`CH_P_EVE_Hair02`: a root and its hair chains, no body bone) can't be halved, so the head never is. Lua puts the neck plane at the neck's base. Mode 1 keeps everything above it whole within `NECK_BASE_R` (7 cm) of the neck axis up to halfway to the head bone, and `NECK_R` (10 cm, her collar) above, and closes the strip under it on the far side into the neck (halfway up the neck, a split leaned 2 cm beside it took the far side's lower neck; 10 cm wide at the base, it kept the far trapezius, which stretched to where the dropped arm collapsed); mode 2 lets the head go into a sink at the neck and sends a second opening, `n` (`np=`). Its kept skin keeps nothing on the head's subtree: that weight goes to the head's parent, the neck, as a gone limb's does (557 of CH_P_EVE_10's neck vertices hung partly on the head, Neck 204 / Head 51, and stretched after it). No sink may be a bone the bone watch saw move while she played (an animation-driven corrective such as the `Ab-AX3` family would carry the flattened sheet with it), nor one the head's or the neck's skin hangs on (sinks are the mesh asset's, so the other half's head would let go of it), and such a bone is never one the kept skin drops, wherever it lies (`Ab-NeckSub` swings past the plane in a pose). The answer adds `proj=` and `point=`. `<sink>` forces a sink bone for sections that have nothing to flatten onto: Eve's eyes, teeth and brows only know the head bone, so the face borrows `Bip001-Spine1`, which no face vertex uses. The DLL adds it to the section's BoneMap in place when the array has room (an engine array always has room for 8 entries); a fuller one is swapped for a copy with room for 512, put back on undo. Borrowing only in place had left a waist split's copy with 10617 vertices of the upper body standing on the legs, its sections full and, on `k`, points the component's `MasterBoneMap` entry for it at the master's bone `<remap>` (the head), so the face's vanished side flattens in the head's frame. An F11 / F5 / odds split leans its plane from the crotch to `tune.gore.splitBeside` (2) cm beside her neck, so the head stays whole on her half and the cut runs between her breasts (blade splits follow the blade). Lua sorts a split's arms by the shoulder joint (`SPLIT_LIMBS`), not the clavicle by the neck. A dropped limb's point rides the spine bone nearest where it joined, else any spine bone (never flattened onto the plane: a flat hand).

`p<src>,<dst>` copies one component's pose onto another on the same skeleton, for the split's copy.

`s<component>,<lod>` is the stretch probe: it skins every vertex exactly as the GPU does (the component's pose, its overrides) and answers `ok stretched=<n> of <triangles> hidden=<n> longest=<cm> bones=<bone>:<count>:<cm>;... shown=<bone>:<count>;...`, the triangles with an edge more than 3x and 15 cm longer than at bind (`hidden`: those wholly on sinks with under 1 cm² left; a split's flattening sinks only squash the skin onto the plane, so a sheet stretched between two bands still shows) and the bones they hang on most (`shown`: counting only the triangles that show). `gore.lua` logs it with bone names (`split stretch, her half / copy` at 0.5 and 2 s after a split).

In every mode, a limb-side vertex in a section that can't follow the limb, and every limb-side vertex of a section that has seam vertices on that side but no limb sink, vanishes: it joins the body side's seam and collapses with it, which beats a triangle stretched from the flying piece to the body. The log shows `vanished=`.

`x<component>,<lod>` releases the override, restores that mesh's matrices and the component's borrowed `MasterBoneMap` entries. `r` restores every collapsed matrix and borrowed BoneMap entry, since they live on the shared mesh asset. A BoneMap that had no room for a borrowed bone gets a bigger array from the engine's own allocator (`GMalloc`), so the engine can free it when the mesh goes; one from `VirtualAlloc` corrupted its heap and the game crashed on quit (Alt+F4: `MallocBinned2 Corruption Canary`). A swapped array the restore puts back is freed through `GMalloc` too. A borrowed bone also joins the LOD's `ActiveBoneIndices`: the GPU skin builds a matrix each frame only for those (the sections' own BoneMaps and their ancestors), so a helper with no skin of its own (`Dm-R-Thigh-point`, `Ab-AX3` on CH_P_EVE_10) skinned whatever its sink or stub vertices with leftover matrix data, and they flew across the screen somewhere new every frame. The array isn't in the exe's symbols, so the DLL finds it in the LOD's render data by its contents, once (`active=<offset>/<bones added>` in a cut's result; `0/0` = not found, and then no bone outside it is ever borrowed). The harness keeps the same array and fails a cut that leaves any vertex on a bone outside it (`NOACTIVE=1` hides it from the DLL).

- the neck, with the chest clearly on the other side from the head: a beheading, leaning with the swing;
- anywhere else through or along the spine: the body in two along the plane, at any angle (down the middle, across the waist, shoulder to hip). - nothing usable (no plane, or a plane missing her): the usual roll.

## Active ragdoll

**Animated deaths (experimental, `active.animated`).** Stand and light-hit deaths play her own fall instead of a ragdoll. The death table points `StandDeadShow`, `Step0DeadShow` and `Step1DeadShow` at `P_Eve_Dead`, `P_Eve_Dead2` and `P_Eve_Dead3` (they pointed at the animation-less ragdoll clones), and the ragdoll key in those, `P_Eve_Dead_Nikke` and `Eve_Dead_Down` carries `ImpulsePower 1` and `ImpulseLimitVelocity 701`. That key is only the signal that she died: `main.lua` reads the 701 in `RagdollInfo`, turns physics back off within a frame and lets the show's animation play on. It holds its end pose (`bHoldEndTime`), so nothing jiggles on the floor. Cuts happen on the animated body as on a standing Eve (F9): the piece simulates on its own. A waist cut or a split switches to the ragdoll before cutting (`animated death: ragdoll after all`). Heavy hits (`Step2`, `Step3`), explosions, airborne and ledge deaths stay ragdolls. Only death shows carry a ragdoll key, so a knockdown (`Result_Knock_Down*`, `Down_BS`, `Groggy_*`, getups) never counts as a death. The log says `death: animated fall, physics off` or `death: ragdoll show`. More falls: the data mod builds eight death shows from her knockdown falls (`Ragdoll/EveDeath_*`: `Result_Knock_Down_Start`, `_Start1`, `_Forward`, `_Backward`, `Result_State_KnockDown_S_Fw`, `_S_Bw`, `_Tumble_Fw`, `_Tumble_Bw`), each a copy of `P_Eve_Dead` with the fall swapped in (`animshow` in `patch_shows.py`), held on its last frame for 120 s (`hold`: ending 0.9 s after the fall let the game's dead pose, a different one, snap in; recovery stops it with `StopAllShow`), `Eve_Dead_Common` (death face and voice) kept and no ragdoll key. Nothing in the game refers to them and they aren't in its asset registry, so UE4SS's `LoadAsset` can't find them: `P_Eve_Dead` refers to all eight (`preload`): a play-show key each, in its play-show channel but disabled (`bEnable` false) and timed at 1e9 s, so they load with it, stay loaded while it is, and it never plays them. A bare import of them, the first try, was dropped by the IoStore conversion; keys in no channel loaded them, but nothing referred to the keys and the next garbage collection took them and the falls (`show ... didn't load` a minute after the show loaded; the line now says whether the host and its first key are there); `verify.py` checks the keys. The knockdown shows and animations themselves are untouched. An animated death picks one of the nine at random (her own show's fall or one of these, `animatedPool`; off by default, so only her own falls play) and plays it over her death show through `SBBlueprintLibrary.PlaySBShowToPlayerCharacter` (`animated death: fall ...`; `PlaySingleShow` is only on the game's show interface, which Lua can't call, and silently did nothing). CUE4Parse can't decode the game's animation codec, so their end poses aren't checked offline: at 3 s and 5 s the log gives her head's height over the floor and flags a fall that doesn't end lying (`NOT LYING, drop this fall`). `meshtool anims` reads the lengths.

`ue4ss/StellarBladeGore/Scripts/active.lua` runs on every ragdoll (real deaths and F7), settings under `active` in `tune.lua`:

- **Masses.** Vanilla physics assets set `bOverrideMass` on the spine and jiggle bodies without a value, so each weighs the engine default of 100 kg; cape and accessory links weigh up to 80 kg. The main bodies get a human distribution (about 60 kg in all, `massScale`). Every other body is capped at `accessoryMaxKg`, and all of those hanging off one main body share `accessoryShare` (15 %) of its mass: CH_P_EVE_09's cape hangs off the upper arms (a twist body and 7 links a side) and the ponytail off the head (9 links), and at 0.8 kg a link they weighed 6.4 kg on a 1.7 kg arm and 7.2 kg on a 4.5 kg head and pulled both out of their joints. The log line shows the masses before and after and how many hang off each body. A split's copy gets the same masses (it kept the outfit's 100 kg chest bodies). `stretch at ...` lines (0.5, 1, 2 and 3 s) show the three main joints furthest off their length at the start, in cm; joints gore cut or broke on purpose (a severed limb's root, the split's spine break) are left out.
- **Momentum.** The death show stops the capsule before the ragdoll starts, so she used to drop on the spot even at a sprint. Her speed from the last third of a second carries into the fall (`momentum`, `momentumMax`).
- **Impact.** A push along the killing blow (the death key's own impulse, else from where the blow came: her mesh's hit or the nearest enemy, else from the hit location through the pelvis), spread over the upper body (`impact`, 450 cm/s, `impactUp`), plus a small random turn about the vertical (`twist`, 35°/s). A death used to drop dead on the spot: the key gave no impulse and nothing else knew the direction.
- **Muscle tone.** A `PhysicalAnimationComponent` drives every main joint towards the animated pose in local space, so it shapes the fall but never holds her up. It stays at full strength for `tone.hold` (0.35 s) and fades over `tone.fade` (2.5 s) down to `tone.rest` (10 %), which stays: fully limp, she folded flat at every joint like a sack. The angular damping (`tone.damping`, 0.3 of the strength) keeps limbs from flailing. Legs get about 40 %, so the knees give first and the torso folds over them. The head, hands and feet get none: pulled towards an animated pose the floor was in the way of, they shook at 500-5000°/s while the rest of her lay still. Nor do the neck, clavicles and upper arms: with tone they flipped their spin round on 40-60% of ticks in mid-air (`buzz over the first 2 s` in the log, with `active.bisect`), without it hardly. Tone ends when she lands (`tone.landOff`). Every body's top angular speed is capped (`spin.main` 720°/s for the torso, arms and legs, `spin.ends` 360 for the head, hands, feet and accessories) and put back to the engine's 3600 when she gets up. The engine's cap alone didn't hold (her head shook at 1000-8000°/s on the floor), so every tick each main body turning faster than its cap is slowed to it (`spin.clamp`). A main body flying faster than her torso by more than `spin.linearOver` (1200 cm/s, and over `spin.linearFloor` 1500) is slowed to that every tick too (`spin.linear`; a leg cut that switched her fall to the ragdoll shot a hand off at 15000 cm/s); the log names the first (`flung: ...`) and the settle line counts them. A severed limb's root drive is dropped at once.
- **Settling.** On the floor she kept jittering (head and limbs turning at 50-80°/s for seconds): the tone pulling towards the animated pose against the floor, and accessory bodies far lighter than the main ones. Accessories are now capped at 0.8 kg. She counts as lying still when her main bodies (and a split's copy's) are all slower than `settle.speed` (15 cm/s) or none got further than `settle.drift` (4 cm) over the last `settle.window` (0.5 s); bodies that no longer simulate (a split half's gone limbs) don't count: after the joint limits went in, a body shivering in place stayed fast enough that speed alone almost never settled her. The tone lets go the moment she lies still (it pulled her against the floor and the limits), and after `settle.time` (0.5 s) of it every body is put to sleep (`settled after ...`); at least `settle.after` (1 s) in. Past `settle.most` (3 s) she settles once nothing drifts more than `settle.driftMost` (6 cm), however fast it shivers. Every 2 s she doesn't, `not settled at ...` names the fastest body, the one that drifted furthest and the fastest turning one. Something moving her faster than `settle.wake` wakes her, and she settles again. A split's copy gets no tone: it plays no animation, so the drives would pull it into the bind pose. Her half lets go of its tone the moment the split opens: with it, her half stood stiff on its one leg and toppled slowly like a plank while the limp copy dropped, and the drives towards her live animation kept her half shaking on the floor. Both halves turn at most `spin.splitMain` (360°/s) and `spin.splitEnds` (240), and settle past `settle.splitMost` (1.5 s) once nothing drifts more than `settle.driftMost`.
- **Weapons.** Her sword hangs off a prop bone without a physics body, so in a ragdoll it stayed in the air where she died. Each weapon showing (mesh slots Weapon1-4) is attached to an invisible box (`PhysicsActor`, not colliding with pawns, CCD on) (its imported bounds, at most `weaponMaxCm` long: the padded bounds made a 217 cm box for her sword) that falls and tumbles, starting with her hand's speed plus `weaponToss` (150 cm/s), weighing `weaponKg`. Recovery puts each back on its socket (`dropWeapon` false turns it off).

- **F7.** A repeatable fall to tune all of this on, pushed two ticks after physics starts (pushed the same tick, most of it was lost: her pelvis read 0 cm/s right after, her head 100 instead of 900; `F7: pushed ...` logs the pelvis speed before and after). Her whole body gets `push` (450 cm/s, every body the same velocity change, so her centre of mass goes), and on top her upper body is shoved `impulse` (900 cm/s) backwards from her own facing, spread over the chest, neck, head, arms and pelvis, and her feet (calves less) are swept the other way at `legKick` (550), so she goes over onto her back (`direction`, `relativeTo` "camera" for the camera's view, `up`). It was one 1200 cm/s push on Spine1 away from the camera, which yanked that body out of the rest. The F7 ragdoll gets everything above except momentum and the death's impact.

Recovery removes the component, puts the masses back and the weapons on their sockets before gore's cleanup recreates the bodies.

A death reloads the level, and the old Eve is freed. The script used to keep her pawn and ask UE4SS whether it was still valid, which reads the freed object: the game crashed on the new Eve's first tick. It now follows the actor whose `ReceiveTick` runs it; a new one is taken once the old one has been silent for a second, and everything held from the old one is dropped without being touched (`new Eve ...` in the log).

## Her voice

The moment she dies (a real death or a test key's ragdoll) she cries out once: one of Eve's own death cries, `vo_eve_die_01`-`05` (the SoundWaves the game's cues pick from, under `Sound/Dialogue/ActionVoice/Eve`, localized like the rest of her voice), at her head with her action voice's attenuation, pitch 0.97-1.03. Nothing follows it: the screams on later cuts, moans and last breath of the first version played on over her body and are gone. A recording rests `rest` (8) s before it plays again. `tune.gore.voice`: `enabled` (false: only the game's own death voice), `volume`; the log line is `voice: <recording> at <volume>`.

## Hits she lives through

A hit she lives through leaves a slash of blood on her skin, no cut (`tune.gore.hitMarks`, on by default; `enabled = false` turns it off). The hit her mesh reports (`mesh hit` in the log) is marked `delay` (0.25) s later if she is still alive, so a killing blow's hit gets the death cut alone, and with `needDamage` only when her HP bar (the HUD's `ProgressBar_HP`, see Glass cannon) dropped, so a guarded or parried hit leaves nothing. The physics only says where its capsule was hit, so the DLL finds the skin (sbslice `m`): a ray from the axis of the part nearest the hit (neck, spine, upper arms, forearms, thighs, calves) out through the hit point, the first skin it leaves through within 30 cm (not an arm beside her body further out), then the outermost layer within 3 cm of that (a strap or a plate), and the skin's normal there. The mark is a deferred decal (the Raven cinematic's streaky floor blood, `MI_FloorBloodDecal_5`) attached to the bone that weighs most on that skin, so it moves with her. It projects into the skin along that normal and is stretched `length` (12 cm) by `width` (2.2) along the swing: the nearest enemy's fastest weapon bone's motion over the last 0.3 s, else the hit's push. It's 75-125 % of that size at random, its length kept within 1.3 times the part's radius so it stays on a forearm's curve, and its box reaches only `depth` (0.5) of the part's radius into her, so it never shows on the far side. At most `max` (12) at once, the oldest goes first; `blood` adds the hit's blood burst on it. Marks heal: each remembers her HP bar before its hit, and once healing has lifted the bar from its lowest since then back to that reading, the mark fades over `healFade` (2.5) s and goes (a mark from before the HUD's bar was found waits for a full bar). All of them go when she recovers or respawns. F10 puts a mark on a part at random, on its side facing the camera. The log says `hit mark on <part> (rides <bone>): L x W cm at ..., N cm out of the bone, N cm off the reported hit`, `hit mark healed: ...` and `hit mark skipped: <why>`. `test/run.sh mark` checks the skin query on CH_P_EVE_09, 10 and 30, plain and posed: rays four ways out of the torso, an upper arm and a thigh must meet the skin 3-20 cm out, on a vertex that part carries, with the normal along the ray.

## Hits after death

An enemy still swinging at her body cuts it again (`tune.gore.afterHits`). During the death the script keeps following the nearest enemy's weapon bones (the killing swing's tracker) and checks the step its fastest bone made each tick against her parts (head, neck, spine, arms and legs). A step faster than `swingMinSpeed` passing within `reach` (35) cm of one is a blow there: `cutChance` (0.75) of them cut along the plane the weapon swept, the same way a killing blow does (a limb, the head, or, while she is still whole, her body in two), the rest leave a hit mark where they passed (see Hits she lives through), all with blood. A blow that would split a body already cut leaves a mark instead. One hit per swing (the weapon has to slow down first), at most `max` (6) a death, none in the first `from` (0.6) s, so the killing swing doesn't land twice. A split body only bleeds. The log line is `after death (N s in): <enemy>'s <bone> at N cm/s passed N cm from her <part>, hit i of n`.

## Death face

Eve's death face is [Proper Death Expression](https://www.nexusmods.com/stellarblade/mods/2716) by **blabmice**, used with credit as its permissions allow (details and the file's origin in `third_party/proper-death-expression/README.md`). The vanilla face is a blank stare.

- `vendor` job: the author's `EVE_Facial_ActionVoice_DIE1` facial animation (12 s) ships unchanged in place of the vanilla one, which the death voices `DIE1` / `DIE2` already play.
- `voice` job: `CSS_PC_EVE` gets `DIE3`, the same face with a breath cue at 0.0001 volume, as the author's fixes do.
- `notify` jobs: the grapple, explosive and falling death animations play no death voice, so each gets a new `SBAnimNotify_CharSESound` for `DIE3` at the author's time. The author's own fix reused an existing notify in each, which silenced a cloth or footstep sound; here every vanilla notify stays.

`verify.py` checks the vendored package is unchanged, the new voice copies `DIE1` except for its sound, volume and start, and each animation has exactly one new notify with every vanilla one kept. Don't install the original mod alongside this one; it replaces the same files.

In the mod's deaths the face never stayed: `DIE1` starts as she dies (her eyelids were closing 0.2 s in), but by 1 s every curve was back at 0 and she stared with open eyes. The mod holds a dead face itself (`tune.gore.deathFace`): from `at` (0.3) s into a death it sets morph targets on her face mesh, fading them in over `fade` (0.5) s and setting them again every tick until she gets up (a master-posed face adds its own morph targets over the ones it takes from her body's curves). `morphs` lists each as its JALI name, its ARKit twin and a weight; the first name her face mesh has is used, so the eyes never close twice. By default: eyes shut, jaw a little slack, mouth corners down, a slight wince. The log names what it holds (`face (death): holding N of her M morph targets`), or lists the face's morph names when none matched. Shift+F7 shows it on her living face for 3 s.

## Enemies

The generic humanoid enemies get the same cuts as Eve (`enemies.lua`, `tune.enemies`): the skulls (sword, spear, hammer, gunner), the lab mutant, the three statues and the two lurkers (`kinds`, each with its `CharacterTableID` and body mesh; hunchbacks have no Bip001 spine and VipGuard's head helpers are hair only, so they're left out), and the Airborne Squad, the angels of the endings (`N_AirborneSquad`, 30030). Every live character of those classes is adopted as it shows up, the game's own slicer switched off on it (`nativeSlice` false). When one dies within `reach` (25 m) of her, a blade cuts it (`cutChance`): her swing (`bladeChance` of the time; the arc of her right hand's fastest stretch over `swingWindow`, at least `swingMinSpeed` cm/s, else that stretch and her forward) through where its mesh was hit in the last `hitWindow` s, or through the part nearest the plane; with no swing, a level, slanted or downward blade from her side through a hit or random part. Gore then cuts the limbs and neck that blade crosses, else rolls `odds` (limb, head, torso). No splits, no face, no death show. Gore busy with another cut retries for `killWait` s, falling back limb, head, torso. Her swings through a lying corpse cut it again (`corpseHits`: up to `max` per corpse, `cutChance`, else a hit mark there), and a corpse that leaves (destroyed, pooled and hidden, or gone from the level) takes its pieces, caps, bleeds and decals with it. The game dissolves a corpse some 15 s before it hides it, its despawn show swapping the materials for `MI_DeadMaterial_*`: once a slot shows one, its caps, bone stubs, bleeds and drips go (`dissolving` in the log; they hung in the air where it had been), and it is cut no more. `maxCorpses` cut corpses at once.

Keys: F5 spawns the picked kind `spawnDistance` cm in front of her, facing her (through `SBNetworkPlayerController:ServerRequest_CreateActor`, which runs locally in single player), Shift+F5 picks the next kind, Shift+F6 shows or hides the spawner window. The window (`native/sbslice/spawner.c`, in `sbslice.dll`, its own thread) lists the kinds from `spawner.list` (written by the script) and has Spawn, Spawn 3, Remove all and Kill nearest; it asks the script through `spawner.req`, so all spawning happens on the game thread. Closing it only hides it. While it shows (`spawner.state`, written by the window), the script gives it the mouse: the player controller shows the cursor and takes game and UI input unlocked (`WidgetBlueprintLibrary.SetInputMode_GameAndUIEx`), and goes back to game-only input when it hides (`spawner window open: mouse freed` in the log). The game had kept the cursor captured, and nothing in the window could be clicked. Kill nearest (also Ctrl+F6) ragdolls the followed character nearest her, which the script then takes for its death and cuts.

The Airborne Squad are built like Eve: `CH_P_EVE_01_Body` on her skeleton and physics, a face mesh of their own (`CH_P_EVE_Face_003`) and her hair. They are cut with her rules (`face = true` on the kind): a head cut moves the face whole onto the head, as on her, and their `Ab_` helpers aren't sinks. They are passive NPCs (no behaviour tree, a tribe her blows don't hurt), so they die only by Kill nearest, and they are never taken for her killer (`gore.attackerIgnore`). Their body is the same mesh asset as her first outfit: while a cut angel lies around, Eve in that outfit may pinch near the helpers its cut used. The game has no hostile angels besides the bosses Tachy and Raven; the Elder's angels in the final fight are his skill's own meshes, not characters.

Enemies of a kind share one mesh asset, and a cut collapses inverse bind matrices on that asset (its sinks). So a cut corpse releases its own override with `x<comp>,<lod>,1` (its weights only, the asset's sinks kept), and the asset's sinks are restored (`r<mesh asset>`) once no cut corpse of it is left; the full `r` (which also gives back borrowed BoneMaps) only runs with no cuts at all. Live enemies of a kind may pinch near the helpers a cut used while one of its cut corpses lies around. An enemy's `Ab_` bones may be sinks (they are its only deform helpers; its head vanished without them), and its head is cut like a limb (`MODE_CUT`: past the plane goes with the head) since it has no separate face. When a section has no sink of a side in its BoneMap, the DLL borrows the nearest helper of that side into it. The bodies need LOD0 CPU access (`cpuaccess` entries under `Art/Character/Monster` and the Airborne Squad's in `mod/ragdoll_spec.json`). `native/sbslice/test/run.sh` cuts an arm, the head and the waist on each kind's real body (with the enemy sink list) and checks no stretched triangle stays visible; the waist test's flung arms take the arm helpers that hang off the clavicle along, as the animation does.

## Glass cannon

A realistic glass cannon mode, off by default (`tune.glassCannon.enabled`; Shift+F8 turns it on and off in game). While her shield is up a hit takes the shield, never HP (vanilla lets part of it through). Once the shield is down, any hit kills her on the spot: cut along the enemy weapon's swing when one landed (as any killing blade, so a swing through her arm goes on into her body), else where it landed (the head off; the body split when the blow came down from above, else cut across the waist; an arm or a leg off). When the HP runs out the usual way, the finishing blow takes off what it landed on. Her blows do `damageMult` (8) times the damage: what every enemy within `foeReach` of her loses, HP and shield, is multiplied, though a multiplied blow leaves it at 1 HP rather than killing it.

Where a hit landed: the nearest enemy weapon's steps over the last `swingBack` s (the swing tracker of the killing cuts) passing within `reach` cm of a part of her, else where her mesh says it was hit (`meshHitWindow`), else a part picked by weight (`glass: ... landed on ...` in the log).

Her HP and shield exist only in native memory (no reflected property, and the cheat manager's HP and effect commands are stubs in the shipping build), so `sbslice.dll` finds them (`native/sbslice/stats.c`): an array of floats indexed by `ESBActorStatType` (HP 1, MaxHP 2, Shield 7, MaxShield 8), looked for in her actor and the objects it points to, two pointers deep, anchored on the numbers her HUD widget holds (`WB_MainHUD_PlayerInfo`'s `HpValue`, `MaxHpValue`, `ShieldValue`, `MaxShieldValue`, from the live instance showing a MaxHP: `FindFirstOf` handed back the all-zero class default). Never by the array's shape alone: thousands of float runs look like stats, and the one taken read HP 0 of 700. The game fills those numbers only now and then (both live widgets read MaxHP 0 for a whole fight in the twenty-third playtest, so nothing was guarded and her damage wasn't multiplied), so without them the stats are searched by her hits: `vs` notes every stats-shaped array in her actor tree (candidates), `vd<actor>,0` after a quiet spell drops the ones whose MaxHP changed, whose HP rose or whose HP changed on 6 checks running (timers; two running lost her array to a combo), and `vd<actor>,1` just after a hit on her mesh keeps the ones whose HP is under what it was when noted (so a hit the script missed doesn't lose her array); each hit's log line also shows what her HUD widgets hold (numbers, HP bar, HP text). Her HP bar (`ProgressBar_HP.Percent`) is the one HUD value the game keeps live (0.41 after a hit in the twenty-sixth playtest, the numbers 0, the text a stale "123 / 123"), so every 0.5 s, once the bar has held still at a new reading under 0.98, `vd<actor>,3` (the reading in LocX) keeps the candidates whose HP / MaxHP is within 0.015 of it, heals included; the bar is read off the widget showing it, kept (FindAllOf only every 5 s). Once what is left reads as one array, its paths are the stats (`glass: after a hit: ok found=...`). The first hit lands as in the vanilla game. Her hits mostly go unseen (the mesh's hit shake came 18 times in a whole log), so her death settles it: `vd<actor>,2` keeps what went from above 0 to 0 (nothing at 0: nothing dropped), the bar's check goes first (that playtest's death left the bar at 0.044 and nothing at 0: `err none at 0`); her first life in the mode is the vanilla game's, and a note says when her HP is found (`glass: at her death: ...`). the stats found are checked against the HUD's maximums every 2 s and looked for again after three misses (the HUD's HP lags what the guard gives back, so it isn't compared). An enemy read at HP 0 isn't followed. The pointer path is followed again on every read, and enemies, also `ASBCharacter`s, are read through the same path. Every tick the DLL gives back HP a hit took while her shield was up, multiplies the enemies' losses and writes what happened to `sbslice.evt` (`glass: hit ...`, `guard ...`, `enemy ...`). Shift+F8 shows a note at the top of the screen that fades out by itself ("Glass cannon ON" / "OFF"): a click-through popup drawn by `sbslice.dll`'s window thread (`toast\t<text>` in `spawner.cmd`), since the shipping build strips the engine's own screen messages. `damageMult` is 8 by default (it was 4). A head or body hit sets her HP to 0; if the game doesn't take that as a death within `deathWait` s, the mod's own death runs (as Ctrl+F5's, with the cut) and she is up again after `recoverAfter` at full HP. `native/sbslice/test/run.sh` checks the find (her array, not a decoy of the same shape; refused without the HUD's numbers, none when they match nothing), reads and writes through the path, the guard, the multiplier and a lost actor on made-up actors (`test/test_stats.c`). Untested in game: whether the found array is the one the game uses, and whether HP 0 kills her.

## Asset tools

`assetkit/sbtool`: `dump <uasset> <json>`, `fromjson <json> <uasset>`, `dumpall <content_root> <json_root> [class]` (batch dump with a JSON round-trip check, package paths on stdin), `validate <dir>` (byte-exact round-trip check), all with `SB_USMAP` set. `build.sh` prefers `mappings/SB.usmap` (UE4SS `DumpUSMAP` from the game, full engine schema) over the header-generated one. Only that file decodes engine assets like `SkeletalMesh` and `PhysicsAsset`.

`assetkit/meshtool` (CUE4Parse, built with `assetkit/meshtool/build.sh`) reads meshes from retoc legacy output. `dump` writes bones, sections, positions and skin weights as JSON; `bones` writes only reference skeletons, used by the outfits step; `gltf` writes a `.glb` that imports into Blender with all 323 bones and weights. New meshes can't go back in without the UE editor to cook them. The generated mappings decode 172/197 of Eve's shows byte-exactly. The rest only miss engine structs (camera-shake oscillators, post-process settings) and still round-trip as raw exports.

## Legacy

`legacy/` holds the old 0.x UE4SS Lua mod and the native `PlayShow` log hook. It is superseded: it needed exe byte signatures and a polled text file, and it never worked reliably.

## UE4SS script

`ue4ss/StellarBladeGore` is the UE4SS script. It started as a probe for the ragdoll flinging (runtime fix + logger) and now also runs the gore and the active ragdoll. Install it with `./ue4ss/install.sh`, which sets up the Stellar Blade UE4SS build (pinned and checksummed), and add the Steam launch option `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="dwmapi=n,b"`.

The first probe run showed the impulse isn't the problem. Her pelvis barely travels, but every bone keeps 500–1400 cm/s and up to 9000 deg/s and picks up energy again after settling. That points to self-collision. The mesh runs the `Ragdoll` profile (object type `PhysicsBody`, blocks `PhysicsBody`), so the bodies of her outfit's physics asset collide with each other and with `SBHair` (PhysicsOnly, attached to the head).

On each ragdoll the script makes the mesh ignore `PhysicsBody` and turns `SBHair` collision off. Self-collision is off by default (`tune.selfCollision = false`): with it, an arm landing on her body set her spinning and folding at wild angles. Ignoring `PhysicsBody` didn't stop pairs inside her own physics asset, so the data mod disables those (`outfits.self_collision`); this switch still covers unpatched outfits. Muscle tone ends as soon as she lands (`tone.landOff`: pelvis fell faster than `fallSpeed`, then slowed under `landSpeed`). `"auto"` keeps it when the outfit's physics asset carries the patch (detected by the pelvis body's damping). It restores both on recovery, and re-attaches the mesh to the capsule if respawn left it detached.

The invisible model after respawn had two causes in SB's engine. `ASBCharacter::SetLockupSimulatePhysics(true)` copies each mesh's world transform into `LockupTransform` (an SB addition to `USkeletalMeshComponent`), and the mesh stays pinned there; the script clears that lock on recovery. The death ragdoll also sets a non-reflected counter on `USceneComponent` (+0x180) that stops the parent from moving the component. Only the mesh had it, so the capsule respawned and the body stayed at the death spot. Lua cannot reach it, so `sbslice.dll` clears it (formationPath `u<hex>` on the same cheat), on recovery and once after the respawn. The probe runs from Eve's `ReceiveTick`: this game gives UE4SS no `UEngine::Tick` hook, so `ExecuteInGameThread` callbacks can land on Blueprint worker threads, and one deadlocked the game. It logs bone motion to `Win64/ue4ss/Mods/StellarBladeGore/stellarbladegore.log`.

With `cameraFollow` on (off by default: it fought how the death plays out, and the game died a second after it moved a really dead Eve's capsule), during any ragdoll her capsule, which the camera rides, drifts after her pelvis instead of staying where she died: nothing within `cameraSlack` (40 cm), then `cameraRate` (0.06) of the rest each tick, held `cameraLift` (95 cm) over it, ignoring her bodies meanwhile so it never shoves them (top level of `tune.lua`: `cameraFollow`, `cameraSlack`, `cameraRate`, `cameraLift`). A recovery then gets her up where she lay.

Sandbox keys: F7 ragdolls her with a shove onto her back using `Scripts/tune.lua` (`push` on the whole body, `impulse` on the upper body, `legKick` sweeping her feet the other way, `direction` relative to her facing or the camera, `up`, self/hair collision, mass scale), F8 recovers, F6 re-reads `tune.lua` without a restart, F9 cuts a limb off, F10 puts a living hit's mark on her (see Hits she lives through), F11 splits her (Gore), Ctrl+Shift+F5 cuts along a made-up blade, Shift+F7 shows the death face on her living face for 3 s (see Death face), F5 spawns an enemy, Shift+F5 picks the next kind, Shift+F6 shows the spawner window, Ctrl+F6 kills the followed character nearest her (see Enemies), Shift+F8 turns the glass cannon mode on and off (see Glass cannon). (`SBSuicide` is a no-op in the shipping build, so there is no real death key.) Ctrl+F5 plays her animated death shows in turn (`P_Eve_Dead`, `Dead2`, `Dead3`, then `P_Eve_Dead` with each of the eight knockdown falls) through `SBBlueprintLibrary.PlaySBShowToPlayerCharacter` (`PlaySingleShow` is interface-only and can't be called from Lua): their ragdoll key does nothing while she is alive, so the script starts the death itself `f5Delay` (0.5) s into the fall, as an animated death, with blood and recovery but no death screen. With `f5Split` (the default) no show plays: the death starts on the next tick and splits her top to bottom at once (`F5: show ... via SBBlueprintLibrary: played` in the log). On first load the script also runs UE4SS `DumpUSMAP` once, giving the full engine schema needed to edit physics assets. Remove it with `./ue4ss/install.sh --uninstall`.
