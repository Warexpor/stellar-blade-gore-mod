# Stellar Blade Gore Mod: technical notes

How the mod works, how it is built and every tuning knob, for anyone working on it. The player's guide is the [README](../README.md).

A gore mod for Stellar Blade: when Eve dies she ragdolls, bleeds and can lose a limb. Three parts:

- **Data patch**, `ZZ_StellarBladeGore_P` (under 1 MB: death shows that ragdoll the player, the death table, her death face animation, voice), an IoStore pak built from your installed game and the other mods in it. The build also makes `ZZ_StellarBladeGore_Outfits_P` (under 1 MB: every outfit's physics asset with the ragdoll fixes), which no longer ships: `physfix.lua` and `sbslice.dll` make the same fixes in game on every outfit, any mod's too, and `test/run.sh phys` holds them to it value for value. It's installed only with `SB_OUTFITS=1`.
- **UE4SS script** (`StellarBladeGore`): [gore](#gore) (dismemberment through her own skinning, caps, blood), the [active ragdoll](#active-ragdoll) (masses, momentum, impact, muscle tone), [fixes for outfits the data patch missed](#other-mods), respawn fixes and a log.
- **`sbslice.dll`**: a small native helper the gore needs, loaded by UE4SS.

Formerly `sb-eve-ragdoll-death` / `EveRagdollDeath` / `EveRagdollProbe`; the installers remove the old names.

## How it works

Deaths are driven by `SBShowData` "shows". `Local/Data/CharacterDeadTable` row `Player` picks the show per death type. Most of them (stand, skill steps, explosions) are the shared `Dead/Dead_Monster_*` shows, which already contain an `SBShowRagdollKey` (channel 23 in `KeyContanerMap`). The player never ragdolls only because the key's constructor sets `bIgnorePlayer = true` (`mov byte [rbx+0x1F0], 1` in `USBShowRagdollKey::USBShowRagdollKey`), and no asset overrides it.

The patch (`mod/ragdoll_spec.json`):

- **clone**: copies the shared `Dead_Monster_EffectType_Stand / Explosion / ExplosionLight` and `SkillType_Step0-3` shows to `CH_P_EVE_01/Ragdoll/EveRD_*` with `bIgnorePlayer=false` and Eve-sized impulses (the enemy values of 5k–18k launched her across the map). The `Player` row of the death table points at the copies, so enemy deaths stay fully vanilla. Every `*DeadUIDelayTime` of that row is 0, as vanilla: the death screen is made at once and the script holds it hidden (its intro waits while hidden) till `reviveAt` or Shift+F4, as `deathScreen` says, so it fades in only after her fall and the gore have played out. (At `reviveAt` the game started the intro only then, and Shift+F4 earlier showed an empty screen.) The game respawns her only once a button on that screen is pressed, and the script gets her up just before: when one of its buttons fires (`WB_MainHUD_Dead_C`'s `WB_Common_btn_1_Event`/`_2_Event` (either spelling, with `_0` or without) or `FinishPressAnimation`, hooked as soon as the class is loaded, tried once a second in a death; `FinishYouDieAnimation` says the screen came up; `hook death screen: ...` and `death screen: <function>` in the log) or the screen goes away once seen up (any instance of the widget but its class default, shown when it and its `CanvasPanel_Dead` are visible: it lives inside the main HUD and is never in the viewport itself), never before `recoverAfter` (12 s), so her cut body stays behind the screen till then (`tune.recoverOn` "button"; "timer" gets her up at `recoverAfter`, under the screen, as before). A death that shows no screen by `recoverAfter` + `screenWait` (4) s (the mod's own deaths, F5's) gets her up then. With a revive item on her the game puts its death screen up the moment she dies, over her death: whichever screen is up before `reviveAt` (10) s into the death (`WB_MainHUD_Dead`, or `WB_MainHUD_ResurrectionItem` should that be the one) is held hidden and disabled till then, about when the death screen fades in without one, and she waits while it is up. Both are caught as they are made (`NotifyOnNewObject` on `UserWidget`, the class read; the death screen is made as the level loads, while her tick still waits 2 s on a new level, and it went stale before it was ever checked (`keepFresh`), so the hold never found it and the screen came up with `deathScreen` "key" (playtest): a death screen not held is looked for once a death by its class from 1 s in): a scan of every object for the death screen every 2 s while it wasn't found was 60 ms a time in a big level, and its class loads only at the death; one scan a death is left, from 5 s in. When the held screen goes away after it showed and no death screen is up 2.5 s later, she was revived and is got up at once (`revive screen: made / up / shown / gone` and `recovering ... (revived with the item ...)` in the log). Shift+F4 in a death holds it (`screen.frozen`): both screens are held hidden and disabled whenever up, their going away is not counted, and she isn't got up, till Shift+F4 again restores them. While it is held the game's photo mode may open (`tune.deathPhoto`): photo mode is native (its UI, `ASBPhotoModePawn`), entered from the settings menu or the `PhotomodeOneKey` / `PhotomodeTwoKey1+2` actions (P; D-pad left + R1), and turned down while she is dead. The game keeps named constants in a singleton (`exe+0x7032540`, filled with defaults at `0x25d3ad0`); its bools are a `TMap<u32, bool>` at `+0x378+0xf0`, keyed by `FCrc::Strihash_DEPRECATED` of the name (`0x117c180`). The cheat that overrides one (`SBConfigOverrideBool`) is a stub in the shipping build, as are `SBPhotoMode` and the other photo mode cheats (their exec thunks read the parameters and return), so `sbslice.dll`'s `f1` / `f0` write `SBPhotoMode_Bypass_ActivationConditionChecks` (default 0) itself, the key hashed with the game's own function and `SBPhotoMode_Trim_LetterBox` looked for beside it as a check that it is the table. It is set while the death is held and cleared when it is let go or she gets up. The bypass alone did nothing: the death also keeps the key from reaching photo mode's key handler (`0x142362d00`), and the check that decides (`0x141cd3b10`, asked by the `PhotoModeActive` event's handler `0x141abe100` after the key handlers' `0x142361aa0` queues the event) never reads it in the shipping build, and says no to her dead (her state at `+0x2600`, the open UI list `SBPhotoMode_CheckUITypeList`, ...). So P in a held death has `sbslice.dll` (`f2`) call what that handler calls once the check passes, `0x1422a98b0()`: the enter itself, which takes the photo mode manager (a singleton at `exe+0x7032598`, made at `0x141adc360`), checks `0x14236b800`, sets its state `+0x84` to 1 and registers its tick. It is only called while the manager is idle (`+0x38`, `+0x84` 0); the answer also logs the manager and the key path's gates (`f3` only reports). P in a death not held yet holds it first (`screen.hold`, Shift+F4's). Once in, photo mode sets every character mesh's collision to query only and its Pawn channel to overlap (`0x1423a33e0`, over the `MeshInfoList` slots: virtuals `+0x668` `SetCollisionEnabled(1)` and `+0x860` `SetCollisionResponseToChannel(2, 1)`), and puts them back on leaving: on her ragdoll that ended the physics, the mesh went back to its animated pose (a cut-off head onto her neck) and was flung from there as it closed. So `f2<mesh>` patches those two slots of her mesh's class vtable in place (once per class, at most 4) with stubs that drop her mesh's calls while the manager's `+0x84` is not 0 and pass every other call on to what the slots held; the patch stays (another mod may chain onto it), `f5` only stops naming her mesh (`blocked=N` in `f2` / `f3`'s answer counts the calls stubbed out). Her mesh used to be moved onto a copy of its vtable instead: UUEPBS (a body slider) patches `FinalizeBoneTransform` (slot 303) in the class vtable and finds the engine's function by the object's vtable, so on the copy it called nothing and her pose froze at the cut through every death (Nexus report). The game's own photo mode key works while she is alive, cut by `F9` or not dead yet, and it put an `F9`-cut head back on her neck the same way (playtest), so `screen.photo_guard` shields her mesh with `f4<mesh>` as soon as she has a cut (`f5` takes it off once none is left) and reads photo mode's state every 0.5 s while she has one: the watch below takes over however photo mode opened (`photo mode opened by the game's own key`). The shield never caught a call there (`blocked=0`) and the head still went back, 213 cm within 0.5 s, its body simulating: photo mode pauses the game but keeps her ticking, so her animation runs while the post-physics step that blends the simulated bodies over it doesn't (a whole ragdoll kept its pose). So while `IsGamePaused` with a piece on her and her pelvis not simulating, `screen.photo_freeze` turns her mesh's tick off, checked every frame from her `ReceiveTick` before the 33 ms throttle (her tick runs before her mesh's), and back on when the pause ends. Photo mode pauses the game but not the script, so while it is on (`screen.photo_watch`) the tick does nothing but read photo mode's state every 0.5 s (`f3`, its manager's `+0x84`) and her ragdoll's (`photo mode on: ...`, logged when it changes, with how far her head is from where it was before), undoing the game's lockup and pose snapshot whenever they show up. Once `+0x84` is 0 again the enemy's weapon trail starts over (its jump over the pause read as a swing) and hits after death wait `gore.photoGrace` s. Photo mode looks through its own pawn: the held camera's tick sees the view target turn into a `PhotoMode` class and stands aside, and once photo mode closes it makes itself the view target again. If the game respawns her while she is still down anyway (`NotifyBP_ReInitActor`), she is got up at once. Getting her up also clears the game's own ragdoll state (`RagdollInfo.EnableRagdollCount`, `CurrentActiveTime`, `BlendWeight`), which a real death leaves set until the respawn clears it: the revive in place never respawns her, so she stayed ragdolled to the game, stuck to the floor, and her next death's ragdoll key did nothing. Her movement, off (`MOVE_None`) since the death, is put back to walking if it still is 3 s after she got up with no respawn. The log's `recovering after ...` ends with which of these it was.
- **inject**: adds a ragdoll key, cloned from `Dead_Monster_SkillType_Step1`, to Eve's own death shows: `P_Eve_Dead`, `P_Eve_Dead2`, `P_Eve_Dead3`, `P_Eve_Dead_Nikke`, `Eve_Dead_Down`, `P_Eve_Dead_Falling`, `P_Eve_AirDead` and the laser trap deaths `P_Eve_Dead_Laser` / `P_EVE_Dead_Laser_BalanceBeam`. The laser shows had no ragdoll key (a 2 s additive twitch, shock decal and sparks), so she never simulated and the mod never saw the death; their key starts the ragdoll 0.35 s into the jolt with a soft 300 push, and the death cuts like any other. Each key is tuned per show (impulse, start time), and `bKeepPlaying` is set. Every Eve ragdoll key also gets `ImpulseLimitVelocity=700` and `CapsuleComponentFollowingSocketName=None`. The constructor defaults that to `Bip001-Pelvis`, which drags the player's still-colliding movement capsule onto the ragdoll every frame and flings her around spinning.
- **table** (in game since 0.4.0, below): `Player.AirborneDeadShow` points at the vanilla `CH_P_EVE_01/Basic/P_Eve_Dead_Air`, which does not exist, so airborne deaths had no show at all. It now points at `P_Eve_AirDead`.

The rest of the HUD goes for the death (`tune.hideHud`, `screen.hud`): 0.3 s in, the main HUD (`WB_MainHUD_Master_C`, reached from the death screen's widget by its outers, its widget tree then the master, else kept from the last death, else one `FindAllOf` a death) is walked from its widget tree's root (`Canvas_HUD`, `Canvas_Notice`, `Canvas_Post`, `Canvas_TPS` under `CanvasPanel_Root`): each child not on the way down to a widget whose name or class holds `Dead`, `Resurrection` or `Revive` is collapsed, its visibility noted, and put back as the death ends (`HUD: N widgets hidden for the death` / `shown again`). The death screen's own branch is gone into, not hidden, so it keeps to `deathScreen`. Not with F7's test ragdolls.

Since 0.4.0 the pak carries no data table. Whole copies of `CharacterDeadTable` and `EffectTable` overrode every other mod's (a moveset mod's nano suits gone, a shop mod's purchase crashing, Nexus reports), so `tables.lua` changes the rows in whatever tables the game loaded, every 3 s till both are done and again when a table object is remade: the `Player` row's eight death shows (sbslice `D`, an FString set in place), its `*DeadUIDelayTime` (`d`: 0 for `deathScreen` "game", else `reviveAt`), and `Down_Eve` (`A`: a deep copy of `Down` added to the row map, its FStrings and arrays in buffers of its own, the TSet's hash checked to be UE 4.26's FName hash (`fname_hash`: the entry's block and offset spread apart, plus the number; not the index plus the number, which no row matched in game), a free slot taken or the sparse array grown through GMalloc; then `d` sets its ID) with the three tumbling knockdowns' `ChainEffectAliasArray` re-pointed from `Down` to it (`N`). The heap fields to copy come from the row struct's reflection. The game resolves a chained effect through two maps of its own, built from the table at load: alias -> ID (`TMap<FName, int32>`) and ID -> alias (`TMap<int32, FName>`, hashed by the ID itself), elements of 20 bytes each. A row added to the table alone is in neither (a knockdown chained into it stood her straight up), so `tables.lua` finds them once a launch: `Y` scans private read-write memory for the knockdown row's name and `Yd` for its ID, the hits whose next value is the ID (or the name) are elements, `Mh` / `Mi` find the TSet header holding one (a qword pointing at or before it, a multiple of 20 back, followed by a plausible count, bits and power-of-two hash size, then every element checked to hash as UE 4.26's), and `M` adds `Down_Eve` to both after checking `Down`'s ID under its alias and its alias under its ID match the table's row (about a second in all, in the lobby). The scans (`&` + the command) run on a thread of the DLL's own so the game goes on meanwhile (`&?` asks for the answer); the game freeing memory under one (a level streaming in) crashed it, so an access violation on that thread is caught by a first-in-line vectored handler that resumes the thread at its `__builtin_setjmp` (`err memory freed`), and `tables.lua` runs the scan again 2 s later, up to 10 times. The readable-memory check's one-region cache (`stats.c` `readable`) belongs to the first thread that uses it; others always ask `VirtualQuery`. The log's `tables:` lines say what was done.

- **collision**: Eve's blueprint (`CH_P_EVE_01_Blueprint`) has two components on her own skeleton that block `PhysicsBody`: the sword box `WeaponPhysicsCollision` (on `Bip001-Prop1`) and the `SBHair` mesh. A ragdoll runs on the `Ragdoll` profile (object type `PhysicsBody`), so they shove her own bodies every frame. The patch appends an Ignore entry for `PhysicsBody` to their raw collision responses; nothing else in the blueprint changes. Outfit or hair mods that replace this blueprint will conflict.

- **hit bone**: vanilla keys leave `bOverrideHitBone` false, so every death pushes `ImpulseBoneName` (Spine1). Ragdoll key code (`0x14245eb70`) takes the bone from the hit info when the flag is set and falls back to `ImpulseBoneName` when the hit has none. Eve's keys set it, so a leg hit folds the leg and a head hit snaps the head. Explosions keep the centre push.
- **outfits**: every outfit mesh that `EquipmentTable` can put on Eve (SkeletalMesh on her body skeleton; hair excluded) gets its physics asset patched. `assetkit/outfits.py` finds them, and `build.sh` runs it before patching.
  - **physics**: vanilla physics assets let Pelvis collide with both thighs although their capsules overlap by ~10 cm at rest. The solver pushes them apart every frame, which was the flinging. Almost every other pair is disabled, so limbs pass through each other. The patch makes bodies collide across five groups (torso, each arm, each leg). Pairs inside a group never collide: some outfits ship with neighbouring torso bodies colliding, and CH_P_EVE_09 spun from it. Pairs closer than 0.5 cm in that outfit's bind pose stay disabled, for every body. The accessories (hair, cape, breast and hip jiggle, neck pieces: every body outside the five groups) collide with nothing of hers, only the world (`accessory_collision`, off): CH_P_EVE_09 still had 114 accessory pairs colliding with her main bodies, the hip and breast bodies inside her torso shoved the thighs and arms every frame and the cape and hair got pinned between her and the floor, so she shook on the ground (pelvis turning 150-300°/s while lying still). With `self_collision` off (the default) every pair of her bodies is disabled as well, the groups too: an arm or leg landing on her own body set every joint jittering until each part found the floor, and the runtime switch below didn't stop it. Limbs may pass through her. These pairs are measured per asset from the mesh's reference skeleton and the body capsules, and for `CH_P_EVE_30` the result matches the hand-checked list. `LinearDamping` drops from 5.0 to 0.2: at 5.0 she fell at about 2 m/s and looked floaty. `AngularDamping` drops from 10 to 1 (`body` in the spec; a body that has no such property gets one): she toppled and turned as if under water. Some outfits (25 physics assets, e.g. CH_P_EVE_09) give twist or helper bones a body, as the anchor of a cape or skirt chain, but no constraint to anything above it. In gameplay those bodies are kinematic, so it never shows; in a ragdoll they fly free and drag the skin weighted to them, which bent and stretched her arms. Each of them gets a locked joint to the nearest ancestor body, framed at the bind pose (`outfits.lock_orphans`, on by default). The main joints bend about their twist axis (PhAT framed them as hinges) within a range centred on a zero of the asset's own, as soft limits: knees went 19° past straight, elbows 24°, hips bent forward only 30° and back as far, the spine 5° a joint, and under a push the soft limits gave way further. Each joint gets the range of `outfits.ranges` (degrees its far end may swing towards her front and her back from the bind pose: knees 140 back and 3 forward, elbows 145 and 3, hips 110 forward and 20 back, shoulders 120 and 40, each spine joint 15 and 8, the neck 25 and 15). `outfits.py` measures each asset's zero at the bind pose and turns the joint's frame 2 about its twist axis to centre the range there. The shoulders and hips also get `inward` / `outward` (their sideways swing, Swing1: shoulders 25° in and 50° out, hips 15 and 45, from the bind pose; see the physfix paragraph below). `swing` overrides cone limits (none now: it spread the hips ±30°, which crossed her legs), and `hard_limits` turns every limit of the main joints hard (`bSoftConstraint` off). A main joint's motion limited to 0° (CH_P_EVE_09's pelvis-to-spine cone) is locked instead: a hard limit with no range shook it every frame. `locked_joints` locks joints whole (none: locked clavicles hung both arms rigidly off the light neck and the spine buzzed instead). Any motion limited under `lock_below` (6°): the 5° clavicle cones, shoulder roll, hip sideways swing, neck and pelvis-to-spine twist sat at their hard limits all the time and every joint buzzed, even in mid-air. These bodies are kinematic in gameplay, so only ragdolls change.
  - **cpuaccess** (off, `outfits.cpuaccess`): sets `bAllowCPUAccess` on LOD0 of every outfit mesh, for a game without `sbslice.dll`, which keeps every skeletal mesh's LOD0 CPU copy itself. Repacked copies of outfit mods' meshes (SBPR's) crashed the game's async loader on some outfit changes, so the meshes aren't shipped.

  With `cpuaccess` on, every patched mesh is a full copy in the outfits pak (all 134 make it about 560 MB compressed). An outfit mod's mesh is patched in its own version, see [Other mods](#other-mods). Use `outfits.only` / `outfits.skip` (mesh-name globs) in the spec to trim the list, or set `outfits.enabled` to false to drop all of it. Meshes and physics assets that don't round-trip byte-exactly through UAssetAPI are skipped and listed in the build log. `ClothingAssetCommon` exports stay raw bytes, because UAssetAPI loses cloth LOD data when it writes them back.

Scripted deaths are left vanilla: swimming, ocean, sliding, desert hole, grinder, laser and balance beam.

## Build / install

Needs `git`, `cargo`, `python3` and `curl`. The .NET 10 SDK is fetched into `.cache/` if it is missing. Point `SB_GAME` at another install if needed.

```bash
./build.sh --install
```

```bash
./build.sh --uninstall
```

`./build.sh --install` installs the core pak only; `SB_OUTFITS=1` adds the outfits pak (a dev reference: the in-game fix leaves an asset it patched alone).

### Other mods

The build reads every container in `Paks/~mods` (subfolders too) over the game's own, in the order the game mounts them (paths sorted case-insensitively, a later one wins), and patches what they ship in their version: a nude or outfit mod's mesh and physics asset, a face mod's face, a mod's `EquipmentTable` and the new meshes it points at. So an outfit mod's look stays and gets the cut and the ragdoll fixes. Packages taken from another mod are listed in the build log and `dist/sources.txt`. Rebuild (`./build.sh --install`) after adding or removing such a mod: the paks hold a copy of what they patched, so a removed mod's outfit would stay, and a new one would load unpatched. A mod that loads after ours (its file name sorts after `ZZ_StellarBladeGore_P`) wins in game over what both replace; the build names it, and renaming it to sort earlier fixes that. Mods that only replace textures or materials don't touch anything of ours. Encrypted containers can't be read and are skipped with a note. Mod containers of an older TOC layout (`DirectoryIndex`, as Eve with no Clothes on ships; the game's are `PartitionSize`) are read alongside the game's: `assetkit/patches/retoc-mixed-toc.patch` lets retoc mix layouts that share the chunk IDs and package format (everything up to `PerfectHash`). An outfit mod's mesh exported without a physics asset (Eve with no Clothes on's `CH_P_EVE_InnerSuit`) still gets CPU access (the build log names it), and in game `physfix.lua` gives it the outfit's own `<mesh>_Physics` (patched by the data mod), else `tune.physfix.fallback` (the base body's): without one she has no bodies and can't ragdoll at all. A given asset is the component's own and can be dropped again (gore's undo re-makes the bodies from the mesh's asset, which is none): `physfix` gives it again on the next tick she is animated (`lost the physics asset it was given`), and every script asks `physfix.asset` (component's, mesh's, else the given one) for her physics asset. Mods only ever change our input: the cache key covers their containers.

Without the outfits pak (the release has none) every outfit is caught by `physfix.lua` when she puts it on: the log says `physfix: outfit ..., physics ...: NOT patched, LOD0 CPU access false`. Without CPU access it may not be cut (gore logs the DLL's `no CPU copy`), and she just ragdolls; the flag isn't the whole story (InnerSuit read false and still cut). sbslice.dll now forces it at load (it patches `USkeletalMesh::NeedCPUData` to answer yes for LOD 0, `cpu copy LOD0 on` in the `sbslice.dll ready` log line), so meshes loaded after UE4SS starts keep their CPU copy without the data patch (confirmed in game: SBPR's outfits cut with no Outfits pak), and the log then says `LOD0 CPU access kept by sbslice.dll` instead of warning. Its physics gets what can be done from the script: the main bodies' damping (in the asset's defaults, bodies re-made while she is animated), hard joint limits, the torso's jiggle bodies (breasts and other leaf bodies under the pelvis, spine or neck) set to collide with nothing (`UBodySetup.CollisionReponse`: inside her chest they shoved her arms and torso every frame and she buzzed on the floor, as CH_P_EVE_30 did with no Outfits pak while CH_P_EVE_10, which has none, lay still), and a locked joint for each loose body (twist and cape anchors with no joint: CH_P_EVE_29's thigh twists carry her upper thighs' skin), the build's own: `physfix.add_joints` makes a `PhysicsConstraintTemplate` framed at the bind pose (`outfits.py`'s `orphan_joints`) and the DLL's `j<asset>,<joints>,<first joint>,<new>...` appends it to `ConstraintSetup`, found as the asset's TArray of that length whose first element is that joint (without the DLL, a locked constraint component while she is ragdolled). Self-collision stays off for such an asset. The rest of the data patch's physics is made in game too, so such an outfit ends up as the build would have patched it: the main joints' twist ranges are re-centred on her bind pose (`tune.physfix.ranges`, the spec's `outfits.ranges`; `sbslice.dll`'s `b<mesh>,<bone>,...` composes each bone's bind pose from the mesh's `FinalRefBonePose`, and `physfix.joint_ranges` is `outfits.py`'s `joint_limits` step for step), motions limited under `lockBelow` (6) degrees are locked, the hips' sideways swing is set (`swing`), and none of her bodies collide with each other (`collisionTable`). The collision table (`CollisionDisableTable`, a `TMap<FRigidBodyIndexPair, bool>` the physics scene reads by pointer when her bodies are made) isn't reflected, so the DLL's `t<asset>,<bodies>` finds it by its contents: the first TSet from the asset's start (the scan reaches past its end, where the next asset may keep a table of its own; later assets are looked at only at the offset found, `+208` in this build) whose every element is a pair of body indices sitting in the hash bucket the pair's hash puts it in, with every bucket's chain reaching exactly its elements. That proves the layout and the hash (`a + 23 b`) before anything is written; the new arrays (every pair) come from GMalloc and the old ones go back to it. Without the DLL the torso's jiggle bodies get "collides with nothing" instead (`quietJiggle`). `native/sbslice/test/run.sh phys` holds all of it to the build: `test_phys.c` checks both commands on made-up data, and `phys/parity.py` runs `physfix.lua`'s fix on every vanilla outfit's physics asset (made up from the build cache's JSON) and compares every value it leaves with the build's patched asset, the loose bodies' joints frame for frame (71 of 71). `tune.physfix` switches each part. Her arms collide with her body and with each other (`physfix.armCollision`): each death the DLL writes the collision table again (`t<asset>,<bodies>,<a>,<b>...`: every pair but the ones listed, `physfix.arm_table`), leaving out the pairs of her upper arms, forearms and hands against `Pelvis`, `Spine`, `Spine1` and `Spine2`, each arm's bodies against the other's, her forearms and hands against her thighs, and each leg's thigh, calf and foot against the other's. A pair whose bodies are within `armGap` (2) cm of each other as she goes limp stays in the table, since bodies that start overlapping fling apart; the gap is between the bodies' own shapes (each `AggGeom` sphyl, sphere and box as a capsule, placed at the bone's world transform), as `outfits.py`'s `body_capsules` measures them (the bind pose leaves out only each upper arm against `Spine2`). Measured between the bones with a 22 cm margin, the upper arms never collided and the forearms mostly didn't (playtest 43). The mesh then blocks its own `PhysicsBody` channel, switched off and on so the engine filters her body pairs again by the new table (log: `arms: N arm pairs collide: ...; M touching at the start, left out (...)`). A cut keeps it (the table keeps every other pair apart, a broken joint's too); a split keeps the pairs whose bodies are both on her half, the table written again with only those and the channel switched off and on (across the waist: her arms and chest, not her hips), else her bodies stop colliding with each other as before (`split: her arms ...`). Each cut adds a piece's main bodies (at and below the cut bone) against the rest of her main bodies (`physfix.piece_collision`, `pieceCollision`), by the same gap rule where she is cut (the piece's root and the body it came off stay apart), the DLL's table taking up to 256 colliding pairs (`PT_KEEP`); when she gets up (and when her cuts are undone) the table is written with no pair colliding (`collision pairs reset`), and a piece cut off her standing pairs with nothing (the rest of her is kinematic, moved by the animation: her thigh threw the hand hanging beside it off at 2500 cm/s); log `cut piece <bone>: ...`. Split halves don't collide with each other (the copy ignores `PhysicsBody`): each half keeps the other half's bodies, freed and invisible, right where the other half's real ones are, and a component's bodies can only be switched on or off together from the script. The shoulders' and hips' sideways swing (Swing1, about frame 2's Z, the engine's default ±45° round her A pose, where the arm hangs 30° out: an arm folded 15° into her chest) is set by the range too: `inward` 25 and `outward` 50 degrees from the bind pose for the shoulders, 15 and 45 for the hips (the hips' ±30° crossed her legs; the spec's `swing` override is gone), Swing1 their half sum, and frame 2, turned for the twist, turned again about its Z by the bind pose's swing plus half their difference (`physfix.joint_ranges` and `outfits.py`'s `joint_limits`, held equal by the parity test, `PriAxis2` too).

`ue4ss/install.sh` keeps other UE4SS mods: ours are turned on by their `enabled.txt`, and it only takes our old lines out of `mods.txt`. `--uninstall` removes this mod's script and DLL and leaves UE4SS; `--uninstall-all` removes UE4SS too.

The script and DLL: `./ue4ss/install.sh` (see [UE4SS script](#ue4ss-script)).

The build pins and fetches [retoc](https://github.com/trumank/retoc), [UAssetAPI](https://github.com/atenfyr/UAssetAPI) and the [SB header dump](https://github.com/a5tronomy/SB), generates `.usmap` mappings from the headers (`assetkit/mkusmap.py`), extracts the assets from the installed game, patches them (`assetkit/patch_shows.py`), repacks `dist/ZZ_StellarBladeGore_P` and `dist/ZZ_StellarBladeGore_Outfits_P` (`.utoc`, `.ucas`, `.pak`) (UE4.27 TOC layout, zlib blocks through `assetkit/patches/retoc-compress.patch`; `SB_COMPRESS=none` stores them raw) and reads the result back to verify it (`assetkit/verify.py`). The output lands in `SB/Content/Paks/~mods/`.

The source side (extracted packages, their JSON, the outfit scan) is cached in `.cache/work` under a key of the game's and the other mods' containers, the mappings, the tool sources and revisions and the spec's outfit selection, so later builds only patch, pack and verify; the first build after a game update extracts everything again. Outfit meshes never go through JSON: `sbtool meshinfo` reads what the outfit scan needs, `sbtool cpuaccess` sets the flag in place and `sbtool checkcpu` verifies that nothing else changed. sbtool's batch commands run `SB_JOBS` packages at a time (default: half the cores), and retoc compresses blocks in parallel.

Tuning: edit `mod/ragdoll_spec.json` (field names as in `USBShowRagdollKey`) and rebuild. `DirectionType`: 0 Static, 1 HitDirection, 2 TargetToSelf, 3 HitLocationToSelf, 4 Velocity, 5 StaticLocal.

Since 0.4.1 the script does nothing for its first 10 s after the lobby's player start (`screen.START_S`): her tick hook goes on at `ClientRestart` but returns at once, and the `NotifyOnNewObject` watches (widgets, the photo mode pawn, characters) are registered on the first tick after it (`screen.late`). A launch crash in the audio mixer's thread, as the lobby loaded, kept coming with the mod's sound loading put off.

## Gore

The game's own dismemberment (`ASBCharacter` slicing, `0x142129c30`, used by the player's sword on some monsters) can't work on Eve. It copies the cut region into procedural meshes and hides it on the original through a vertex-colour override plus the material parameter `DissolveMaskRadiusFactor`. Only the gore master `MA_CH_DamageGore` has that parameter, and cooked materials can't be given one. So the cut is made in the skinning instead, on her own mesh with her own materials.

`native/sbslice` is a tiny freestanding DLL (clang + lld, no MSVC; `native/sbslice/build.sh`). It loads as a UE4SS C++ mod and waits for `gore.lua` to send it the address of the unused cheat UFunction `SBRewardGroupDropFormationPathLocation`. It then swaps that function's native thunk for its own handler, which takes commands through the cheat's string parameter. The exe has no ASLR. Every engine address it uses (`resolve_addresses`, as it loads) is checked by its bytes at the Steam build's address first, and looked for by byte pattern across the code section (`.trace`) when they differ, the globals read out of the found functions (GMalloc, the photo manager and config getters, GUObjectArray from `FWeakObjectPtr::Get`); its answer says `addresses: steam`, `found` or which are missing, and with any core one missing it refuses. The thunk is matched by its address, else taken from the UFunction's `Func` (+0xd8) when that points into the code section. Its one code patch is `NeedCPUData` (every skeletal mesh keeps a LOD0 CPU copy), checked against the expected bytes first. `sbslice.rc` gives the DLL a version resource (version from main.lua, built by `llvm-rc`): a bare DLL with no CRT, resources or signature was deleted as a false positive by a few antivirus scanners' machine-learning verdicts.

How a cut works (`c<component>,<lod>,<bone>`, a plane point and a normal):

- **Split.** For a plane through bone B, the DLL reads LOD0's positions, indices and skin weights (CPU copies) and skins every vertex in the current pose. A vertex weighted to B's subtree and in front of the plane joins the limb and keeps only limb bones. Every other vertex keeps only body bones. A limb hanging free of the cut goes with B whichever side of the plane it hangs: a bone of B's subtree whose joint and its parent's both lie more than 3 cm in front of the plane, and everything under it (the arms on a waist cut). By position alone, a hand hanging down past the waist stayed on the legs, and her gloves and the suit's cuffs floated where she was cut.
- **Seam.** No vertex is shared across the cut any more. Every vertex of a triangle that crosses the plane goes 100 % to a sink on its own side: a helper bone near the joint that sits where the bind pose puts it relative to its parent, preferably a leaf with few vertices of its own, chosen per mesh section. The section has to contain the sink, because the engine drops weights on bones a section doesn't have. Before each cut `gore.lua` sends `n<component>,<bone>,...`, the bones that may never be a sink: the main skeleton (`Bip001-*`; the pelvis, a clavicle and the head had been picked, and a sink gives up its weights everywhere), hair, capes and breasts (they swing, and the seam swung with them) and accessory bones (`Ab_*`). For a cut through the spine it adds everything under the clavicles and thighs: they swing on their own, and the bind pose's arms hang at the waist, where a strap's seam had collapsed onto a triceps helper. For a limb or head cut it adds every limb but the one being cut: a thigh cut in a fall, her hand resting by the hip, took `Ab-L-Elbow` for the hip's seam, which then rode the flailing arm as a sheet from the hip to the elbow (`ELBOWHIP=1` in the harness puts the elbow on the hip joint and fails if any sink is on another limb). For a limb or head cut it also sends the bones with a physics body (after `fffe`), and the seam then follows the stump: a sink must ride the stump's own body or the one next to it (two bodies away scores ×64), a seam whose sink would ride another body borrows a still helper of the stump's body into its section, and the seam collapses to the middle of the opening, 2 cm inside, instead of the helper's origin (a thigh's seam on a chest helper 44 cm away was drawn up to it as a sheet). Inside means past the rim itself (never on the other side of the plane, and also for a rim further than `SINK_DEEP` in) where the rim lies further in than the plane: the cap sits on the rim, and on coarse limb skin (CH_P_EVE_01 to 05) the rim lies up to 5 cm in from the plane, so a point 2 cm in from the plane stood out past the cap with the seam drawn through it. Bones the watch saw moving stay out, and so do the helpers hanging under one, still as they are against it: `Ab-R-Side` hangs under `Dm-R-Side-Point`, an aim helper the animation points at the arm, and with the shoulder's seam on it, the seam chased the flying arm in an animated death, a sheet from her shoulder to the arm (CH_P_EVE_29; `DRIVEN=1` turns the shoulder's helpers with everything under them, and fails a sink under one). Moved bones stay out except as a last resort for a seam whose only other choice is two bodies away (`HIPMOVED=1` in the harness turns the hip's correctives), or one with no other choice at all: after a long watch had seen all of InnerSuit's helpers move, a waist cut left one side's seam on its old weights, stretched between the halves (`ALLMOVED=1`). A cut through the torso sends no bodies and keeps the sinks where they were. It sends the rig's `Dm-` drivers and every helper under one (before the main skeleton) after `fffd` instead: they count as moved, so a seam takes one only when its section has nothing else, after the helpers the watch saw move, and the last resort borrows one into the section if need be. On CH_P_EVE_30 a waist cut's lower seam had gone to `Ab-L-AX2`, under `Dm-L-AX2` on the pelvis, and that half's opening stretched 3.5 m after the flying upper body; with the hip's helpers moved the harness's seam went to `Dm-Spine-bland` and tore 123 triangles. Now it goes to `Ab-L-Venter` on the pelvis or the spine's AX3 family (`ARMSAFTER=1` cuts both upper arms off the flying torso after the waist, as that death did, and the waist runs fail a new sink under a driver). A split sends them too, and its point and band sinks try every band without a driven helper before one with (`side_sink`). After `fffc` it sends the drivers of the side whose limbs the half lets go (`gore.M.side_drivers`: the limb a driver follows is read off the name of the topmost `Dm-` bone over it, `-L-`/`-R-` with an arm word (Trape, Shoulder, Pectro, ...) or a leg word (Thigh, Hip, Knee, AX2, ...); not a breast's or a cloth's): the animation moves them after those limbs as they fall free, and the half's skin that hung partly on them (`Dm-R-Trape`'s `Ab-R-AX1` and its gear) stretched out after them as cones. The half's kept skin lets go of them as of the limbs (`limb[]`). The harness's split moves them with the dropped limbs and fails a kept vertex that follows (`OLDDRV=1`: not sent).
- **Only the body.** A section that doesn't go round the cut (the same test as the caps below: straps, plates, pads, a cape, a loose sleeve) gets no seam. Each of its connected pieces that crosses the cut goes along whole with the side holding at least 85 % of its weight; one tied to both goes whole with the side holding most of its vertices (a strap over the shoulder weighted 2:1 to the arm but lying on the chest stays on the chest), so nothing of it can stretch. It used to vanish whole into a sink, which read as the cut taking a piece of her outfit. Pieces over 4000 vertices or reaching more than 40 cm from the cut are cut like the body instead, unless at most 15 % of their vertices lie on the other side (CH_P_EVE_09's cape, 9 % of it past an arm cut, had a seam torn across it). A seam through them had collapsed onto whatever bone their section had, often far off or swinging, and stretched across the body. A piece reaching more than 6 cm past the plane on both sides, with over 15 % of its vertices and 3 % of its weight on each, is cut too: sent whole, a waist cut's belt straps stood up off her hips like horns and a harness piece reaching 16 cm down her hips went with the upper body. So is a piece with over 15 % of its weight on each side reaching more than 4.5 cm past the plane both ways, however few of its vertices lie over it, as long as those stay within 12 cm of the plane: CH_P_EVE_29's side panels under her arms (22-30 % of their weight on the upper body, 8-12 % of their vertices over a waist cut) went whole with the legs and stood up 6-8 cm off her hips, stretched and open, while the cape past an arm cut (its few vertices there hang 67 cm down the arm) still goes whole. And so is a piece whose few vertices past the plane reach more than 12 cm down the other side, most of them on that side's bones, unless it has weight on a chain the limb cut keeps on the body (the cape): CH_P_EVE_14's leg laces, 14 % of their vertices 62 cm down a cut thigh, went whole with her body and lost the leg's weights to `Bip001-Spine`, the knee's skin stretched from the severed leg to her. Such a seam takes the sink nearest itself, borrowed into its section's BoneMap when the section has none of that side (CH_P_EVE_09's harness knows only `Bip001-Spine` on the hips). The log shows `pieces=` (how many went whole).
- **Skirts on the thighs.** A thigh cut keeps every skirt chain with no physics body on her (`gore.skirt_chains`: the tops of the `*Skirt*` bones whose parent isn't one, sent in `g` with `0x8000` on the index, `KEEP_RIGID`). CH_P_EVE_37 (Black Rose) hangs its front and side panels (`Ab-*-SkirtD/O/E`) off the thighs' twist bones, animated only, so the leg took them along and the skirt stretched from her hips to it. Unlike a kept cape, their bones stay the limb's (they still follow it): only their skin stays on the body, and gives its weight on the limb up to the stub's carrier, held where it hangs at the cut. The chains on her pelvis and her other leg count too, for their skin partly on the cut thigh.
- **Collapse.** The sink's inverse bind matrix becomes a zero scale at its own origin, so the seam collapses onto the sink's live position whatever drives it (animation or physics). A stump's seam (a section that goes round the cut; a cape or a strap that crosses somewhere else keeps the sink it was given) is then held on the bone its rim rides, `bb` / `lb`: the weights stay on the helper, since collapsing the rim's bone would take the whole limb or the torso, and every frame while a piece is off (`H` from her tick, no result file — that file is what made a per-frame command stutter) the collapse point is rewritten so it stays at the opening in the rim bone's frame. That point is `SINK_IN` (2 cm) in from the middle of that side's rim, all its sections (a piece that doesn't go round the plane point: its own section's middle, on the plane when within `SINK_DEEP` of it). It used to be 2 cm in from the plane at the bone, but a thigh's rim lies up to 3.4 cm in from the plane and 6 cm off the bone, so the point sat in front of the cap and drew half the suit out over it. The harness fails a cut whose collapsed seam reaches within 1 cm of the body cap's centre (`bc=`). A thigh twist, the spine's `Ab-AX3T` or a knee driver keeps being animated after the cut, and the seam point used to swing off the opening as a cone, the cut face's black cavity. The harness shoves a leg stump's sink 30 cm and twists it, and fails if the seam moves over 1 cm; it then shoves the rim's bone and fails if the seam doesn't follow. Triangles across the cut become lines, and the ring next to them closes the opening. The sink's own vertices move to their strongest other bone first. Weight that leaves any bone (a sink, a helper the cut drops) goes to that bone's nearest ancestor that stays, else to the strongest other bone: a helper without a body rides its parent at a fixed offset, so the parent skins the vertex where the helper did. Moved to the strongest bone, a split's shoulder skin went onto the chest and tore off the clavicle by up to 23 cm once she moved. On a limb or waist cut, a body-side vertex whose bones all go with no kept ancestor in its section's BoneMap gets that ancestor borrowed in (`stuck=` counts what still has nowhere to go): left as it was, CH_P_EVE_14's jacket hem under a waist cut flew off with the upper body. `HideBone` can't do this: a hidden bone renders at its parent's bind origin and stops updating, which stretched the severed limb back to the cut.
- **Apply and report.** The weights go to the engine's own `USkinnedMeshComponent::SetSkinWeightOverride`. The DLL reports both openings, fitted to the rim vertices in their new pose: centre, axes and an ellipse in the cut plane. The cap belongs on the limb, not on what is strapped to it, so rim points are grouped by mesh section first. A section whose points go round the cut axis (11 of 16 directions) is a layer of the limb. The innermost layer and the ones sitting tight on it (skin, a body suit) get the cap; straps, plates, capes and loose sleeves are left out and close up in their own material. With no layer at all, every point counts. Of what is left, points further than 1.6× the median from the cut axis are dropped too. The log line shows the grouping (`bsec=section/points/directions/radius`, `bcore=` the kept sections, `l...` for the limb side). `bb=` and `lb=` name the bone each side's rim rides (the most weighted one over its rim vertices, sinks left out): the caps, stubs and bleeding ride it, not the cut bone's parent, so they stay on the stump's skin (a thigh's parent is `Bip001-Spine`, while its stump hangs on the pelvis helpers, and the stub slid to the edge of the stump as she moved).
- **Open.** The sinks are recorded but collapse only on `k`, a few frames later. Collapsing them under the old weights would pull every vertex those helpers moved onto their origins until the GPU has the new weights.

The command is `c<component>,<lod>,<bone>[,<mode>,<radius mm>[,<sink>,<remap>]]`. Besides the plain cut (mode 0) there are three modes (3, 5 and 6 were the old wounds: a hole, a dent and a rupture, removed with them; a hit she lives through now leaves a mark, see Hits she lives through). `m<component>,<lod>,<reach cm>` asks where a ray from LocX/Y/Z along DirX/Y/Z meets the skin, changing nothing (`hit=`, `n=`, `bone=`, `out=`):

- **Whole (1).** Every vertex follows B's subtree; no seam. Eve's face is its own mesh, weighted to the head and the neck, so a beheading used to move all of it onto the head (cut in Plane mode like the neck, 8300 of its vertices vanished into the stump and the head came off faceless). Whole, the head took the face's whole neck along, down to her collar, so it came off with a long neck. Now the face is cut like a limb (`tune.gore.faceCut`), at the head bone (`headAt` 1.0 of the way from the neck bone; it was 0.7). Its eyes, teeth and brows know only the head bone, so the head side's seam closes on `Bip001-Spine1`, a bone no face vertex uses, made to follow her head from the collapse on (the `<sink>,<remap>` fields: a borrowed sink on MODE_CUT's limb side, on MODE_SIDE's vanishing side). The face's neck stays on her, and her stump's cap goes on the face's rim, unless the body's goes all round her neck and is wider (a high collar). Most bodies end under the plane or just past it (CH_P_EVE_30's neck at 0.88, CH_P_EVE_29's collar round the back only). Her chin's underside is only 1-3 mm above a plane at the head bone, so a nod or a leaning blow ran the plane through it and left a slice of chin on the neck. `gore.lua`'s `clear_jaw` moves the plane down its normal until 22 points of her jaw and skull base (`JAW`, in the head bone's space: the lowest vertices only the head moves for any plane within 60 degrees of the neck) are `jawClear` (0.5 cm) above it; past `jawDrop` (3 cm) it leans the plane back toward the head's own axis first, along which 0.5 cm is enough. On the real face mesh in 768 poses (nod -30 to 40 degrees, roll 20, the plane leaned up to 35 degrees every way) no vertex only the head moves ends under it. The harness cuts the real face mesh (`HEAD=1 FACESINK=1`) at 0.5 (the lowest the clearance takes it), 0.7, 1.0 and 1.2 and flings the head off: nothing stretches, nothing of the head stays behind. Past about 1.2 the plane runs through her jaw.
- **Plane (2).** The limb side is what is in front of the plane and within the radius of its axis, whatever the weights. The head's subtree is the head alone (the clavicles hang off the neck) and the outfit skin has no head bone near the neck, so the neck is cut by position, mid-neck.
- **Side (4).** Half of a split, at any angle: what is in front of the plane stays, what is behind it goes. Kept skin on a bone the half gives up (a sink, a let-go limb's driver) goes to that bone's nearest kept ancestor, borrowed into the section's BoneMap when it isn't in it; before, a section with none of them in its map put it on the section's first kept bone by index, and CH_P_EVE_01's outer suit (14,659 vertices on 11 shoulder and hip helpers, no main bone) put 4,103 hip vertices on the dropped side's `Ab-L-AX1T-BKWING`, which then followed the flying arm. A let-go limb's drivers stay let go when the kept head's skin hangs on them too (that suit's collar is on the shoulders' AX1T helpers). It isn't collapsed into points: the first version did that with one sink per section, so the whole other half, legs included, went into one spot near the chest and every triangle along the midline stretched from the hips or the neck to it. Now the torso just behind the plane (5 cm) is flattened onto it, and what lies deeper collapses to a point on the plane in its band (flattened, a slice across the waist would spread the shoulders out past the opening). Its sinks get a projection instead of a zero scale: inverse bind matrix, then onto the plane (2 cm inside the kept side, behind the lids; at 0.3 cm the sheet came out in front of the lid wherever she bent) in the sink bone's frame as posed at the cut. Each vertex lands where it was, only pushed onto the plane, so the midline closes without anything reaching across. The sheet is banded the way the lid is cut into pieces: each vanishing vertex goes to the band of the spine bone it lands nearest to (Lua's `SPLIT_RIDE`, sent after the limbs; each half sends only the spine bones it keeps, `SPLIT_RIDE.of`: the copy's head breaks free in its ragdoll and spun at up to 8000 deg/s, and the lid and sheet that rode it rose from the body in tall dark sheets), and a band's sinks must ride that bone (the first main bone up their chain; under a clavicle or a breast they swing on their own). A bone no free helper rides (CH_P_EVE_09's Spine and Spine1) gets no band. A flattened vertex is weighted between the two bands of its section it lies between, by where it sits along the spine, so the sheet bends like skin. With four bands along the opening and whatever sink was nearest, a pelvis helper carried 43 cm of the sheet up to the ribs, clavicle helpers swung with the arms, and once she bent the sheet tore between bands and came out through the lid (the playtest's stretch probe: thousands of stretched triangles, all on the split's own sinks). A limb never splits lengthwise: Lua sends `g<component>,<gone roots>,0,<kept roots>,0,<ride bones>[,0,<dropped chains>]` (each arm and thigh by the joint it hangs from, shoulder or hip: by the middle of its first segment, an arm hanging past a slash across the stomach went with the legs and was cut off at the shoulder), the kept half's limbs stay whole wherever they lie, and the other half's limbs collapse to a point on the plane where they joined. Accessory chains off the torso or neck (the top of each chain of 2+ physics bodies outside `Bip001`, not under a limb or the head) go the same way, each to the side where its bodies lie on average: kept whole with the kept roots, or dropped whole (each vertex mostly on it collapsed to a point in its band). Halved, the neck chain's swinging half stretched to the flattened sheet across the opening. Kept skin lets go of every helper past the plane and on it (within 1 cm), so the other half's sinks never pull on it, and the halves no longer need re-cutting. Both halves are components of one mesh asset, so their sinks are shared: a vertex the vanilla skin puts wholly on a helper the other half has just made its sink is reweighted like any other (it used to count as an earlier cut's seam vertex and was flattened with the other half: CH_P_EVE_09's neck, on `Ab-NeckSub`). A section with no helper to flatten onto (CH_P_EVE_09's neck lace knows only the neck and the spine) borrows the half's nearest one into its BoneMap, like the face below. The opening runs head to crotch and isn't convex. The DLL traces it as one outline (`s0p=`, up to 200 points): the rim points and the mesh's own cross-section with the plane are drawn on a 0.8 cm grid, the outside is flooded from a corner, the inside filled and its largest piece's border chained; a fill much smaller than its outline's area means the outside leaked in through a gap, and the gap closing is doubled (1.5 cm up to 12). It is also doubled while the outline is under 40 % of the ellipse the rim's points spread over: through gaps wider than the closing the outside flooded round most of the torso on CH_P_EVE_06, 19, 28, 43 and 59, and the scrap left (180-380 cm² of a 1000 cm² opening) was taken as the opening. With none big enough, the biggest outline found is sent. Bands along it only pick the sinks now. `h<component>,<mode>,<head bone>` (point and normal: the neck plane) goes before a half's split cut: Eve's hair (`CH_P_EVE_Hair02`: a root and its hair chains, no body bone) can't be halved, so the head never is. Lua puts the neck plane at the neck's base. Mode 1 keeps everything above it whole within `NECK_BASE_R` (7 cm) of the neck axis up to halfway to the head bone, and `NECK_R` (10 cm, her collar) above, and closes the strip under it on the far side into the neck (halfway up the neck, a split leaned 2 cm beside it took the far side's lower neck; 10 cm wide at the base, it kept the far trapezius, which stretched to where the dropped arm collapsed); mode 2 lets the head go into a sink at the neck and sends a second opening, `n` (`np=`). Its kept skin keeps nothing on the head's subtree: that weight goes to the head's parent, the neck, as a gone limb's does (557 of CH_P_EVE_10's neck vertices hung partly on the head, Neck 204 / Head 51, and stretched after it). No sink may be a bone the bone watch saw move while she played (an animation-driven corrective such as the `Ab-AX3` family would carry the flattened sheet with it), nor one the head's or the neck's skin hangs on (sinks are the mesh asset's, so the other half's head would let go of it), and such a bone is never one the kept skin drops, wherever it lies (`Ab-NeckSub` swings past the plane in a pose). The answer adds `proj=` and `point=`. `<sink>` forces a sink bone for sections that have nothing to flatten onto: Eve's eyes, teeth and brows only know the head bone, so the face borrows `Bip001-Spine1`, which no face vertex uses. The DLL adds it to the section's BoneMap in place when the array has room (an engine array always has room for 8 entries); a fuller one is swapped for a copy with room for 512, put back on undo. Borrowing only in place had left a waist split's copy with 10617 vertices of the upper body standing on the legs, its sections full and, on `k`, points the component's `MasterBoneMap` entry for it at the master's bone `<remap>` (the head), so the face's vanished side flattens in the head's frame. An F11 / F5 / odds split leans its plane from the crotch to `tune.gore.splitBeside` (2) cm beside her neck, so the head stays whole on her half and the cut runs between her breasts (blade splits follow the blade). Lua sorts a split's arms by the shoulder joint (`SPLIT_LIMBS`), not the clavicle by the neck. A dropped limb's point rides the spine bone nearest where it joined, else any spine bone (never flattened onto the plane: a flat hand).

`p<src>,<dst>` copies one component's pose onto another on the same skeleton, for the split's copy.

`y<src>,<lod>,<dst>,...` puts src's weights (its override as the GPU skins it, else the mesh's) on other components of the same mesh asset: another mod's see-through copy of her mesh, and a split's copy of a body cut before. Each destination becomes a co-owner (`also`) of src's sinks, so its own cuts leave the seams on them: cut from the mesh's own weights, the copy of a body whose arm was off put the stump's seam back on the upper arm and clavicle, stretched out to the flung arm. A destination whose MasterBoneMap entry matches one src borrowed (the face's seam bone, made to follow her body's head) gets the same entry, applied with src's on `k` and given back when its weights are cleared: Ultra Violence's face blood copy follows her body, the face's own master, and its neck-to-head seam stayed on the borrowed bone's own transform. `gore.copies_find` takes copies on the cut mesh's pose or on its master's. `S<comp>,<lod>` saves a component's weights (its override, or none) and the sinks and borrowed BoneMap entries there are; `R<comp>,<lod>[,<other>...]` puts them back after a split that failed on a body cut before: the weights as saved (or the override released), the sinks and BoneMap entries made since for it and the others named (the copy) dropped, a collapsed one's matrix put back first, and the co-owners as they were (`ok back, dropped N`). `x` there would have taken the arm cut with the split (the sinks are the asset's: an `x` on the copy restores them all). `S<comp>,<lod>,1` forgets the save once the split held; `r` forgets them all. The harness (`ARMSPLIT=L|R`, in the `split` group) cuts that upper arm (`ARMAT` along it, 0.4), flings it, saves her, copies her weights to the copy, splits beside the neck and checks both halves as usual (the arm cut's sunk vertices are the baseline), then collapses, restores and checks her weights, her sinks, the co-owners and every other inverse bind are as before the split.

`s<component>,<lod>` is the stretch probe: it skins every vertex exactly as the GPU does (the component's pose, its overrides) and answers `ok stretched=<n> of <triangles> hidden=<n> longest=<cm> bones=<bone>:<count>:<cm>;... shown=<bone>:<count>;...`, the triangles with an edge more than 3x and 15 cm longer than at bind (`hidden`: those wholly on sinks with under 1 cm² left; a split's flattening sinks only squash the skin onto the plane, so a sheet stretched between two bands still shows) and the bones they hang on most (`shown`: counting only the triangles that show). `gore.lua` logs it with bone names (`split stretch, her half / copy` at 0.5 and 2 s after a split; with `probes`, `cut stretch, <bone>` at 1 and 5 s after a limb cut, and for a beheading the face's, `cut stretch, Bip001-Head (face)`). A component on a master pose is skinned through its own `MasterBoneMap`, so the face's borrowed `Spine1`, remapped onto her head, is skinned where the head is. With a third field, `s<component>,<lod>,<n>`, it also writes the skin as posed to `probe<n>.obj` next to the log (component space, cm, `vb <bone>` after each vertex: its strongest bone): with `probes` on, a split's halves at 2 s (`probe1` her half, `probe2` the copy, whose attached caps and stubs are logged with where they are and their scale) and a cut at 5 s (`probe3`), to see what a half shows in game.

In every mode, a limb-side vertex in a section that can't follow the limb, and every limb-side vertex of a section that has seam vertices on that side but no limb sink, vanishes: it joins the body side's seam and collapses with it, which beats a triangle stretched from the flying piece to the body. The log shows `vanished=`.

`x<component>,<lod>` releases the override, restores that mesh's matrices and the component's borrowed `MasterBoneMap` entries. `r` restores every collapsed matrix and borrowed BoneMap entry, since they live on the shared mesh asset. A BoneMap that had no room for a borrowed bone gets a bigger array from the engine's own allocator (`GMalloc`), so the engine can free it when the mesh goes; one from `VirtualAlloc` corrupted its heap and the game crashed on quit (Alt+F4: `MallocBinned2 Corruption Canary`). **Cutting along the blade.** The skill-hit message never came in a single-player session (0 calls), so the killing blow comes from three places, best first: the game's skill hit (within `hitWindow`), the last hit reaction on her mesh (`PhysicalHitForce*` and `VertexShakeInfo`, within `meshHitWindow`, logged as `mesh hit:`), or the nearest other character within `attackerReach` (800 cm). Without a swing, one is made up across the push from there: level, slanted or down, through where the mesh says she was hit or else a part of her picked with odds (`swing_at` in `main.lua`): mostly the chest and stomach, then the neck and arms, a leg about 1 in 6, a limb struck 40 % of the way down it. An even pick of 12 bones, a third of them legs and struck at the joint, had put nearly every death through a leg. `death: blow from ... (made up: <bone>)` names it. A made-up blow comes straight down when her mesh's hit points down or the nearest enemy drops onto her faster than `stompSpeed` (a stomp), else at a random angle. A blow from above (`splitDown`) never cuts her across the waist: a rolled torso cut becomes a split along it, and along a real blade the hip, knee, `bodyBias` and one-side rules split her through her spine along the blade instead, and a blade down her length always splits her. A made-up blow only aims the death: what comes off flies with its push, and it says whether the blow came from above, but the death rolls its odds (`death: no blade seen (blow from ...), rolling the odds`). Cut along, its guessed plane went through her spine most of the time, and 4 of 6 deaths in a playtest took her body, none a leg. ## Active ragdoll

**Animated deaths (experimental, `active.animated`).** Stand and light-hit deaths play her own fall instead of a ragdoll. The death table points `StandDeadShow`, `Step0DeadShow` and `Step1DeadShow` at `P_Eve_Dead`, `P_Eve_Dead2` and `P_Eve_Dead3` (they pointed at the animation-less ragdoll clones), and the ragdoll key in those, `P_Eve_Dead_Nikke` and `Eve_Dead_Down` carries `ImpulsePower 1` and `ImpulseLimitVelocity 701`. It holds its end pose (`bHoldEndTime`), so nothing jiggles on the floor. Cuts happen on the animated body as on a standing Eve (F9): the piece simulates on its own. A waist cut or a split switches to the ragdoll before cutting (`animated death: ragdoll after all`). Heavy hits (`Step2`, `Step3`), explosions, airborne and ledge deaths stay ragdolls. Only death shows carry a ragdoll key, so a knockdown (`Result_Knock_Down*`, `Down_BS`, `Groggy_*`, getups) never counts as a death. The log says `death: animated fall, physics off` or `death: ragdoll show`. More falls: the data mod builds eight death shows from her knockdown falls (`Ragdoll/EveDeath_*`: `Result_Knock_Down_Start`, `_Start1`, `_Forward`, `_Backward`, `Result_State_KnockDown_S_Fw`, `_S_Bw`, `_Tumble_Fw`, `_Tumble_Bw`), each a copy of `P_Eve_Dead` with the fall swapped in (`animshow` in `patch_shows.py`), held on its last frame for 120 s (`hold`: ending 0.9 s after the fall let the game's dead pose, a different one, snap in; recovery stops it with `StopAllShow`), `Eve_Dead_Common` (death face and voice) kept and no ragdoll key. The knockdown shows and animations themselves are untouched. above 0, `1 - exp(-dt/response)` of it), capped at `accel` x strength x dt, by turns added to its angular velocity (`SetPhysicsAngularVelocityInDegrees` adding: `AddAngularImpulseInDegrees` as a velocity change did nothing in game) with its centre of mass moved round the joint by the same turn (`w x (com - joint)` added to its linear velocity: turned about its middle alone, the joint dragged it back and nothing was kept), the bodies below it down the limb turned with it about the same joint (`DOWN`, up to a cut: the forearm and hand left standing held the upper arm still), and minus its mass share (at most 0.6) on the parent alone, so she moves herself. A velocity servo, not a spring: a speed asked for can't run away, and contacts and joint limits still win. `roll` steers the torso's four bodies' turn about her length the same way (`roll.gain`, `roll.speed`, `roll.accel`). Pulses (`pulse`, a sine of speed over its time, a tremor riding it, each step steering the bone's turn against its parent to that speed, at most `accel`; the breathing alone adds each step's change, the rise's servo on the same spine owning the rest) carry the breathing (Spine2 against Spine1 about her right axis, `breath.depth` deg at full strength down to a third, every `breath.fast` to `breath.slow` s), the stump reflexes (the stub, the bone a cut root hung from, 1-3 jerks of `reflex.speed` across it with a 10-14 Hz `reflex.quiver`, every 0.4-1.4 s over strength) and the dying tremors. Each act's log line ends with how her joints followed: the angle off on average, and the share of the turn a step set that is still there the next (little kept: something else holds the joint; much kept and still short: too weak). Steered at most `rate` (60) times a second, each step reading each bone's place and turn once (`seen`, `spun`). Every step (an alone plan: only during a try) it asks active.lua to keep her awake (`twitch`) and wakes all bodies every 0.5 s; she settles again after. convulse.lua leaves her limbs alone meanwhile (`busy`: no arm swings or body events) and its neck struggle waits through a try (`rising`). Paused or in photo mode nothing moves. The log has the plan as it starts, an act a line and its result (`log`), `dying`, `her last breath`, and `over after ...` (the acts, the roll's range, reflexes, leg draws, chest height, how far she moved), or `none: ...`.

Recovery removes the component, puts the masses back and the weapons on their sockets before gore's cleanup recreates the bodies.

Everything the script does runs from her Blueprint's `ReceiveTick` (game thread). That hook is put on once, from the player controller's `ClientRestart` (game thread, as the level's player starts); it used to be put on from an async loop (`FindFirstOf` over every object, then `RegisterHook`, off the game thread while the save loaded), and now and then a launch crashed a second or two after it (null + 0x88). The loop is left only for a script reloaded mid-game, from 20 s after load, with a name lookup alone.

A death reloads the level, and the old Eve is freed. The script used to keep her pawn and ask UE4SS whether it was still valid, which reads the freed object: the game crashed on the new Eve's first tick. It now follows the actor whose `ReceiveTick` runs it; a new one is taken once the old one has been silent for a second, and everything held from the old one is dropped without being touched (`new Eve ...` in the log).

An object held past a tick (the characters seen, the enemies the swing tracking follows and their weapons, photo mode's widgets, the knockdown checks, an enemy's own objects) is kept as its address (`goreCtx.keep`) and checked before it is touched (`goreCtx.held`): `IsValid` on a freed object reads it and crashed UE4SS. 0.3.0 checked it by its path in the name table, but UE4SS's `StaticFindObject` finds no level actor by its path (`.../F02.F02:PersistentLevel.Name`), so no enemy was ever held and every death rolled its cut; a lookup by name under the level crashed the game at launch. `sbslice.dll`'s `G<object>,<index>,...` answers `ok` and a 1 or 0 for each: whether its item in `GUObjectArray` (found by index, chunks of 64Ki items of 0x18 bytes) still points at it and isn't flagged Unreachable or PendingKill, never reading the object. Every held object asked in the last 3 s goes in one call on the tick's first check (`goreCtx.frame`), the answers kept for the tick. The index is read off the object (`ffffffff` asks for it, answered `<bit>:<index>`) at its first check, within `keepFresh` (2 s) of `keep()`, which runs in NotifyOnNewObject's callback too (characters as they spawn, photo mode's widgets); a character first checked later (a level loading, no tick) is dropped and the characters are found again with `FindAllOf` (at most every 5 s). Nothing is held before `sbslice.dll` is up. `test/run.sh alive` checks `G` on a made-up object array. The first misses are logged (`held: <address> gone`).

## Her voice

The moment she dies (a real death or a test key's ragdoll) she cries out once: one of Eve's own death cries, `vo_eve_die_01`-`05` (the SoundWaves the game's cues pick from, under `Sound/Dialogue/ActionVoice/Eve`, localized like the rest of her voice), at her head with her action voice's attenuation, pitch 0.97-1.03. Nothing follows it: the screams on later cuts, moans and last breath of the first version played on over her body and are gone. A recording rests `rest` (8) s before it plays again. `tune.gore.voice`: `enabled` (false: only the game's own death voice), `volume`, `sounds` (the recordings to pick from instead: names under that folder, or full object paths of any SoundWave or SoundCue, so another mod's new recordings can be used; a mod that replaces `vo_eve_die_*` in place, as Violent Death Mod does, is heard with no setting); the log line is `voice: <recording> at <volume>`.

## Hits she lives through

A hit she lives through leaves a slash of blood on her skin, no cut (`tune.gore.hitMarks`; parked for now: `enabled` is false and the switch is commented out of `settings.default.lua`, so none of this runs, F10 included). The hit her mesh reports (`mesh hit` in the log) is marked `delay` (0.25) s later if she is still alive, so a killing blow's hit gets the death cut alone, and with `needDamage` only when her HP bar (the HUD's `ProgressBar_HP`, see Glass cannon) dropped, so a guarded or parried hit leaves nothing. The physics only says where its capsule was hit, so the DLL finds the skin (sbslice `m`): a ray from the axis of the part nearest the hit (neck, spine, upper arms, forearms, thighs, calves) out through the hit point, the first skin it leaves through within 30 cm (not an arm beside her body further out), then the outermost layer within 3 cm of that (a strap or a plate), and the skin's normal there. The mark is a deferred decal (the Raven cinematic's floor blood, `MI_FloorBloodDecal_5`, in an instance of its own shaped by the game's sword scar `T_A_SwordScars_01` with no normal relief, `slash`: the puddle's own lumpy shape and normals read as a dent) attached to the bone that weighs most on that skin, so it moves with her. It projects into the skin along that normal and is stretched `length` (14 cm) by `width` (4, the streak fills about a third of it) along the swing: the nearest enemy's fastest weapon bone's motion over the last 0.3 s, else the hit's push. It's 75-125 % of that size at random, its length kept within 1.3 times the part's radius so it stays on a forearm's curve, and its box reaches only `depth` (0.5) of the part's radius into her, so it never shows on the far side. At most `max` (12) at once, the oldest goes first; `blood` adds the hit's blood burst on it. Marks heal: each remembers her HP bar before its hit, and once healing has lifted the bar from its lowest since then back to that reading, the mark fades over `healFade` (2.5) s and goes (a mark from before the HUD's bar was found waits for a full bar). All of them go when she recovers or respawns. F10 puts a mark on a part at random, on its side facing the camera. The log says `hit mark on <part> (rides <bone>): L x W cm at ..., N cm out of the bone, N cm off the reported hit`, `hit mark healed: ...` and `hit mark skipped: <why>`. `test/run.sh mark` checks the skin query on CH_P_EVE_09, 10 and 30, plain and posed: rays four ways out of the torso, an upper arm and a thigh must meet the skin 3-20 cm out, on a vertex that part carries, with the normal along the ray.

## Hits after death

An enemy still swinging at her body cuts it again (`tune.gore.afterHits`). During the death the script keeps following the nearest enemy's weapon bones (the killing swing's tracker) and checks the step its fastest bone made each tick against her parts (head, neck, spine, arms and legs). A step faster than `swingMinSpeed` passing within `reach` (35) cm of one is a blow there: `cutChance` (0.75) of them cut along the plane the weapon swept, the same way a killing blow does (a limb, the head, or, while she is still whole, her body in two), the rest leave a hit mark where they passed (see Hits she lives through), all with blood. A blow that would split a body already cut takes the arms and legs its plane crosses near the hit instead (nearest first, the rest queued), else her body along the blade while it is whole, else a mark. One hit per swing (the weapon has to slow down first), at most `max` (6) a death, none in the first `from` (0.6) s, so the killing swing doesn't land twice. A split body only bleeds. The log line is `after death (N s in): <enemy>'s <bone> at N cm/s passed N cm from her <part>, hit i of n`.

## Death face

Eve's death face is [Proper Death Expression](https://www.nexusmods.com/stellarblade/mods/2716) by **blabmice**, used with credit as its permissions allow (details and the file's origin in `third_party/proper-death-expression/README.md`). The vanilla face is a blank stare.

- `vendor` job: the author's `EVE_Facial_ActionVoice_DIE1` facial animation (12 s) ships unchanged in place of the vanilla one, which the death voices `DIE1` / `DIE2` already play.
- `voice` job: `CSS_PC_EVE` gets `DIE3`, the same face with a breath cue at 0.0001 volume, as the author's fixes do.
- `notify` jobs: the grapple, explosive and falling death animations play no death voice, so each gets a new `SBAnimNotify_CharSESound` for `DIE3` at the author's time. The author's own fix reused an existing notify in each, which silenced a cloth or footstep sound; here every vanilla notify stays.

`verify.py` checks the vendored package is unchanged, the new voice copies `DIE1` except for its sound, volume and start, and each animation has exactly one new notify with every vanilla one kept. Don't install the original mod alongside this one; it replaces the same files.

In the mod's deaths the face never stayed: `DIE1` starts as she dies (her eyelids were closing 0.2 s in), but by 1 s every curve was back at 0 and she stared with open eyes. The mod holds a dead face itself (`tune.gore.deathFace`): from `at` (0.3) s into a death it sets morph targets on her face mesh, fading them in over `fade` (0.5) s and setting them again every tick until she gets up (a master-posed face adds its own morph targets over the ones it takes from her body's curves). `morphs` lists each as its JALI name, its ARKit twin and a weight; the first name her face mesh has is used, so the eyes never close twice. An entry may have `from` and `over`: its weight as the face comes on, eased (smoothstep) to its weight over `over` s. `morphs` holds what every death wears (her eyes half open, closing over 7 s; her pupils widening); one of `moods` is added per death, picked by `weight` and never the same twice running: `agony` (brows knit, a squint, nose wrinkled, lips stretched, slackening over 4-6 s), `grit` (teeth bared and clenched, letting go late), `shock` (eyes and mouth wide, brows up), `sorrow` (brows up in the middle, mouth down, chin up) `fading` (the pain nearly gone, eyes closing in 4 s) and `dread` (one eye half shut, the other staring, brows up unevenly, the mouth twisted to one side, the jaw off to it; `mirror` turns it to the other side half the time, swapping `_L`/`_R`, `_left`/`_right` and `Left`/`Right`, and leaves out `jawSide`); a mood's entry replaces the base one for the same morph. Every weight, start weight and time is scaled by up to `jitter` (0.25) either way, each side on its own; the eyes end `lidsShut` (0.7-1) shut, drift up, down or aside by up to `gaze` (0.35), and the jaw hangs to one side by up to `jawSide` (0.25), picked per death. The convulsions' poses are added over it, morph targets it doesn't hold included (set while a pose moves them, then 0). The log names the mood and what it holds (`face (death): grit, holding N of her M morph targets`), or lists the face's morph names when none matched. Shift+F7 shows the moods in turn on her living face, 6 s each.

## Enemies

The generic humanoid enemies get the same cuts as Eve (`enemies.lua`, `tune.enemies`): the skulls (sword, spear, hammer, gunner), the lab mutant, the three statues and the two lurkers (`kinds`, each with its `CharacterTableID` and body mesh; hunchbacks have no Bip001 spine and VipGuard's head helpers are hair only, so they're left out), and the Airborne Squad, the angels of the endings (`N_AirborneSquad`, 30030). Every live character of those classes is adopted as it shows up, the game's own slicer switched off on it (`nativeSlice` false). When one dies within `reach` (25 m) of her, a blade cuts it (`cutChance`): her swing (`bladeChance` of the time; the arc of her right hand's fastest stretch over the `swingWindow` s up to its last hit, else before its ragdoll started, at least `swingMinSpeed` cm/s, else that stretch and her forward; 1.5 s of her hand are kept, since the ragdoll starts a while after the blow) through where its mesh was hit in the last `hitWindow` s, or through the part nearest the plane; with no swing, a level, slanted or downward blade from her side through a hit or random part. The hit is moved on with the body since (its pelvis then and now), and a hit more than 30 cm from every part (the game reported one above a skull's head, and the plane missed it) is moved onto the nearest. Gore then cuts along that blade as on her (`gore.blow`, why `enemy`): a limb, the head, or the body along the blade, then every other limb it crosses (`multiCut`); a blade at the hips cuts the waist square; one down its length takes the limbs it crosses (never a split), else rolls `odds` (limb, head, torso). No face, no death show. Gore busy with another cut retries for `killWait` s, the blade moved on with the corpse meanwhile, falling back limb, head, torso near the hit. Her swings through a lying corpse cut it again (`corpseHits`: a part within `reach` cm of her swing's plane and `blade` cm of her hand, her sword's length; up to `max` per corpse, `cutChance`, else a hit mark there), and a corpse that leaves (destroyed, pooled and hidden, or gone from the level) takes its pieces, caps, bleeds and decals with it. The game dissolves a corpse some 15 s before it hides it, its despawn show swapping the materials for `MI_DeadMaterial_*`: once a slot shows one, its caps, bone stubs, bleeds and drips go (`dissolving` in the log; they hung in the air where it had been), and it is cut no more. `maxCorpses` cut corpses at once.

A dying enemy's throw (`enemies.pushScale`, `push`, `pushUp`) is followed whether the cuts are on or not: `push` cm/s along the blow (its mesh's last hit force, else away from her) on every body under `Bip001` as a velocity change when its ragdoll is first seen; `pushScale` reads its pelvis's speed 0.06 s later (the death show's ragdoll key fires 0.01 s in) and adds it again times the scale less one. Every vanilla monster death show has a ragdoll key (`Dead_Monster_StandCommon`, `EffectType_*`, `SkillType_*`), so the game ragdolls every death of theirs itself.

`smoothAnim` sets `bEnableUpdateRateOptimizations` off on every live character's mesh (`enemies.lua`'s `smooth_tick`, once a second, each character once, given back when switched off).

Keys (dev only, `settings.lua`): F5 spawns the picked kind `spawnDistance` cm in front of her, facing her (through `SBNetworkPlayerController:ServerRequest_CreateActor`, which runs locally in single player), Shift+F5 picks the next kind, Shift+F6 shows or hides the spawner window. The window (`native/sbslice/spawner.c`, in `sbslice.dll`, its own thread) lists the kinds from `spawner.list` (written by the script) and has Spawn, Spawn 3, Remove all and Kill nearest; it asks the script through `spawner.req`, so all spawning happens on the game thread. Closing it only hides it. While it shows (`spawner.state`, written by the window), the script gives it the mouse: the player controller shows the cursor and takes game and UI input unlocked (`WidgetBlueprintLibrary.SetInputMode_GameAndUIEx`), and goes back to game-only input when it hides (`spawner window open: mouse freed` in the log). The game had kept the cursor captured, and nothing in the window could be clicked. Kill nearest (also Ctrl+F6) ragdolls the followed character nearest her, which the script then takes for its death and cuts.

The Airborne Squad are built like Eve: `CH_P_EVE_01_Body` on her skeleton and physics, a face mesh of their own (`CH_P_EVE_Face_003`) and her hair. They are cut with her rules (`face = true` on the kind): a head cut moves the face whole onto the head, as on her, and their `Ab_` helpers aren't sinks. They are passive NPCs (no behaviour tree, a tribe her blows don't hurt), so they die only by Kill nearest, and they are never taken for her killer (`gore.attackerIgnore`). Their body is the same mesh asset as her first outfit: while a cut angel lies around, Eve in that outfit may pinch near the helpers its cut used. The game has no hostile angels besides the bosses Tachy and Raven; the Elder's angels in the final fight are his skill's own meshes, not characters.

Enemies of a kind share one mesh asset, and a cut collapses inverse bind matrices on that asset (its sinks). So a cut corpse releases its own override with `x<comp>,<lod>,1` (its weights only, the asset's sinks kept), and the asset's sinks are restored (`r<mesh asset>`) once no cut corpse of it is left; the full `r` (which also gives back borrowed BoneMaps) only runs with no cuts at all. Live enemies of a kind may pinch near the helpers a cut used while one of its cut corpses lies around. An enemy's `Ab_` bones may be sinks (they are its only deform helpers; its head vanished without them), and its head is cut like a limb (`MODE_CUT`: past the plane goes with the head) since it has no separate face. When a section has no sink of a side in its BoneMap, the DLL borrows the nearest helper of that side into it. The bodies' LOD0 CPU copy is kept by `sbslice.dll` (the `extract` entries under `Art/Character/Monster` and the Airborne Squad's in `mod/ragdoll_spec.json` only put them in the build cache for the harness). `native/sbslice/test/run.sh` cuts an arm, the head and the waist on each kind's real body (with the enemy sink list) and checks no stretched triangle stays visible; the waist test's flung arms take the arm helpers that hang off the clavicle along, as the animation does.

Since 0.4.1 an enemy's cut sends its physics bodies too (`bodies_of`, after `fffe` in `n`), so its stump seams close in the opening's middle on the stump's own body, as hers do; without them they closed on helpers 40-50 cm off the cut. `find_sink` never takes a bone the cut bone hangs from (a statue's root, `Base`, at its feet), and `body_hops` counts a bone under no body at all as 3 hops, the farthest. Without bodies (a waist cut's halves) a sink's collapse point is kept within `SINK_DEEP` (6 cm) past the plane and, if outside the opening's rim, moved into the rim's middle. A piece whose rim doesn't go round the plane point closes on its own rim's middle put on the plane, but only when that middle is within `SINK_DEEP` of it: a statue's arm seam follows its weights at the shoulder, some 30 cm up from a cut down the arm, and a tilted plane put its point 20 cm down the stump. In a limb or torso cut (`MODE_CUT`) a vertex wholly on an earlier cut's sinks is marked collapsed (`vkind` 9), and a triangle of only such vertices starts no seam (a waist cut after a thigh cut took the collapsed thigh's triangles and drew its opening 72 cm up). The harness's `SEQ=bone:at[:tilt],...` (`real_split.c`) cuts an enemy one cut after another as its corpse takes them, flings every piece after each and holds the seams (`sink_hold`), and fails on more stretched triangles showing than right after the cut, or a stretched edge longer than twice the opening plus 15 cm (`SEQNAMES`, `SEQSINKS`, `SEQON`, `SEQFROM` print bones, sinks and where vertices went; `ENEMYNOBODIES=1` cuts without the bodies).

Corpse hits (`enemies.lua` `corpse_hit`) skip parts already off the body (it or a bone it hangs from cut off, `gore.enemy_off`, parents asked once per enemy through `GetParentBone`), come at most one per `corpseHits.gap` (0.5 s), none in the first `from` (0.6 s) nor while a cut on the corpse is opening or queued (`gore.enemy_cuts`), and cut along her blade only (`blade_only`: no fallback to another part). Gore sounds are at least 0.35 s apart (`sfx_last.apart`). A cut that finishes on a corpse already dissolving has its caps and effects removed at once (`enemy.faded`, set by `enemy_fading`, cleared by `enemy_gone` and `forget`).

## Glass cannon

A realistic glass cannon mode, dev only for now (`dev = true` in `settings.lua`), off by default (`tune.glassCannon.enabled`; Shift+F8 turns it on and off in game). While her shield is up a hit takes the shield, never HP (vanilla lets part of it through). Once the shield is down, any hit kills her on the spot: cut along the enemy weapon's swing when one landed (as any killing blade, so a swing through her arm goes on into her body), else where it landed (the head off; the body split when the blow came down from above, else cut across the waist; an arm or a leg off). When the HP runs out the usual way, the finishing blow takes off what it landed on. Her blows do `damageMult` (8) times the damage: what every enemy within `foeReach` of her loses, HP and shield, is multiplied, though a multiplied blow leaves it at 1 HP rather than killing it.

The kill goes by her HUD (`WB_MainHUD_PlayerInfo`), which the game keeps live: her HP bar (`ProgressBar_HP.Percent`) dropping by more than `barDrop` (0.002) since the last tick is a hit (`glass: her HP bar 0.94 -> 0.88; landed on ...`), and her shield is its segments (`WB_MainHud_Shield_C` widgets, each a `ProgressBar_Shield`, found with FindAllOf: the HUD has no property to reach them by; `glass: her shield up/down (...)`). The game lets part of a hit through the shield, so a drop with any shown segment holding some (before or at that reading) is the shield's: no death, and the HP is given back (below). The bar slides down over a few ticks after a hit (1.000 -> 0.994, then 0.951), so drops within 0.8 s of one the shield took are its too. Not while the game is paused (gear that raises her MaxHP lowers the bar's share). None of this needs her stats from memory.

Her death is the game's own if it can be had: with her stats found, HP 0 written; without them, the game's own calls, `SBNetworkPlayerController`'s client RPCs, which a single-player game runs locally (the game itself never sends them there): `ClientBroadcast_ActorApplyStat` (her actor's `BroadcastSpawnInfo.ActorGuid`, which read 0 in the thirty-fourth playtest, so the calls may have gone to nobody; HP, value 0), and if she is still up `deathWait` (0.3) s later `ClientBroadcast_ActorDead`; still up as long again, the mod's own death (as Ctrl+F5's, she gets up after `recoverAfter`). A shield hit's HP goes back the same way (`ClientBroadcast_ActorApplyStat` with the HP before it, her MaxHP read off the HUD's HP text), and the log says what the bar did after (`glass: HP bar ... after giving back ...`). What these calls do with their arguments is untested. The damage multiplier still needs her stats.

Where a hit landed: the nearest enemy weapon's steps over the last `swingBack` s (the swing tracker of the killing cuts) passing within `reach` cm of a part of her, else where her mesh says it was hit (`meshHitWindow`), else a part picked by weight (`glass: ... landed on ...` in the log).

Her HP and shield exist only in native memory (no reflected property, and the cheat manager's HP and effect commands are stubs in the shipping build), so `sbslice.dll` finds them (`native/sbslice/stats.c`): an array of floats indexed by `ESBActorStatType` (HP 1, MaxHP 2, Shield 7, MaxShield 8), looked for in her actor and the objects it points to, two pointers deep, anchored on the numbers her HUD widget holds (`WB_MainHUD_PlayerInfo`'s `HpValue`, `MaxHpValue`, `ShieldValue`, `MaxShieldValue`, from the live instance showing a MaxHP: `FindFirstOf` handed back the all-zero class default). Never by the array's shape alone: thousands of float runs look like stats, and the one taken read HP 0 of 700. The game fills those numbers only now and then (both live widgets read MaxHP 0 for a whole fight in the twenty-third playtest, so nothing was guarded and her damage wasn't multiplied), so without them the stats are searched by her hits: `vs` notes every stats-shaped array in her actor tree (candidates), `vd<actor>,0` after a quiet spell drops the ones whose MaxHP changed, whose HP rose or whose HP changed on 6 checks running (timers; two running lost her array to a combo), and `vd<actor>,1` just after a hit on her mesh keeps the ones whose HP is under what it was when noted (so a hit the script missed doesn't lose her array); each hit's log line also shows what her HUD widgets hold (numbers, HP bar, HP text). Her HP bar (`ProgressBar_HP.Percent`) is the one HUD value the game keeps live (0.41 after a hit in the twenty-sixth playtest, the numbers 0, the text a stale "123 / 123"), so every 0.5 s, once the bar has held still at a new reading under 0.98, `vd<actor>,3` (the reading in LocX) keeps the candidates whose HP / MaxHP is within 0.015 of it, heals included, and only a second reading can take one (one reading took a stranger's array out of 6730, thirty-second playtest); the bar is read off the widget showing it, kept (FindAllOf only every 5 s, and not at all for the HUD's numbers once that widget shows none: a 50 ms hitch every 5 s). Once what is left reads as one array, its paths are the stats (`glass: after a hit: ok found=...`). The first hit lands as in the vanilla game. Her hits mostly go unseen (the mesh's hit shake came 18 times in a whole log), so her death settles it: `vd<actor>,2` keeps what went from above 0 to 0 (nothing at 0: nothing dropped), the bar's check goes first (that playtest's death left the bar at 0.044 and nothing at 0: `err none at 0`); her first life in the mode is the vanilla game's, and a note says when her HP is found (`glass: at her death: ...`). the stats found are checked against the HUD's maximums every 2 s and looked for again after three misses (the HUD's HP lags what the guard gives back, so it isn't compared). An enemy read at HP 0 isn't followed. The pointer path is followed again on every read, and enemies, also `ASBCharacter`s, are read through the same path. Every tick the DLL gives back HP a hit took while her shield was up, multiplies the enemies' losses and writes what happened to `sbslice.evt` (`glass: hit ...`, `guard ...`, `enemy ...`). Shift+F8 shows a note at the top of the screen that fades out by itself ("Glass cannon ON" / "OFF"): a click-through popup drawn by `sbslice.dll`'s window thread (`toast\t<text>` in `spawner.cmd`), since the shipping build strips the engine's own screen messages. `damageMult` is 8 by default (it was 4). A head or body hit sets her HP to 0; if the game doesn't take that as a death within `deathWait` s, the mod's own death runs (as Ctrl+F5's, with the cut) and she is up again after `recoverAfter` at full HP. `native/sbslice/test/run.sh` checks the find (her array, not a decoy of the same shape; refused without the HUD's numbers, none when they match nothing), reads and writes through the path, the guard, the multiplier and a lost actor on made-up actors (`test/test_stats.c`). Untested in game: whether the found array is the one the game uses, and whether HP 0 kills her.

## Asset tools

`assetkit/sbtool`: `dump <uasset> <json>`, `fromjson <json> <uasset>`, `dumpall <content_root> <json_root> [class]` (batch dump with a JSON round-trip check, package paths on stdin), `validate <dir>` (byte-exact round-trip check), all with `SB_USMAP` set. `build.sh` prefers `mappings/SB.usmap` (UE4SS `DumpUSMAP` from the game, full engine schema) over the header-generated one. Only that file decodes engine assets like `SkeletalMesh` and `PhysicsAsset`.

`assetkit/meshtool` (CUE4Parse, built with `assetkit/meshtool/build.sh`) reads meshes from retoc legacy output. `dump` writes bones, sections, positions and skin weights as JSON; `bones` writes only reference skeletons, used by the outfits step; `gltf` writes a `.glb` that imports into Blender with all 323 bones and weights. New meshes can't go back in without the UE editor to cook them. The generated mappings decode 172/197 of Eve's shows byte-exactly. The rest only miss engine structs (camera-shake oscillators, post-process settings) and still round-trip as raw exports.

## UE4SS script

The log (`stellarbladegore.log`) is made anew each start, the last kept as `.old`, capped at 20 MB (errors only past it). `tune.verboseLog` (`lg.v` in main.lua, `ctx.vlog`, the modules' own `vlog`) gates the per-poll death watch (`p###`, `a###`), face and camera probes, hook calls, every cut stage (else only slow ones), the active ragdoll's settle lines and each enemy followed; without it slow ticks and frame hitches are summed once a minute (`screen.perf_note` / `perf_flush`).

`ue4ss/StellarBladeGore` is the UE4SS script. It started as a probe for the ragdoll flinging (runtime fix + logger) and now also runs the gore and the active ragdoll. Install it with `./ue4ss/install.sh`, which sets up the Stellar Blade UE4SS build (pinned and checksummed), and add the Steam launch option `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="dwmapi=n,b"`.

The first probe run showed the impulse isn't the problem. Her pelvis barely travels, but every bone keeps 500–1400 cm/s and up to 9000 deg/s and picks up energy again after settling. That points to self-collision. The mesh runs the `Ragdoll` profile (object type `PhysicsBody`, blocks `PhysicsBody`), so the bodies of her outfit's physics asset collide with each other and with `SBHair` (PhysicsOnly, attached to the head).

On each ragdoll the script makes the mesh ignore `PhysicsBody` and turns `SBHair` collision off. Self-collision is off by default (`tune.selfCollision = false`): with it, an arm landing on her body set her spinning and folding at wild angles. Ignoring `PhysicsBody` didn't stop pairs inside her own physics asset, so the data mod disables those (`outfits.self_collision`); this switch still covers unpatched outfits. Muscle tone ends as soon as she lands (`tone.landOff`: pelvis fell faster than `fallSpeed`, then slowed under `landSpeed`). `"auto"` keeps it when the outfit's physics asset carries the patch (detected by the pelvis body's damping). It restores both on recovery, and re-attaches the mesh to the capsule if respawn left it detached.

The invisible model after respawn had two causes in SB's engine. `ASBCharacter::SetLockupSimulatePhysics(true)` copies each mesh's world transform into `LockupTransform` (an SB addition to `USkeletalMeshComponent`), and the mesh stays pinned there; the script clears that lock on recovery. The death ragdoll also sets a non-reflected counter on `USceneComponent` (+0x180) that stops the parent from moving the component. Only the mesh had it, so the capsule respawned and the body stayed at the death spot. Lua cannot reach it, so `sbslice.dll` clears it (formationPath `u<hex>` on the same cheat), on recovery and once after the respawn. The probe runs from Eve's `ReceiveTick`: this game gives UE4SS no `UEngine::Tick` hook, so `ExecuteInGameThread` callbacks can land on Blueprint worker threads, and one deadlocked the game. The `LoopAsync` that finds her Blueprint hooks that tick once a session and stops, so in play nothing of the mod's runs off the game thread but the `NotifyOnNewObject` callbacks (a death screen, a character, a knockdown check made), which only note the object. `sbslice.dll`'s own thread watches the game thread's mod commands: one running over 3 s goes to `sbslice.stuck`, and a crash in the DLL's code or inside a command to `sbslice.crash` (code, offset, command; a vectored handler that only looks); the next start logs them (`sbslice.dll last session: ...`). Its DllMain notes each step of its own start (the address search, the CPU copy patch) in `sbslice.boot`, by its own path (the game's working directory may not be set yet), logged as `sbslice.dll boot: ...`: a launch that closed before the first window left nothing else to go by. It logs bone motion to `Win64/ue4ss/Mods/StellarBladeGore/stellarbladegore.log`.

SB's ragdoll drags the capsule after `RagdollInfo.CapsuleComponentFollowingSocket` a step every tick, which made the camera stutter after her. The data patch sets it to `None` in every Eve ragdoll key, and the script clears it on every ragdoll tick as well (`tune.capsuleStill`), for an animated death that goes ragdoll partway and the test ragdolls, which never run a key and keep the default `Bip001-Pelvis`. The log's `capsule: drifted at most N cm during the ragdoll` (while she simulates, not recovery's move to her body) says whether it held. That wasn't all: an animated death that goes ragdoll partway (a split, a torso cut, a leg) has the game move the pawn after her body itself, 50-65 cm a tick, with the socket `None` and her movement already `MOVE_None` (playtest 41), and the view rides the pawn; putting the capsule back every tick made the camera strobe. So at a death the view holds still instead: a `CameraActor` is spawned where the view is (its rotation and field of view) and made the player controller's view target, turned every frame by the mouse and the right stick, read off the input (`GetInputAnalogKeyState` MouseX/Y, Gamepad_RightX/Y; `cameraMouse` degrees a unit of mouse input (3; SB reports a slow move as about 0.06 a frame), `cameraStick` degrees a second; the game turns the controller's rotation only sideways while she is dead), all the way round both ways, over the top upside down, about the point it looked at (her capsule's distance down the view, at least 150 cm), kept 10 cm short of whatever a Camera-channel trace from that point meets, flown by WASD, Q/E (world down and up), the left stick and the triggers (`IsInputKeyDown`; forward along the view, sideways on the level; `cameraMove` cm a second, `cameraFast` times with Shift, at most `cameraRange` from where it started, the point stopping 10 cm short of what a trace along its step meets; once flown it no longer slides after her), that point sliding after her pelvis on the level by as much as the pelvis has moved since she died, eased in (`cameraTrack`, 2.5: the share of the gap closed a second, as 1 - e^(-rate dt) each frame; its height kept, 0 holds it), and the view goes back to her at recovery (`tune.cameraStill`; `camera: view held at ...`, `camera: view back on her`, `camera at N s: ...` for 3 s). `capsule: moved N cm this tick` logs the pawn's moves (at most 5). The flying keys count only once all of them have been let go (she died running, W down, and it flew off at once). Esc on a real death (parked for now: the key binding is commented out in `main.lua`; not the test ragdolls; `tune.deathSkip`) sets the global time dilation to `deathSkipSpeed` (6) until the game's death screen widget `WB_MainHUD_Dead_C` is in the viewport and visible, or the game's time is past `recoverAfter`; the recovery clock counts the game's time meanwhile, so she still gets up under the screen (`death: Esc, fast forward x6 ...`, `death: fast forward off after N s (the death screen is up)`). While the death screen is up, the log says every 0.5 s, when it changes, what covers the view: the widget's `black` and `FadeOut` images, its `BackgroundBlur_116` and `Root` (visibility, render opacity, colour alpha, blur), and the camera manager's fade and view target (`death screen (N s into the death): ...`, `death screen: gone`; only in a death, from 5 s in, the widget found once and kept: a `FindFirstOf` every 0.5 s cost 17 ms each): it came up all black, nothing of the game behind it.

Cut stages and frame time: each `c` is mostly the engine building the new skin weight buffer (`SetSkinWeightOverride`, 15-30 ms in game; the DLL's own work is a few ms, the whole real-mesh harness runs in about 50), so no frame carries more than one. The split runs `cut` (the copy made), `cut2` (her half's cut), `cut3` (the copy's, then the checks), `caps` (her half's lids), `caps2` (the copy's, and the copy's physics bodies made kinematic in her pose, `split_bodies`), `open`; the limb, head and waist cuts run `cut`, `caps`, `stubs`, `open`, and bleed the tick after the opening.

With `cameraFollow` on (off by default: it fought how the death plays out, and the game died a second after it moved a really dead Eve's capsule), during any ragdoll her capsule, which the camera rides, drifts after her pelvis instead of staying where she died: nothing within `cameraSlack` (40 cm), then `cameraRate` (0.06) of the rest each tick, held `cameraLift` (95 cm) over it, ignoring her bodies meanwhile so it never shoves them (top level of `tune.lua`: `cameraFollow`, `cameraSlack`, `cameraRate`, `cameraLift`). A recovery then gets her up where she lay.

Enemies attacking her after her death (`tune.enemies.afterDeath`, enemies.lua `M.after_tick`). Parked in 0.4.1: nothing runs unless `tune.enemyAfterDeath` is true, and it is gone from settings and the menu (docs/TODO.md says why): while she lives, every 2 s, each character within `reach` cm (not hers, not `gore.attackerIgnore`) has its AI controller's `Blackboard` read: the `BlackboardKeyType_Object` keys of its `BlackboardAsset` (and parents) that hold her are kept per actor. From her death (`goreCtx.dead_since`) for `time` s: every `SBBTTask_SbDetectTarget` with `bEnemy` and not `bDead` gets `bDead` true (the search takes dead targets; kept through `goreCtx.keep` with their indexes read at once, put back for the ones still alive at the end), and every 0.25 s each live enemy within reach whose kept keys no longer hold her gets them set back (`SetValueAsObject`); one with no kept key uses its object keys named `Target`. The log has `after death: enemies keep at her ...` (searches changed, their `TargetCancel` values, enemies with her on their blackboards), the first 5 keys set back, and `after death: over ...`. The trees' target searches also get `TargetCancel` Destroy (2), the patrol's `SbMoveToHome` with `bDetectTarget` the same, and every `SBBTDecorator_SbIsAlive` on the target has its `CheckType` swapped (Alive 0, Dead 1); those nodes are looked up ahead (`after_scan`, while no enemy is within `scanClear` cm, whenever enemies of a new class turn up), not in the death's frame (four `FindAllOf` there were 195 ms). That brings them to her body, but the game's skills never fire at a dead target; playing their attack `AnimSequence`s by hand (`PlayAnimation`, with a lunge) found none to play. docs/TODO.md has the rest.

Enemies attacking her while she is down (`tune.enemies.attackDown`, enemies.lua): about 100 of the game's 149 enemy behaviour trees (`GameDesign/Combat/BehaviorTree`) hold their attack branch behind an `SBBTDecorator_SbCheckActorEffect` on the target (`ActorType` Target) whose `EffectAlias` / `OrCheck_EffectAliasArray` / `AndCheck_EffectAliasArray` name her knockdown effects (`KnockDown{Forward,Backward}[Tumbling[Ready]]_Eve`, and the shared `Getup`, `FastGetup`, `Down`, `DownFace{Up,Down}[_E]`). Her knockdown effects (`EffectTable`) set no `NoDamageNoHit`, so nothing else keeps her from being hit. The script finds the loaded decorators once (`FindAllOf`, 50-90 ms: run every 3 s it stuttered the game), then catches each new one as it is made (`NotifyOnNewObject`) and, a second later, once its asset's names are in, swaps those names for `SBGore_NotDown`, which no effect has; off, it puts the originals back (kept by the decorator's full name). The monsters' own `KnockDownForward` / `KnockDownBackward`, `Item_Resurrection_Ground` and `LV_FinishQTE_FailDown` are left as they are. With `enemies.attackDown` off and `holdDown` on, the 281 target checks whose name lists hold `Down` get the data mod's `Down_Eve` in place of `KnockDownForward` (no check lists one without the other), so the trees wait through her held knockdown; the 11 with `EffectAlias` `Down` alone (Juggernaut, Scarlet, Tachy, Sawshark trees, inverse checks) are left.

Ultra Violence's blood at a respawn (`tune.uvClean`): UV clears her blood only at a player restart (a load), Eve gone, a camp rest or in water (its `tickCombat`, every 0.5 s, reads `CharacterMovement.MovementMode` 4 as swimming and calls its `forceClean`), and the game's respawn (`NotifyBP_ReInitActor`, the same pawn) is none of these. So when the mod gets her up for a respawn (not a revive in place) it writes `MovementMode` 4 as a property for `screen.UV_POLLS` (36, 1.2 s) with her movement component's tick off (else its swim physics puts the mode back outside water), then `SetMovementMode(1)` and the tick back on, or leaves the movement to the game's respawn if it comes first.

As she comes into the world her ponytail starts on top of the sword on her back or off her hair tie and jiggles there till photo mode is opened and closed. The ponytail is cloth (`SBPonytail`, no animation of its own; KawaiiPhysics only moves her front and side strands) and collides with her bodies, which physfix re-makes as she spawns; the cloth settles on the wrong side of the tie, held there by those collisions, and cloth resets, `ResetAnimInstanceDynamics` and the game's `NeedPonytailClothConfigUpdate` all ran it again from where the collisions let it. Photo mode switches every character mesh's collision to query only while it is on (0x1423a33e0), which lets it drop back through. So `screen.cloth_soon` / `cloth_tick`, `CLOTH_AFTER` (4 s) after the last of a new Eve, `NotifyBP_ReInitActor` (a respawn), `NotifyBP_SetMesh` or `ApplyMeshInfo` (an outfit) or her bodies re-made (physfix `ctx.on_remade`), set every `SkeletalMeshComponent` in her attach tree (`screen.cloth_meshes`) with physics collision to `QueryOnly` (kept with `goreCtx.keep`, asked alive before they are given back), reset the cloth once (`ForceAllClothNextUpdateTeleportAndReset`, and on each mesh `ResetAnimInstanceDynamics(ResetPhysics)` and `ForceClothNextUpdateTeleportAndReset`), and give the collision back 1.5 s later; at once in a death or photo mode. The log says `cloth: her ponytail reset (<why>): N mesh(es), M of them query only for 1.5 s (as photo mode)` and `cloth: her meshes' collision given back (M)`.

Photo mode up close (`tune.photoUnhide`): photo mode hides her actor (`AActor::bHidden`, set inline, not through the virtual `SetActorHiddenInGame`) when its camera comes close. Its subject visibility (0x14239c410) hides her while any of its reasons is set (+0x220 hidden as it opened, +0x228 a count, +0x22d, +0x231, +0x22f); +0x22f is the close-range one, set by 0x14239b2c0 (the camera's distance to her bone against `SBPhotoMode_CloseRange_BoneToCamDistanceThreshold`) through `mov cl, 1` at 0x14239c0b3. Keeping +0x22f alone from being set (`mov cl, 0`) still left her hidden (playtest), so sbslice's `f6<0|1>` NOPs the jumps for +0x231, +0x228, +0x22d and +0x22f (0x14239c45b, 46b, 478, 485) or puts them back, every one's bytes checked first (`ok photo close <0|1> (was <0|1>)`); +0x220 still hides a subject that was hidden as photo mode opened. main.lua sends it once (logged once while sbslice starts), and again when the setting changes. Photo mode's own hide-a-character option goes with it. Besides that, main.lua `screen.photo_unhide` runs while photo mode is on (its manager's `+0x84` not 0, read by `f3` every 0.5 s, or `GetPlayerPawn` or the camera's view target a PhotoMode class; `photo probe: ...` logged as these change): her actor and her parts (her root's attach tree as it opens; one hidden then, an outfit piece the gore took off, stays hidden) are shown again, hidden, invisible and owner-no-see undone, her actor taken out of the controller's `HiddenActors`, and the near clip plane is `tune.photoNearClip` cm (`r.SetNearClipPlane` through `KismetSystemLibrary::ExecuteConsoleCommand`, back to the game's 10 as it closes). That runs from her ReceiveTick, which photo mode's pause stops; with dev on, a watcher every 0.25 s from that same tick logs `vis watch: ...` (sbslice f9, her parts' hide flags, the camera's distance while photo mode is up) as these change; it doesn't see through the pause. It was a `LoopAsync(250)` off the game thread, which crashed the game at a death screen (it found the command channel and read a pawn the respawn was remaking while the level loaded). Not the cause, found on the way: the gameplay camera's own player hide (`PlayerHideDistFromPlayerCameraXY`/`Z`, 0x1424085e0) and MPC_DitherActor's `SBWorldEnv_DitherDisable` (BG actors only).

Holding her down (`tune.holdDown`, main.lua `screen.held`): `tables.lua` adds `Down_Eve` to `Local/Data/EffectTable` (a copy of `Down`, ID 3009001, `LifeTime` 100000) and chains her tumbling knockdowns into it; `Down` allows the fast getup, so dodge or jump still gets her up. (`SBSuicide` is a no-op in the shipping build, so there is no real death key.)
