# Testing the mod

The Lua simulations are fast regression checks against a fake game, not proof of behavior or performance in Stellar Blade. The fake engine supplies objects, time, hooks and native responses; it cannot reproduce the engine's threading, garbage collection, render stalls, real assets, or every animation and outfit. Use a game playtest and its log for those questions.

On Windows, run `.\windows.ps1 -Action test-lua` from PowerShell, or pass `-Scenario split,frame` to select scenarios. The shared syntax and unexpected-global preflight runs before scenarios when a Lua compiler is available; a missing compiler is reported as a skipped check. Each scenario runs in its own scratch game directory; the runner requires a successful Lua exit, a `PASS` line, and no `FAIL` assertion line. The default list is in `ue4ss/test/run.sh`; `diag` is opt-in because the diagonal feature is parked. The native harness is `.\windows.ps1 -Action test-native`. Default native runs can skip mesh and physics-parity cases when build caches are absent; inspect the output. Explicitly selected cache-dependent groups fail when their required cache is missing.

The default runner includes 97 scenarios. `diag` is opt-in because the diagonal feature is parked; the newer `struggle_selection` regression is also run explicitly with `-Scenario struggle_selection`. This inventory establishes inclusion, not that every assertion is strong.

## What representative checks establish

- `clothscan`, `f7cloth`, `cutscene_hair`, `reload` and `lowarm` check fake cloth-reset timing, collision restoration, cinematic suppression and detached-arm handling; real cloth and physics need a game test.
- `newobj_fallback` checks watcher fallback choices in isolation. It cannot exercise UE4SS callback threading.
- `charcache` checks lifetime-batch sizes, cache reuse, and fake character read cadence; `reload` checks cleanup against a simulated freed screen. Native `test_alive` separately checks the native object-array logic. Neither can prove the real engine never frees an object between calls.
- `cutlist` compares selected cut lists with fake engine traversal and checks use of warmed trees; real skeletons still need game verification.
- `nosplits_blade` checks the resulting head or torso cut, absence of a split, and cleanup through the public gore state; it does not establish the appearance of the cut in game. `nosplits` exercises the separate random-death path, checks actual cut records and respawn cleanup, and supplies a successful fake native response. Its earlier log-only assertion could pass when native cuts failed.
- `script_preflight` checks valid code, unexpected global reads and syntax failures in isolated scratch files. It reports a skip if the compiler is absent.
- `asyncload` checks preload pacing and invokes the bone watch through scan cycles directly, so its caller’s error guard cannot conceal a watch exception.
- `scheduler_reentry` verifies the scheduler observes cut work queued by a bleed callback and that recovery cancels it. It is a fake-engine scheduling check, not a frame-time measurement.
- `gore_copies` checks external-copy discovery, exclusion of the mod’s own clones, synchronization and cleanup of 20 copies in batches of 16, queued updates during a sync, recovery, replaced cut lists, and retry after an injected error; it cannot establish compatibility with real mods or engine lifetimes.
- `rock`, `blast`, `smash_area` and `smash_hits` exercise fake projectile callbacks, impact-time pose, identity, classification and post-death queues. They do not establish real engine hook timing or impact locations.
- `hits_after` checks projectile dispatch with replaced settings and gore service, plus a synthetic swing step; `after` and `after_nomontage` test actual follow-up cut records in a fake game. Real attack timing and geometry still need a playtest.
- `outfit_animation` checks physics-node restore, split-rig recovery, the production world-reset entry point, fake freed meshes and address reuse; it cannot reproduce actual Unreal lifetimes or outfit animation.
- `split_cut` checks preparation, copy failure and saved-weight restoration, staged handoff and degenerate-half rejection with fake native responses. Real native cuts and physics need a game test.
- `split_break` checks plane crossing, missing bones and synthetic limb ancestry for both split halves; actual skeletons and physics need a game test.
- `split_plane` checks synthetic anatomical sides, midline, yaw limit, missing bones and mirrored diagonal planes; `split` and `lowarm` exercise lifecycle callers, and the parked `diag` scenario is opt-in. Real cut geometry needs a game test.
- `hit_classification` verifies that replacing settings, the tracked swing and the gore service affects subsequent decisions; `smash_hits`, `blast` and `rock` cover classification and dispatch in a fake game. Actual attack signals need an in-game check.
- `hit_marks` checks nearest-segment skin queries, decal attachment, the mark limit, healing and cleanup with fake native responses and objects; it cannot establish the feature’s appearance in game. The unfinished feature remains off by default.
- `gore_native` checks the fake handshake, copy invalidation, seam hold and fire-and-forget failure handling; it cannot reproduce the actual native bridge or engine threading.
- `body_blood` exercises the legacy skin ramp, recovery, world-change handoff and stains switch with a fake material collection; it cannot verify appearance in game.
- `enemy_tracker` checks ownership of front-inserted smash-chunk blood trackers and their separation during enemy cleanup and player recovery, plus replaced-list tagging, recovery from fake re-entrant shield and pool-growth calls, and restoration after a callback error; real engine object lifetime and effects still require a playtest.
- `split`, `stages`, and `smash_lifecycle` drive the scripts through cut stages and recovery in a fake world. `stages` also verifies the resulting severed-arm record, not just timing and a log line. `smash_lifecycle` checks that recovery cancels an unfinished staged cut as well as queued smash batches. `frame` checks geometric mirror properties, `tachy_geometry` checks bone selection and UV behavior on shipped core data, and `cap_fallback` checks actor placement after a simulated mesh failure. They cannot verify the rendered appearance or real physics.
- `ragdoll_probe` checks that ordinary startup skips three diagnostic snapshots and that probes still enable them. `perf_probe` checks the GC-probe death pause and restoration of original GC options. `hook_perf` counts fake lookups; `swing_perf` compares recorded- and live-pose sweep results to a reference, checks shared-joint reads, fresh poses, settings reload and retry after failure, and prints a synthetic benchmark. These do not measure in-game frame times.
- `settings_manager` checks first-run creation, layered overrides, reload notifications and preservation of a simulated player settings file.
- `death_face` checks fake morph selection, application, cleanup and reloaded settings; it cannot verify visual expression or a real head swap.
- `voiceas` checks alternate-character voice selection with a successful simulated limb cut and a death that allows post-death voice; `split` separately checks post-death movement suppression, but does not directly assert audio output.
- `f6` checks the actual set of cut parts for each struggle case; `outfit` checks that a cut remains while the tested outfit physics is active. Fake physics cannot establish what appears in game.
- `animdeath` directly checks cut records and actual constraint-break calls in its animated/no-cut and waist-recovery cases; other show and lifecycle checks remain log-based.
- `fall` checks actual cut records for fall deaths, landing blows and an enemy death with takeover. `lowarm` checks side selection, recovery, settings reload, and cut records through live and death-time outfit changes. Some other assertions in those scenarios remain log-based.
- `after_geometry` checks a hand crossing, a distant miss and an interpolated blade crossing against a known synthetic limb; it also verifies a shared joint is read once per calculation, refreshed on the next and retried after a failed read. Actual weapon bounds, motion and frame time need a game test.
- `followup` and `followup_off` verify recorded blade cuts with follow-up cuts enabled and disabled and confirm recovery removes them. `after` checks simulated follow-up attacks, verifies an additional cut exists, and checks cleanup. `after_nomontage` checks walking and overhead misses and verifies that a fast swing without a montage cuts the remaining arm. Both fixtures disable the blunt-smash and proximity-odds overrides so the configured initial limb-only death is honored; they do not cover every weapon plane or death.

## Reading results

The `animdeath`, `fall`, and `lowarm` scenarios still depend heavily on log messages; their other state checks provide only partial independent coverage. Review each before relying on a wording-only assertion or deleting overlapping cases. Pool scenarios that inspect the tracker list use the module’s live list supplier, so they continue to observe replacement after cleanup. Some scenarios inspect log messages, and some use `debug.getupvalue` to reach local state. A log assertion can fail after harmless wording changes; an upvalue test can fail after a harmless refactor. Such a failure is a reason to investigate the behavior, not to blindly restore the wording or internal shape. Conversely, passing a log or call-count assertion does not establish that a player sees the intended result. Prefer direct observable state, realistic geometry and independent expected results where the fake environment can express them.

The camera, custom-ragdoll and missing-custom-ragdoll scenarios currently allow failed fake cut calls: their checks target camera or compatibility behavior, not successful cuts. They do not establish cut behavior.

The above is a review of representative scenarios, not an audit of every test. Before removing or merging a scenario, identify the behavior it alone protects and whether another check covers the same failure mode. Preserve the in-game log and settings across installations, and record what still needs a real-world retest in the changelog and technical notes.

`struggle_selection` checks first-action selection for no-cut bodies lying on either side or face down. `asyncload` checks that timed-out assets survive a closed loading window and take priority once loading is safe; `smash_effects` checks the pulsing stump effect and per-update chunk pacing. These use a fake engine and do not measure game frame times or visuals.
